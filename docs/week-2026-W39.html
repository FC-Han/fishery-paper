水产养殖可持续发展与气候变化应对研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文15篇，研究主题涵盖水产养殖技术优化、渔业资源评估、鱼类生物学与生态学、水产品安全及人工智能应用等多个方向。从期刊分布来看，*Aquaculture, Fish and Fisheries* 和 *Journal of Fish Biology* 各发表3篇，*Problems of Fisheries* 发表4篇，其余散见于 *Aquaculture*、*Microorganisms* 等期刊。整体研究趋势呈现两个特征：一是水产养殖的可持续性议题持续升温，涉及替代蛋白源开发、养殖密度优化和季节特异性饲料设计；二是区域性渔业资源评估与生物学研究仍占重要地位，尤其以俄罗斯和韩国水域的种群研究为代表。

While live probiotics are popular alternatives, their real‐world effectiveness remains highly controversial. Variable effectiveness results from complex interactions with different fish species, differences in fish gastrointestinal anatomy, and variations in environmental parameters such as salinity, pH, and temperature. Furthermore, live probiotics pose risks of ecological disturbance and horizontal gene transfer of antimicrobial resistance. This critical review assesses these limitations and proposes postbiotics, preparations of inanimate microorganisms and their metabolites, as a more stable, controlled, and biosafe formulation. Redesigning boat owner-crew labor relations in Kupang fisheries: a systems and behavioral analysis
👤 Nggajo, Raimundus; Nurani, Tri W.; Darmawan, .; Solihin, Iin... 📅 2026-09-25
Informal patron-client arrangements in capture fisheries can combine vessel finance, employment, social assistance, and market control, creating both protection and dependency. This study examined how boat owner-crew labor relations are reproduced and how a feasible transformation could be designed at Tenau Coastal Fishing Port and Oeba Fish Landing Base, Kupang City, Indonesia. A qualitative case study with descriptive economic support used observations, in-depth interviews, structured questionnaires, and document review from April to November 2024. The principal participants comprised 20 matched dyads, each consisting of one boat owner and one crew member; collectors, port managers, and local-government actors were supporting informants. The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) was used to explain why the existing arrangement persisted, while Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) translated the diagnosis into a purposeful activity system and staged intervention strategy. Owners financed vessels and operations and controlled first-sale decisions and records, whereas crew members supplied labor but did not determine buyers, prices, or post-landing revenue allocation. The arrangement persisted because crew work provided income and social support under limited alternatives, while owners sought to protect capital, manage risk, secure labor, and coordinate marketing. Norms of loyalty, gratitude, kinship, and informality reinforced these constraints. The analysis produced one multi-actor root definition and eight minimum activities for shifting opaque and dependency-based relations toward more transparent, clearly defined, and balanced arrangements. The strategy begins with shared information, followed by minimum work agreements and mediation, crew economic capability, and alternative economic access. Social-norm change, behavioral constraints, and system redesign must therefore be addressed together without disregarding the legitimate financing and risk-bearing role of boat owners. Methodologically, the sequential integration of TPB and SSM links behavioral explanation to conceptual system redesign.
