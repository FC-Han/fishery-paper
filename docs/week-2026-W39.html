本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文15篇，研究主题涵盖水产养殖技术优化、渔业资源评估、鱼类生物学与生态学、水产品安全及人工智能应用等多个方向。从期刊分布来看，*Aquaculture, Fish and Fisheries* 和 *Journal of Fish Biology* 各发表3篇，*Problems of Fisheries* 发表4篇，其余散见于 *Aquaculture*、*Microorganisms* 等期刊。整体研究趋势呈现两个特征：一是水产养殖的可持续性议题持续升温，涉及替代蛋白源开发、养殖密度优化和季节特异性饲料设计；二是区域性渔业资源评估与生物学研究仍占重要地位，尤其以俄罗斯和韩国水域的种群研究为代表。

👤 Coulibaly, Safiatou; Kouamenan, N'zebo Moïse; Akesse, Ekumou... 📅 2026-09-24
The present study aimed to compare the adaptability of the Bouaké and Brazil strains of Oreochromis niloticus to brackish lagoon conditions in order to identify the strain best suited to intensive lagoon aquaculture in Côte d’Ivoire and to contribute to the diversification and optimisation of national and sub-Saharan African aquaculture production. A total of 3,600 fingerlings (initial mean weight: 5 g) were stocked equally in two 600 m² lagoon enclosures and reared for eight months (July 2024 to February 2025). Fish were fed a commercial diet, while water-quality variables and zootechnical indicators were monitored monthly. Salinity increased during the study from approximately 0.08 to 6.88‰, with similar mean values between the enclosures. The Brazil strain showed higher final body weight (603.92 ± 68.30 g vs. 429.38 ± 58.29 g), total length (28.74 ± 0.79 cm vs. 25.06 ± 1.64 cm), daily weight gain (2.50 ± 0.28 g day⁻¹ vs. 1.77 ± 0.24 g day⁻¹), and specific growth rate (1.99 ± 0.05% day⁻¹ vs. 1.85 ± 0.06% day⁻¹) than the Bouaké strain. These findings describe contrasting performance profiles under the lagoon conditions tested: the Brazil strain achieved greater growth, whereas the Bouaké strain showed higher survival. However, because the study used only one enclosure per strain and water-quality conditions were not identical between enclosures, the observed differences cannot be attributed solely to strain effects. In addition, salinity increased during the rearing period. Replicated and randomised trials are therefore needed to confirm these results and support strain selection for intensive lagoon aquaculture in Côte d’Ivoire.

Mechanisms Driving Antimicrobial Resistance in Aquaculture
👤 Stratev, Deyan 📅 2026-09-22
Aquaculture is a rapidly growing industry, with worldwide production reaching new levels. Intensive aquaculture often leads to the occurrence of bacterial diseases, which cause large economic losses. Antibiotics are the first choice for the treatment of these diseases, but their frequency or improper use results in the development of antimicrobial resistance. The aim of this study was to present the mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance to the most widely used groups of antibiotics, such as quinolones, tetracyclines, and phenicols, for controlling bacterial diseases in fish. The selective pressure exerted by these pharmaceuticals drives the emergence of resistant bacterial strains and the horizontal transfer of genes encoding this resistance. The transmission of resistant pathogens from aquaculture to other animals and humans is well documented, which complicates the treatment of emerging bacterial diseases. Hence, the aquatic environment is a major reservoir of resistant bacteria, genes, and antimicrobials. Efforts should be made to stop the excessive and improper use of antibiotics in aquaculture and encourage farmers to use alternative means for the prevention and control of bacterial diseases. Several alternative strategies can be employed in aquaculture as substitutes for antibiotics, thereby mitigating the development of antimicrobial resistance. Among the most prominent alternative measures are vaccines, immunostimulants, and bacteriophage therapy. Encouraging the adoption of sustainable farming practices plays a critical role in fostering optimal conditions for aquaculture systems, thereby mitigating disease outbreaks and obviating the need for antibiotic treatments. Antimicrobial resistance is effectively mitigated through the implementation of a holistic One Health approach, which systematically curtails the emergence and dissemination of resistant bacteria while safeguarding human and animal health.

👤 Jibril, Jibril Y.; Bartie, Kerry L.; Baily, Johanna; Desbois... 📅 2026-09-23

Southwest Nigeria dominates national aquaculture output, but fish farming in northern Nigeria is expanding. Understanding the infectious disease situation in this burgeoning segment of the sector, including how farmers act to prevent and manage suspected outbreaks, is important to inform implementation of effective practices. This study aimed to describe practices at farms in northern Nigeria, with a focus on those relevant to infectious disease prevention and control.
