水产养殖可持续发展与生态健康研究前沿

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文15篇，研究主题涵盖水产养殖技术优化、渔业资源评估、鱼类生物学与生态学、水产品安全及人工智能应用等多个方向。从期刊分布来看，*Aquaculture, Fish and Fisheries* 和 *Journal of Fish Biology* 各发表3篇，*Problems of Fisheries* 发表4篇，其余散见于 *Aquaculture*、*Microorganisms* 等期刊。整体研究趋势呈现两个特征：一是水产养殖的可持续性议题持续升温，涉及替代蛋白源开发、养殖密度优化和季节特异性饲料设计；二是区域性渔业资源评估与生物学研究仍占重要地位，尤其以俄罗斯和韩国水域的种群研究为代表。

Market Systems Integration in Zambia's Aquaculture Sector: A Scoping Review of Value Chains, Supply Chains and Sustainability Challenges
👤 Siankwilimba, Enock; Maulu, Sahya; Mphande, Joseph; Phiri, M... 📅 2026-09-22
ABSTRACT Aquaculture is increasingly important to food security, nutrition, livelihoods and economic development in Zambia, yet sectoral growth depends on more than production capacity. This scoping review synthesised evidence on Zambia's aquaculture market systems, focusing on value chains, supply chains and persistent evidence gaps. Web of Science, Scopus and Google Scholar were searched alongside relevant grey literature for records published between 2000 and 2026. Of 652 records retrieved, 563 remained after removal of duplicates and clearly ineligible records, 152 full texts were assessed, and 107 sources were charted through the formal review process; 14 supplementary sources identified during manuscript revision were subsequently incorporated, giving 121 sources in the final synthesis. Evidence was synthesised across six value‐chain stages and four sustainability dimensions. The sector is increasingly dualistic, with rapid production growth alongside persistent structural constraints. Reported production increased from 21,567 t in 2017 to 52,922 t in 2022, whereas the share attributed to establishments captured in Zambia Statistics Agency surveys increased from 74% to 79%; Southern Province accounted for 73% of production in 2022. Administrative and survey estimates for the same year differed by 43%. Feed costs and weak cold‐chain infrastructure recurred across the value chain, alongside reported post‐harvest losses of 20%–30% and formal credit access of 12%–16%. Evidence on smallholder aquaculture was mixed: Some studies characterised it as unsustainable as a primary livelihood activity, whereas farm‐level analyses indicated that individual ponds could be financially viable, suggesting constraints beyond production itself. Major evidence gaps remain in cold‐chain and aggregation economics, gender‐disaggregated analysis, environmental performance, policy evaluation and household production dynamics. Future development requires stronger market‐system integration alongside production growth and targeted empirical and policy research.

ABSTRACT Population structure is important for broodstock management and conservation of Ompok pabda . This study evaluated phenotypic shape variation among three hatchery populations from Bogura (Kahalu), Dinajpur (Sadar) and Mymensingh (Muktagacha) and one wild population from Arial Beel , Munshiganj, using 15 landmark geometric morphometrics. After data‐quality screening and generalised Procrustes analysis, a small but significant common within‐group allometric effect explained 2.28% of shape variation (permutation p = 0.0413) and was removed before multivariate analysis. Towards Season‐Specific Feeds: Maximising Protein Digestibility and Reducing Waste in Hybrid Abalone ( Haliotis rubra × Haliotis laevigata) Aquaculture
👤 Hassan, Abdul Lathiff Inamul; Mock, Thomas S.; Searle, Kiere... 📅 2026-09-21
ABSTRACT Australian abalone grow‐out aquaculture relies on formulated feeds to provide for the nutritional requirements of the animal. At the same time, grow‐out systems are subject to seasonal water temperature fluctuations. As such, the economic and environmental performance of cultured abalone is inherently tied to the utilisation efficiency of dietary nutrients and changes to water temperature. This study aimed to better understand the effect of water temperature and dietary protein level on nutrient digestibility in Australian hybrid abalone ( Haliotis rubra × Haliotis laevigata ). Hybrid abalone were fed five different levels of dietary protein for 150 days at three distinct water temperatures. Digestibility, nutrient utilisation and calculations of nitrogen assimilation efficiency and undigested nitrogenous waste were assessed. Protein digestibility was negatively affected during both warm (22°C) and cold (12°C) water growing conditions and appeared optimal at 17°C, whereas lipid digestibility increased as the water temperature increased from 12°C to 22°C. An increase in the dietary protein level up to 440 g kg −1 at both 17°C and 22°C improved macronutrient and amino acid digestibility, compared to diets formulated with lower dietary protein. Relationships between water temperature and diet protein level were plotted on response surfaces and showed that maximum protein digestibility occurs at 16.8°C and 46.3% diet protein, whereas maximum dry matter digestibility occurs at 18.9°C and 43.1% diet protein. Taken together, these findings can significantly contribute towards developing season‐specific feeds for Australian hybrid abalone to maximise growth performance while minimising effluent discharge.

Here, we demonstrate how Inuit community members can actively contribute to monitoring narwhals and co-occurring taxa using environmental DNA (eDNA). 👤 Nkumbe, Nkwelle Marius; Asah, Ntugho Marvellous; Ambeno, Fid... 📅 2026-09-21
Background: The seasonal dynamics of the Gulf of Guinea, characterised by alternating dry and rainy seasons, exert profound influences on oceanographic conditions, primary productivity, and fish recruitment patterns. Understanding these seasonal patterns is particularly important for artisanal fisheries that depend on predictable seasonal variations in fish availability and abundance. Aims: This study investigated the length-weight relationship (LWR) and condition factor (K) of ten commercially important artisanal fish families from the Bamusso Coast, Cameroon, sampled monthly from January to June 2024. Methodology: A total of 677 specimens from families including Carangidae, Mugilidae, Clupeidae, Sphyraenidae, Polynemidae, Ariidae, Cynoglossidae, Lobotidae, Sciaenidae, and Serranidae were measured for total length (cm) and weight (g). LWR parameters were estimated using the equation W = abL, while the condition factor was calculated as K = 100W/Lb. Results: The results revealed significant interspecific variability in mean lengths (26.42–82.77 cm) and weights (0.18–4.81 g). Sphyraena barracuda exhibited the greatest mean length (82.77±37.78 cm), while Epinephelus marginatus recorded the highest mean weight (4.81±24.97 g). Length-weight relationships varied considerably among species, with Caranx hippos (b=2.30, r=0.86) and Lobotes surinamensis (b=2.96, r=0.94) showing strong positive correlations. All species displayed negative allometric growth (b<3.0), indicating a disproportionate increase in length relative to weight. Condition factor values ranged widely from 0.002±0.001 (Lobotes surinamensis) to 122.10±397 (Polydactylus quadrifilis), reflecting species-specific physiological conditions and habitat utilisation. Conclusion: These findings establish baseline biological parameters relevant to sustainable fisheries management in Cameroonian coastal waters and contribute to regional fish biology databases. The observed variability in growth patterns and condition factors underscores the need for species-specific management strategies to support the long-term conservation of artisanal fishery resources.

Effective conservation of exploited species requires balancing harvest opportunities with the protection of biodiversity. In Pacific salmon, biodiversity among populations contributes to basin-wide resilience, yet spatiotemporal dynamics of adult migrating Chinook salmon remain poorly understood in many large salmon-producing watersheds, creating uncertainty about management impacts. Declining abundance in western Alaska has led to restrictions in subsistence harvest and challenges for upstream communities. In the Kuskokwim River, a large salmon-producing river, a front-end closure limits early-season fishing to reduce exploitation of fish migrating to the upper watershed. 👤 Artemenkov, D. V.; Ivanov, P. Yu.; Sologub, D. O.; Deminov, ... 📅 2026-09-21
The spiny king crab is a commercially fished species of aquatic biological resources in the Far Eastern seas of the Russian Federation. Published data on the biology of P. brevipes are available for populations in the Sea of Okhotsk and the Sea of Japan, off south-eastern Kamchatka, and in the waters around the Kuril Islands and Hokkaido. This is the first description of the life cycle, growth, and sexual and size structure of the spiny king crab inhabiting the Shelikhov Bay area of the Sea of Okhotsk. 👤 Reis-Filho, José Amorim; Carvalho, Adriana R.; Giarrizzo, To... 📅 2026-09-21
Abstract The global 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development calls for more equitable and sustainable pathways by integrating traditional communities into development efforts. Despite these ambitions, challenges persist, as many initiatives, including Social and Solidarity Economy (SSE) businesses, often operate in regional silos with broader transformative potential. Small-scale fisheries (SSF), particularly in the Global South, remain as one of the most affected sectors by economic imbalances and social injustices. Using Latin America fishery nations as a starting point and a particular focus on Brazil, which has the largest population of small-scale fishers in the Americas, we examine how SSE can be more effectively integrated into SSF stewardship by analyzing the quantitative and geographical landscape of SSF organizations and the broader SSE environment. We combine official data in a regional comparative perspective to assess how SSF organizations, fisher populations, labor pools, and cooperative density are configured across Latin America. Our results show that countries with larger SSF populations and related labor pools tend to sustain more numerous SSF organizations, while the broader cooperative environment is positively associated with, but does not fully determine, SSF organizational density. In Brazil, SSF organizations are widespread but remain only weakly integrated into SSE-oriented institutional frameworks, reflecting fragmented policies, limited legal support, and uneven territorial coverage of enabling programs. These findings reveal both opportunities and constraints: existing organizational networks provide a broad institutional base for SSE expansion, yet important territorial and institutional asymmetries persist. By highlighting “investment-need zones” and proposing policy and institutional recommendations, we argue that Brazil has a unique responsibility and opportunity to lead SSE–SSF integration in Latin America. Advancing this agenda could foster greater equity, strengthen social sustainability, and inspire broader transformations across the Global South toward solidarity-driven fisheries development.

👤 Moreland, Jacob A; Neal, J Wesley; Dunn, Corey G; Little, Lo... 📅 2026-09-21
Abstract Objective Jaguar Guapote Parachromis managuensis and Red Devil/Midas cichlids Amphilophus spp. are highly invasive species in the family Cichlidae native to Central America. These cichlids are popular within the aquarium trade, leading to their broad introduction outside of their native range. Information about potential environmental plasticity of a species can improve predictions of invasion potential. In this respect, measures of environmental influences on metabolic rate and hypoxia tolerance are important for understanding limitations in adaptability and subsequently guiding the management of invasive species. Methods Standard metabolic rate (SMR), hypoxia tolerance, and critical thermal maxima (CTmax) were compared between Jaguar Guapote and Amphilophus spp. For SMR and hypoxia tolerance, a range of temperatures spanning subtropical to tropical environments (i.e., 22, 28, and 34°C) was used, followed by stepwise decreases in oxygen saturation until loss of equilibrium. Results Jaguar Guapote and Amphilophus spp. both had lower SMR at 28°C than at 34°C, SMR at 22°C was not different from either temperature. Both Jaguar Guapote and Amphilophus spp. were the most sensitive to hypoxia at the coolest temperature (22°C) relative to warmer temperatures (28°C and 34°C). Upper thermal tolerance (CTmax) was similar between species (∼42°C), although Jaguar Guapote were more resilient based on a higher agitation temperature and recovery following high temperature exposure. Conclusions These results indicate temperatures that are representative of their natural distribution (i.e., near 28°C) offer energetic savings for Jaguar Guapote and Amphilophus spp. relative to warmer temperatures (34°C), with cooler temperatures (22°C) having higher metabolic costs than expected. Both Jaguar Guapote and Amphilophus spp. are sensitive to hypoxia at cooler temperatures (22°C). Therefore, these species are relatively resilient to high temperatures, but low temperatures may restrict their invasiveness.

Scale-Dependent Effects of Riparian and Watershed Land Use on Water Quality and Fish Assemblages: Implications for Reservoir Ecosystem Management
👤 Chen, Yongjin; Yu, Zhenfei; Zhou, Qiong 📅 2026-09-21
Watershed land-use change is a major driver of freshwater ecosystem degradation, yet whether water quality and fish assemblages respond to land use at the same spatial scale remains poorly resolved. This study examined multi-scale land-use effects on both endpoints in Songhua Lake, a large reservoir in Northeast China. Water quality and fish assemblages were surveyed from 2020 to 2022 across seven inflowing sub-watersheds and five open-lake sites. Land-use composition was quantified at five spatial scales (200–2000 m riparian buffers and the sub-watershed), and redundancy analysis was used to assess scale-dependent associations. Water quality deteriorated along the disturbance gradient, with CODMn increasing by 33.9% and water quality index declining by 10.6% from low- to high-disturbance groups. RDA showed that water quality was best explained at the 200 m buffer (97.2%), with forest as the dominant predictor. In contrast, fish assemblage variation was best captured at the 2000 m buffer and sub-watershed scales (up to 99.96%), with urban land as the dominant predictor; β-diversity showed the strongest response (p < 0.001). These results reveal a scale mismatch between physicochemical and biological responses, indicating that effective reservoir management requires a dual-layer strategy combining riparian restoration for water quality with sub-watershed regulation of urbanization for fish conservation.
