渔业研究多维热点：气候变化、社会生态与水产养殖

本周研究概览

本周共收录5篇渔业相关论文，研究方向较为分散，涵盖渔业技术现代化、鱼类行为分析、渔业考古、替代蛋白源开发以及鱼类微生物组学。整体来看，本周论文呈现出明显的跨学科特征：一方面，自动化、电子监控（electronic monitoring, EM）与人工智能（AI）在捕捞业中的应用受到持续关注；另一方面，鱼类健康与饲料可持续性问题仍是水产养殖领域的核心议题。值得注意的是，本周缺乏发表在顶级综合性期刊上的高影响力成果，研究更多集中于专业领域期刊，反映出渔业科学本周以应用导向和方法创新为主。

创新性研究

  1. From Mechanized Longlines to Smart Fisheries: Integrating Automation, Electronic Monitoring, and Artificial Intelligence（Journal of Marine Science and Engineering）

该综述系统整合了71篇文献，梳理了延绳钓渔业从机械化到智能化的技术演进路径，重点讨论了自动化、EM与AI的协同整合。其创新之处在于将分散的技术进展纳入统一框架，为智能渔业（smart fisheries）的落地提供了系统性参考，对渔业管理与合规监测具有潜在政策意义。

  1. Fish 3D Clustering App (F3DCA): A Non-Supervised Clustering Method with a Machine Learning Autoencoder for Analyzing 3D Trajectories of Three Fish Species（Biology）

  1. Bacterial Taxonomic Profiling of Freshwater Fish Across Hydrographic Basins of Kazakhstan（Fishes）

该研究对哈萨克斯坦六大水文盆地、六个科的193尾野生淡水鱼进行了多组织（鳃、肾、脾、肝）细菌群落测序分析，填补了中亚地区鱼类微生物组数据的空白。其创新性在于大空间尺度的跨流域采样设计，为理解环境与宿主因素对鱼类微生物群落的影响提供了基础数据。

  1. Production of African House Fly (Musca Domestica) Maggots using Decomposed Wastes, Cow Dung and Sawdust as a Sustainable Protein Source for Poultry, Fish and Pig Feeds（International Journal For Multidisciplinary Research）

该研究探索了利用分解废弃物、牛粪和锯末培养家蝇（Musca domestica）幼虫作为替代蛋白源的可行性，以应对乌干达鱼粉和大豆粕成本上升的问题。其创新点在于将废弃物管理与饲料生产相结合，具有循环经济与可持续水产养殖的双重意义，但研究深度与规模化验证仍需加强。

Chitosan–Fish Scale Collagen/Glycerin 3D Porous Scaffold EDAC/NHS Cross-Linking: Effects on the Microstructure, Physicochemical Properties, and Cytocompatibility
👤 Ullah, Saleem; Zainol, Ismail; Jaafar, Che Nor Aiza 📅 2026-10-10
Abstract The three-dimensional porous architecture of scaffolds strongly influences cell infiltration and cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions. The mechanical strength and biostability of three-dimensional (3D) porous scaffolds remain challenges for maintaining the 3D porous architecture and facilitating cell infiltration and cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions. In this study, the mechanical strength and biostability of chitosan–fish scale collagen 3D porous scaffolds were improved by using EDAC/NHS cross-linking in the presence of glycerin. Furthermore, the microstructure, physicochemical properties, mechanical strength, biostability, and cytocompatibility of the scaffolds were investigated. Chitosan–fish scale collagen/glycerin 3D porous scaffolds were fabricated via the EDAC/NHS cross-linking. Cross-linking effects on scaffold microstructures and physicochemical properties were investigated, while the scaffolds’ mechanical strength and biostability were determined by using mechanical and biodegradation tests. Cytocompatibility tests were used to explore the ability of 3D porous scaffolds to be used for tissue engineering applications. The scaffolds’ cross-linking density/degree, tensile modulus, and biostability were increased and pore size decreased with EDAC/NHS cross-linking treatment, resulting in enhanced mechanical strength and biostability of the 3D porous architecture of the scaffolds. The average tensile modulus of the cross-linking scaffold in a dry environment was in a range of 4.61 ± 2.33–0.62 ± 0.13 MPa, with a tensile modulus in a wet environment of 25.2 ± 6.32–17.74 ± 6.00 KPa, a biodegradation rate at day 30 of 28.73 ± 4.58–26.014 ± 1.70, and average pore sizes of 151.33 ± 53.66–142.31 ± 60.67 μm. In vitro culture of human fibroblasts and keratinocytes showed that the mechanical strength and biostability of cross-linking scaffolds supported cell proliferation, adhesion, spreading, and infiltration. It was found that the EDAC/NHS treatment improved the mechanical strength and biostability of the chitosan–fish scale collagen 3D porous architecture in the presence of glycerin and enhanced the cytocompatibility of scaffolds.

The overlooked role of food and cultural dimensions in recreational fisheries
👤 Badhon, Mahatub Khan; Castello, Leandro; MacNeil, M. Aaron; ... 📅 2026-10-11
Abstract Our study examines the diversity of values within a superficially homogeneous recreational fishery and its relationship to angler diversity. We collected data on fishing preferences, behaviors, catch usage, and sociodemographics from 443 shore anglers across 10 cities in the lower Great Lakes. We identified five coherent dimensions, including food, cultural, leisure, challenge/performance, and social–financial centrality, consistent with multidimensional value structuring in recreational fisheries. Based on these patterns, anglers were classified into three data-driven groups along a food provisioning–leisure/sport continuum: provisioning/food-oriented, cultural/community-oriented, and leisure/sport-oriented. We further characterized these groups by differences in their fishing preferences and sociodemographics. These findings provide empirical support that recreational and non-recreational values coexist and jointly characterize angler diversity in urban shore fisheries. Our study highlights the need for future research and management to treat food provisioning and cultural practices as core dimensions of recreational fisheries that reflect anglers’ diverse values and needs.

Leaning left: how turn bias and habitat complexity shape spatial cognition in a coral-reef fish
👤 von Drenkmann, Helen; Klinke, Annabell; Brown, Culum 📅 2026-10-11
Abstract In coral reef ecosystems, anthropogenic change is rapidly altering habitat structure, but the consequences for fish cognition remain poorly understood. We tested whether structural habitat complexity influences spatial learning and strategy use in the humbug damselfish ( Dascyllus aruanus ). Juveniles were reared in aquaria containing either high- or low-complexity coral structures and trained in a T-maze task, requiring the use of egocentric (turn-based) and/or allocentric (landmark-based) strategies to locate a reward. Performance was assessed using the number of trials to reach a learning criterion and latency to make correct decisions. Probe trials with conflicting cues assessed navigation strategies. Turn direction was the strongest predictor of performance. Fish trained to turn left reached criterion in fewer trials and consistently relied on egocentric strategies, indicative of a lateralisation bias. In contrast, right-turning fish increasingly relied on landmark cues during probe trials and made faster decisions when doing so, suggesting that allocentric strategies reduced the cost of acting against intrinsic turn biases. Contrary to our expectation, environmental complexity did not affect the number of trials required to reach criterion. However, high-complexity fish located the reward faster during early training and showed longer decision latencies during probe trials, suggesting greater sensitivity to landmark information when cues conflicted. Our results suggest that spatial task performance may be shaped more by turn biases and strategy use than by environmental complexity alone, highlighting the importance of considering intrinsic behavioural constraints when interpreting spatial cognition.

Spatio-Temporal Patterns and Ecological Implications of Microplastic Contamination in Benthic Fish from the Tongkou River, China
👤 Wu, Jiayun; Liao, Qingqing; Ren, Yingying; Zhang, Linhong; T... 📅 2026-10-10
Microplastic pollution in rivers is a critical environmental threat that harms aquatic life. To understand the abundance, physical characteristics, and polymer types of microplastics, this study examined the intestinal tract of four fish groups (three individual species: Saurogobio dabryi, Squalidus argentatus, and Hemibarbus labeo; and a pooled group of four loach species: Paramisgurnus dabryanus, Triplophysa bleekeri, Oreias dabryi, and Paracobitis variegatus) collected from the Tongkou River in October 2023 and November 2024 using tissue digestion methods across different river sections. Ultimately, this research provides essential baseline data to help authorities develop effective strategies to reduce plastic pollution in freshwater ecosystems and protect aquatic wildlife.

Adaptive Mechanisms of Endemic Fish Triplophysa yarkandensis in the Tarim River Basin of Northwestern China
👤 Bibi, Sadia; Zhu, Yafan; Liu, Changcai; Yang, Liting; Wang, ... 📅 2026-10-10
Dryland river ecosystems are among the most susceptible freshwater environments, with climate change, hydrological shifts, salinization and anthropogenic disturbance posing increasing threats to aquatic biodiversity. Endemic fishes in these regions are particularly vulnerable because of restricted geographic range, ecological specialization and narrow environmental tolerance limits, which reduce their capacity to cope with rapid environmental change. Triplophysa yarkandensis has a coordinated set of adaptive responses such as benthic morphological specialization, adaptable trophic modes, osmoregulatory ability to cope with saline–alkaline stress, and thermal tolerance mediated by stress-sensitive genes. Future studies must combine long-term field studies, multi-stressor experimental systems, and genomic–ecophysiological designs to define adaptive limits and improve conservation outcomes under continued climate change.

Length-weight Relationships and Growth Patterns of Fish Assemblages in the Ikot Okubara River, Nigeria
👤 Asuquo, IdopiseAbasi E.; Asuquo, Gwendoline I.; Ukpatu, John... 📅 2026-10-10
Aims: To derive length-weight relationship (LWR) parameters and characterize growth patterns for the fish assemblage of the Ikot Okubara River, establishing the first published baseline reference values for this previously undocumented West African tributary. Study Design: Descriptive cross-sectional field survey with laboratory morphometric analysis. Place and Duration of Study: Ikot Okubara River (approximately 5°02′01.84″N, 7°56′01.04″E), Ediene District, Abak Local Government Area, Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria, sampled monthly from May to November 2024. Methodology: A total of 269 individual fish belonging to 19 species were obtained using encircling nets, cast nets and non-return valve traps operated by local fishers. Specimens were identified using regional keys, measured for total length (TL, nearest 0.1 cm) and total weight (TW, nearest 0.1 g). Parameters a and b of the relationship TW = a·TLᵇ were estimated by ordinary least-squares (OLS) regression on log10-transformed data. Growth type was classified relative to the isometric reference value b = 3. Species with N < 5 were reported descriptively only and excluded from growth-pattern interpretation. Results: Total length ranged from 8.0 cm to 32.0 cm; total weight ranged from 4.3 g to 607.8 g. Growth coefficients (b) for species with N ≥ 5 spanned 0.3551 (Papyrocranus afer) to 2.6403 (Oreochromis niloticus). The numerically dominant species, Oreochromis niloticus (N = 122), exhibited negative allometric growth (b = 2.6403, r = 0.8803). High correlation coefficients (r > 0.86) were obtained for most well-sampled species. Conclusion: The study supplies the first LWR reference values for the Ikot Okubara River fish community. These parameters provide a quantitative baseline against which future changes in growth pattern or condition can be evaluated.

Reduced size of larvae and small fish linked to warming and reduced prey density
👤 Lindmark, Max; Werner, Malin; Thor, Peter; Maioli, Federico;... 📅 2026-10-10
Body size is an integrative trait that has declined across many ecological assemblages in the last decades. Changes in body size of early life stages and small‐bodied fish are driven by complex interactions between size‐dependent mortality, and temperature‐ and food‐dependent growth, and can have consequences for recruitment to the adult stock and food availability to predators. While the mechanisms can be difficult to disentangle using observational data alone, simple indicators such as mean size can provide important information about ecosystem conditions as oceans rapidly change. Using 30 years of observations from a long‐term ichthyoplankton survey in Skagerrak and Kattegat in combination with sea surface temperature data, we investigated changes in the body size of early life stages and small‐bodied fishes for 10 commercial and non‐commercial species using geostatistical mixed models, and trends in prey density using generalized additive models. We found positive associations between chlorophyll‐a concentration and length in 8 species (3 significant), and negative associations between temperature and length in 9 species (4 significant). Standardized indices of length revealed negative trends over time for all species since 2010, and all species were smaller in 2024 compared to 2000. The decline in size since 2010 varied between 30 and 1%, with a mean of 14 and 9% for species predominantly found in Skagerrak and Kattegat, respectively. The trend of decreased body sizes since 2010 coincides with rapid declines in Calanus spp., which constitute a key prey that is also predicted to decline in this range‐edge area due to climate change. In Skagerrak it also coincides with a decline in the density of other calanoid copepods larger than 0.25 mm. The synchronous declines in larval size across taxonomically diverse species experiencing different rates of exploitation suggest a common response to changing environmental conditions, which could have cascading effects throughout marine food webs.

Wintering Fish Habitat of Atlantic Chub Mackerel ( Scomber colias ) Southern Stock in SW Atlantic Ocean
👤 Parietti, Manuela; Spath, M. Cecilia; Orlando, Paula; Menna,... 📅 2026-10-11
ABSTRACT The Atlantic chub mackerel ( Scomber colias ) is an important pelagic resource in the Southwestern Atlantic Ocean (SWAO), supporting socially and economically significant fisheries in Argentina. Two underexploited stocks occur on either side of 39° S, yet their economic importance and contribution to local employment require continued monitoring and informed management. During the austral winter, when most catches occur, the southern stock aggregates in the El Rincón area (ER), forming large, dense schools with pronounced vertical structure. This study identified the wintering habitat of S. colias and characterised the environmental conditions associated with this critical life‐history stage. Acoustic data from acoustic‐oceanographic surveys and official fishing reports between 2010 and 2024 were analysed. The presence and abundance of S. colias were evaluated, and bottom temperature and salinity, water column stability and depth were used as fixed predictors in generalised linear models (GLMs). The results demonstrate that these four environmental factors were related to the winter distribution of S. colias , enabling the delineation of its winter habitat, corroborated by the performance of the commercial fleet fishing on the ER. Combining fishery‐independent and fishery‐dependent data, a wintering habitat is characterised, with schools consistently occupying a narrow corridor aligned with the 50 m depth, defined by vertically homogeneous and thermally stable waters, with a restricted SBT (9.09°C–10.86°C) and SBS ranges (33.51–34.22). Identifying the environmental drivers shaping winter habitat use provides valuable insights for designing improved monitoring strategies and implementing spatially informed management measures for this socio‐economically important species.

In this study, we present four consecutive years (2021–2024) of integrated environmental DNA (eDNA) monitoring and larval surveys for walleye pollock ( Gadus chalcogrammus ) conducted aboard the Korean icebreaker IBR/V Araon in the Chukchi Sea (CS) and Central Arctic Ocean (CAO). Of the 342 eDNA samples from 180 stations (55°N–76°N, spanning the Bering Sea, CS, and CAO, including underway surface‐pump stations), walleye pollock eDNA was detected in 25 samples (7.3%), with annual detection rates ranging from 2.8% to 13.2%. Overall, these findings provide the first multi‐year molecular evidence that walleye pollock occur episodically at higher latitudes than previously documented, with detections spatially correlated with warmer subsurface bottom temperatures. However, a causal relationship cannot be established because hydrodynamic transport data were not available.

Abstract Invasive species may exhibit varied reproductive capacity at the edge of their distributions. The dynamic habitat conditions of prairie streams along the edge of invasive Silver Carp Hypophthalmichthys molitrix ranges may limit reproduction. The objectives of this study were to assess the spatial and seasonal variation in female Silver Carp reproductive readiness, reproductive phases and reproductive subphases across the western invasion front in the Platte River basin in Nebraska, USA. Batch fecundity, Gonadosomatic Index (GSI) and reproductive phases were assessed for female Silver Carp collected seasonally in 2022 and 2023. Reproduction of Silver Carp in prairie streams appears probable given the prevalence of reproductively capable individuals. A Scoping Review of Sanitary Survey Methodology for Shellfish Harvesting Areas Across Seven European Jurisdictions
👤 Campos, Carlos J. A.; Purnell, Sarah; Farkas, Kata 📅 2026-10-10
Sanitary surveys support the classification of shellfish harvesting areas and the design of microbiological monitoring programs under European shellfish hygiene legislation. These surveys identify sources of fecal contamination and assess the effect of environmental factors on shellfish water quality. This study presents a scoping review of 110 sanitary survey reports from seven European countries (Scotland, England, Wales, Ireland, France, Denmark and Portugal), covering the period 2007–2026. Reports were identified from websites of research organizations, regulatory agencies, and technical contractors responsible for sanitary survey preparation, and screened against inclusion criteria based on regulatory requirements and methodological detail. A structured data extraction framework based on the PRISMA-ScR extension for scoping reviews was used to harmonize survey components across reports. Extracted variables included catchment characterization, wastewater treatment and discharge reporting, meteorological and hydrodynamic assessment, shoreline survey implementation, justification of representative monitoring points, and design of monitoring plans. Substantial heterogeneity in report content and analytical approaches was observed. Rainfall was considered in all 110 reports, but formal statistical evaluation of relationships between rainfall and microbiological contamination was undertaken in 43/110 reports (39%). At least one statistically significant rainfall–microbiology relationship was explicitly reported in 36/110 reports (33%). Hydrodynamic modelling was used in 53/110 reports (48%), with substantial variation among jurisdictions. While all surveys identified potential sources of fecal contamination, the inclusion of quantitative indicators such as wastewater population equivalents, discharge characteristics and statistical rainfall analyses varied considerably. Hydrodynamic assessments ranged from descriptive tidal characterization to model-based dispersion analyses, and the justification of monitoring locations and harvesting area boundaries differed across reports. These results indicate that sanitary surveys are not methodologically standardized across the jurisdictions included in this review. Greater harmonization of reporting standards and environmental analysis could improve transparency and comparability in shellfish classification systems and overall shellfish hygiene.
