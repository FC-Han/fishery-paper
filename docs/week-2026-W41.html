First occurrence of a parasitic isopod, Braga nasuta (Schioedte & Meinert, 1881) on ornamental fish in Europe
👤 Hoitsy, Márton; Gál, János; Sós, Endre; Nagy, Antal; Hornok,... 📅 2026-10-08
Abstract Isopods of the genus Braga (Cymothoidae) are haematophagous parasites of neotropical fish, causing anaemia, tissue damage and mortality. Braga nasuta , endemic to Brazil, has been reported from various hosts, including ornamental species exported worldwide. During a routine examination of imported cardinal tetra ( Paracheirodon axelrodi , Schultz, 1956), the authors detected a fish harbouring an adult male isopod (6.21 mm long, 2.66 mm wide) attached anterior to the dorsal fin. Based on morphological identification, the parasite was B. nasuta . No secondary pathogens were observed in the affected fish specimen. Given the global trade in ornamental species, the introduction of B. nasuta into non-native regions underscores the importance of quarantine and parasite monitoring. Phylogenetic analysis has revealed that B. nasuta is most closely related to other freshwater parasitic isopods. While the potential ability of this parasite to establish populations in Europe requires further studies, vigilance is warranted to prevent potential spread and ecological damage.

👤 Nwachukwu, Ujunwa Felicia; Onyia, Ikechukwu Jude 📅 2026-10-08

However, some factors, including inadequate processing, handling, and storage, may compromise their nutritional quality and safety. Therefore, this study assessed the nutritional quality and food safety of dry fish and fermented oil bean seed (Ukpaka) obtained from Eke Agbani Market, Enugu State, Nigeria. The dry fish and fermented oil bean seed collected from different vendors were respectively inoculated into Nutrient Agar (NA), MacConkey Agar, Salmonella-Shigella Agar (SSA), de Man Rogosa Sharpe (MRS) agar and Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA). The inoculated plates were incubated at 37°C for 24–48 h, except for SDA plates which were incubated at 28°C for 7 days. Therefore, improved hygienic handling, processing, and storage practices are essential to preserve the nutritional quality of these indigenous foods and to ensure consumers’ safety.

Rabbitfish Integration in Offshore Gilthead Seabream Polyculture: Improving Growth Performance, Reducing Disease Burden, and Mitigating Biofouling in Tropical Aquaculture
👤 Mansour, Nabil; Ismail, Ahmed G.; Alhmoudi, Sabhah; Lamghari... 📅 2026-10-07
Abstract Gilthead seabream ( Sparus aurata ) is one of the most commercially significant species in the Mediterranean and Middle Eastern marine aquaculture, yet intensified production systems expose this species to persistent challenges, including net biofouling, parasitic disease, and reduced growth efficiency. This case study is based on research conducted over two full production cycles (2023–2024) in open-sea cages at Omega Fish Farm, Dadna, Fujairah, United Arab Emirates. The study compared six cages of seabream monoculture with six cages of polyculture incorporating white-spotted rabbitfish ( Siganus canaliculatus ), a herbivorous species with well-documented grazing activity on algal and invertebrate fouling organisms. Key production indicators, including growth rate (GR), specific growth rate (SGR), feed conversion ratio (FCR), feeding rate, and mortality, were assessed throughout the production cycles using area under the curve (AUC) analysis. Biofouling biomass, annelid abundance, and seabream net-biting frequency were evaluated during the first six months of each production cycle. Overall, polyculture significantly improved seabream production performance compared with monoculture and significantly reduced net biofouling biomass, annelid abundance, and seabream net-biting frequency. Enteromyxum leei infection prevalence and severity were also reduced in polyculture cages. During the highest-risk period, peak prevalence was 29.4%, with a median infection degree of 2.5, compared with 49.8% and 4.0, respectively, in monoculture cages. In addition, net replacement intervals were extended to 344 days under polyculture compared with 196 days under monoculture. These findings demonstrate that integrating rabbitfish into offshore seabream production can simultaneously improve production performance, reduce biofouling and parasitic disease burden, and extend net service life, providing a biologically sound and operationally practical strategy for enhancing the sustainability of marine aquaculture in tropical environments. However, overfishing and habitat degradation have resulted in a marked decline in its wild populations, and the species was listed as a Class II nationally protected aquatic wildlife species in China in 2021. Spermatogonial stem cells (SSCs) provide a valuable resource for fish germplasm conservation and reproductive biotechnology. 👤 Brazauskas, Mantas; Povilaitis, Arvydas 📅 2026-10-09
Closed, low-exchange systems are promoted for sustainable intensification, yet recirculating aquaculture systems (RASs) and biofloc technology (BFT) are rarely compared on a common evidence base. This review compares them across water quality, growth, feed conversion, stocking density, biosecurity and sustainability, using African catfish (Clarias gariepinus) as a reference and appraising each claim against predefined criteria. Bibliometric analysis in two databases shows that the fields barely intersect (2.1% and 3.3% of corpora); tilapia and shrimp account for 85% of biofloc records against 1.9% for C. gariepinus, for which one indexed comparison exists. BFT improved feed conversion in eight of ten trials, but this benefit is feeding-mode dependent: significant for the omnivorous species tested alongside African catfish but not for the catfish itself, whose case for BFT rests on expected capital and water savings, each reported in one trial, and robustness. Short trials largely capture establishment, not operation; whole-cycle trials on commercial species remain the priority.
