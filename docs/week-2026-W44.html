人工智能与水产养殖可持续发展研究热点

本周研究概览

本周共收录8篇渔业相关论文，研究方向涵盖渔业治理与政策（采购问责、鲑鱼养殖标签与追溯）、渔业生态学（温盐锋对饵料鱼营养生态的影响、鳟鱼捕获放流的能量代价）、水产养殖工程与微生物学（RAS生物滤器低温运行、鱼类加工废弃物堆肥）、水产病害快速检测（*Perkinsus olseni* RPA-PfAgo检测）以及海洋生物技术（深度学习辅助抗菌肽发现）。整体来看，本周论文以应用导向为主，治理法规类与病害检测类研究占比偏高，基础生态学研究相对有限；跨学科方法（深度学习、等温扩增）向水产领域的渗透是值得关注的趋势。本周无高影响力综合期刊论文发表。

创新性研究

  1. Deep-Learning-Assisted Discovery of Marine Antimicrobial Peptides（*Marine Drugs*）— 该综述提出将组学挖掘与结构引导的扩散模型设计相整合的技术路线，并明确指向可持续水产养殖的转化应用。其创新点在于不止于候选肽生成，而是将效力、宿主安全性、稳定性与可制造性纳入同一设计框架，为水产抗菌替代方案提供了系统化路径。
  1. Recombinase Polymerase Amplification Combined With PfAgo for Rapid Detection of *Perkinsus olseni*（*Journal of Fish Diseases*）— 将RPA等温扩增与可编程核酸酶PfAgo切割相结合，实现对OIE名录病原*P. olseni*的快速检测，有望摆脱传统培养法和PCR对设备与时间的依赖。该方法对贝类养殖现场诊断具有直接应用潜力。
  1. Long-Term Low- and Normal-Temperature Operation of Commercial RAS Biofilters（*Microorganisms*）— 针对商业规模RAS生物滤器在低温胁迫下的微生物群落响应开展长期观测，弥补了以往多基于实验室规模短期的不足。研究对比了低温与常温条件下群落结构与氮循环功能预测的差异，对寒冷地区RAS运行管理具有参考价值。
  1. Energetic costs of catch and release angling in Brook Trout at two temperatures（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 将C&R的生理胁迫量化为恢复期的能量支出与摄食停滞代价，并考察温度的双重调节作用。该研究把休闲渔业管理中的"放流即无害"假设置于能量学检验之下，对变暖背景下C&R规范制定有直接意义。

<b>Characterization of low-cost aquaculture systems in three municipalities of the mountainous region of Veracruz, Mexico</b>
👤 Campos Páez, Edgardo; Cabal Prieto, Adán; Becerra-Ferniza, A... 📅 2026-10-30
Objective: To characterize small-scale aquaculture systems and identify their key productive, technical, commercial, and social conditions in three municipalities of the mountainous region of Veracruz. Design/methodology/approach: A survey was conducted on eight aquaculture farms using the snowball sampling technique. The questionnaire was structured in two phases: the first collected general information about the interviewees, whereas the second focused on obtaining information regarding the production systems and the implementation of Good Management Practices (GMP) in aquaculture. The data obtained were analyzed using descriptive statistics, specifically absolute frequencies and percentages, with the Microsoft 365 software package. Results: In the mountainous region of Veracruz, semi-intensive systems predominate for the production of rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) and tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), with male (62.5%) and female (37.5%) participation. Aquaculture is integrated as a complementary activity to conventional agricultural practices. Farms with less than five years of operation exhibited limitations in technical knowledge, insufficient water quality monitoring, and a lack of production records and sanitary practices. Despite the government support received by more than 50% of producers, production levels ranged from 50 to 1,000 kg per cycle, with an average fish weight of 500 ± 265 g. Production was intended for both household consumption (0.5–5 kg/week/family) and commercialization. Limitations on study/implications: Survey implementation was limited within the mountainous region; therefore, applying the instrument to a larger number of aquaculture farms and/or producers would provide a more comprehensive characterization of these production systems. Findings/conclusions: Aquaculture systems in the mountainous region of Veracruz constitute an emerging activity that is highly accepted among small-scale producers because of their contribution to the availability of animal protein within local communities. However, continuous technical training is required to enhance sustainability, production efficiency, and integration with complementary production systems such as aquaponics.

Identification and Characterization of a Cathepsin B Protease in the Marine Fish Parasite Cryptocaryon irritans
👤 Chi, Hongshu; Pan, Ying; Liu, Xiaodong; Lin, Nengfeng; Jiang... 📅 2026-10-30
Cryptocaryon irritans, an obligate ectoparasite, causes cryptocaryoniasis and heavy economic losses to marine aquaculture. Cathepsin B (CatB) is highly expressed in C. irritans theronts and hypothesized to participate in parasite host invasion, yet its biochemical properties and pathogenic roles remain poorly defined. In this study, we performed bioinformatic analysis of the CiCatB gene. Following codon optimization, CiCatB was heterologously expressed in Pichia pastoris, and the biologically active recombinant CiCatB protein was purified. Subcellular localization analysis revealed that CiCatB predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of theronts, with partial signals detected at the cell membrane and in the extracellular space. Enzymatic assays showed that yCiCatB hydrolyzed gelatin and type I collagen from large yellow croaker, with optimum activity at pH 5.0 and stable performance from 28 to 37 °C. Mg2+ increased enzyme activity, while Cu2+ almost completely abolished catalytic activity. Both E-64 and anti-CiCatB serum inhibited enzyme activity. Functional blocking using inhibitor or antiserum markedly reduced parasite recovery following challenge. Our results suggest that CiCatB may contribute to host invasion by degrading host structural proteins, advancing our understanding of C. irritans pathogenicity. CiCatB represents a potential target for exploring control strategies against cryptocaryoniasis.

Integrated technology for processing salmonid fish milt to obtain biologically active DNA and hyaluronidase
👤 Gerasimchik, Georgii; Mukhametshina, Ekaterina; Kuprina, Ele... 📅 2026-10-30
Introduction: Fish milt is currently used predominantly as a culinary product, despite containing numerous biologically active compounds with high value-added potential, including nucleoproteins, enzymes, and proteins. Its integrated processing creates an opportunity to recover biologically valuable compounds, including DNA and hyaluronidase, within a single technological cycle. Objective: To develop an integrated technology for the processing of fish milt with the sequential recovery of DNA and hyaluronidase while preserving their target properties. Materials and Methods: Frozen fish milt was mechanically homogenized and extracted at reduced temperature with acetic acid using a raw material-to-extractant ratio of 1:2. Following centrifugation, the soluble fraction containing hyaluronidase was clarified and concentrated by ultrafiltration. The precipitate containing the DNA–protein complex was hydrolyzed with trypsin at a water-to-precipitate-to-trypsin ratio of 2:1:0.3 and subsequently centrifuged to isolate the DNA fraction. Hyaluronidase activity was determined using a pharmacopeial method. The enzyme-containing fraction was additionally characterized by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy. DNA quality was assessed by spectrophotometry; residual protein in the DNA fraction was quantified using the micro-biuret method, and the preparation was additionally characterized by infrared spectroscopy. The yields of DNA and the hyaluronidase-containing fraction were determined using a rapid drying method at 170 °C. Results: The developed technological scheme enabled recovery of the target products from a single raw material. The DNA yield was 11.3% of the initial raw material mass. The final fraction contained 60–65% DNA, and the A260/A280 ratio ranged from 1.80 to 1.90, indicating relatively high purity. The yield of the hyaluronidase-containing fraction was 4.8% of the initial raw material mass on a dry-weight basis. Ultrafiltration increased specific activity of hyaluronidase-containing fraction 1.77-fold, from 1375 to 2437 IU/mg. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy confirmed the proteinaceous nature of the enzyme-containing preparation. Novelty of the Study: This study proposes an integrated technological scheme for fish milt processing that enables the sequential recovery of a hyaluronidase-containing fraction and a DNA fraction from a single raw material. The use of ultrafiltration as a concentration step increases enzyme activity without requiring expensive chromatographic purification, thereby improving the practical feasibility of the process. Conclusions: The proposed technology enables rational utilization of fish milt as a secondary biological raw material and allows recovery of biologically valuable DNA and a hyaluronidase-containing fraction within a single production cycle. The developed approach is technologically feasible and promising for further application in biotechnology, food processing, and pharmaceutical production. Keywords: fish milt, DNA, hyaluronidase, ultrafiltration, integrated processing, bioactive compounds.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN AQUACULTURE: APPLICATIONS, RESEARCH GAPS, AND IMPLICATIONS FOR PROFESSIONAL EDUCATION AND GOVERNANCE — A SYSTEMATIC LITERATURE REVIEW
👤 Marinho-Pereira, Thiago; Cavero, Bruno; Brandão, Lian Valent... 📅 2026-11-01
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly used in aquaculture to support monitoring, prediction, automation, and production management, while also creating new requirements for professional competence, ethical oversight, and governance. This systematic literature review, conducted under an integrative approach and reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020, examined practical applications of AI and their implications for professional education and governance in aquaculture. Searches in Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and Google Scholar identified 877 records, of which 97 met the eligibility criteria and were included in the qualitative synthesis. The evidence was concentrated on technical applications, particularly fish detection and biomass estimation, water and environmental monitoring, automated production systems, health and biosecurity, and feeding and growth optimization. Direct educational evidence was scarce: only one study examined aquaculture professionals' knowledge and interest in AI, and no included study evaluated formal curricular interventions or learning outcomes in Fisheries Engineering. The synthesis also identified concerns involving data quality, algorithmic bias, technological exclusion, privacy, standardization, and accountability. These findings indicate that responsible adoption requires locally representative data, transparent validation, digital and data literacy, human oversight, and coordinated governance. AI has substantial potential to support aquaculture, but its contribution to professional education remains an important and insufficiently investigated research field.

Assessment of Dietary Sources in a Tropical Coastal Fish (Blackspotted Croaker Protonibea diacanthus)
👤 Randall, Jo; Kaestli, Mirjam; Adair, Brendan; Wedd, Dion; Gi... 📅 2026-10-30
Abstract Energy and nutrients from rivers and coastal floodplains are widely recognised as key drivers of productivity in coastal marine food webs, but these inputs may increase productivity for some species while having negligible or negative effects on others. Understanding the sources of nutrition supporting commercially important fishes is therefore critical for effective management. Here, we quantify the relative contribution of terrestrial, freshwater, and marine resources to the diet of the marine sciaenid Protonibea diacanthus in the wet–dry tropics of northern Australia. Bulk stable isotopes (δ¹³C, δ¹⁵N, δ³⁴S) and compound-specific stable isotope analysis of essential amino acids (δ¹³C) were used to resolve trophic pathways, alongside microbial source tracking to assess the influence of freshwater and marine water sources at aggregation sites. Across all approaches, results consistently indicated a dominant reliance on marine-derived resources. Mixing models identified marine sources, particularly marine fish, as the primary contributors to diet, while CSIA-AA independently demonstrated that carbon supporting P. diacanthus was overwhelmingly marine in origin. There was little evidence of freshwater or terrestrial influence in either trophic pathways or microbial assemblages, regardless of season or proximity to river outflows. These findings contrast with studies of other tropical fisheries species that show strong positive relationships with freshwater flow, and suggest that the nutrition of P. diacanthus is supported primarily by marine trophic pathways rather than riverine subsidies. This study highlights the need for caution when generalising the role of freshwater inputs across species and emphasises the importance of species-specific trophic dynamics in coastal ecosystem management.

Processing–Property Relationships in Fish Protein Hydrolysates: From Controlled Hydrolysis and Peptidomics to Sensory Performance and Translational Readiness
👤 Gubaidullin, Nurtay; Gajimuradova, Aisarat; Syzdykova, Alfiy... 📅 2026-10-30
Fish processing generates protein-rich side streams representing 30–70% of whole-fish mass, creating potential for production of fish protein hydrolysates (FPHs). However, their translation into food or nutraceutical ingredients is limited by variable feedstocks, incomplete process reporting and an evidence base dominated by in vitro assays. This review examines how feedstock origin and pre-analytical quality, controlled enzymatic hydrolysis and downstream processing determine peptide composition and techno-functional, sensory and biological performance. It also evaluates peptidomic workflows and grades evidence from chemical assays through randomized human interventions. Across the reviewed literature, FPHs emerge as heterogeneous, lot-defined products rather than a uniform ingredient class. Maximum degree of hydrolysis is not a universally optimal endpoint: extensive proteolysis may improve solubility and assay responses while reducing protein recovery, interfacial functionality and sensory acceptability. Peptide numbers and candidate sequences depend on liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) acquisition, database selection, search strategy and false-discovery control. In silico predictions therefore require confirmation with synthetic standards, targeted quantification in the hydrolysate and evaluation of gastrointestinal stability and intestinal transport. Human findings remain limited and inconsistent, precluding class-wide health claims. Beyond Automation: An Observability and Error Framework for AI-Enabled Marine Recreational Fisheries Monitoring
👤 Martin-Podevin, Loéva; Gorkin, Robert; Hughes, Julian; Aubin... 📅 2026-10-30
Monitoring marine recreational fishing remains difficult because effort and catches are spatially dispersed, participation is heterogeneous, and reporting is rarely mandatory. This narrative review asks what artificial intelligence (AI) can automate and when AI-derived observations can support defensible fisheries inference. We synthesise established on- and off-site monitoring methods, direct recreational-fisheries applications of AI, and transferable evidence from commercial fisheries and aquaculture. Across methods, the limitation is observability: each monitoring approach captures a subset of fishing, and greater data volume does not necessarily increase population representativeness. AI primarily expands the speed, scale and richness of information extraction within observable events while introducing its own measurement and model errors. We propose an observation-to-decision framework in which sampling and coverage error, sensor and measurement error, algorithmic error and estimation uncertainty are considered jointly. Transferability is treated as a domain-shift problem requiring external validation across sites, seasons, devices and species. The most defensible near-term architecture is a calibrated hybrid system in which probability-based surveys anchor population inference, direct observations provide validation, and AI-enabled cameras or angler images provide high-frequency measurement. The value of AI to fisheries management should therefore be judged by the validity of the complete monitoring system, not by algorithmic accuracy alone.

A Pathway to Sustainable Development: The Relationship Between Fishery Digitalization and the Green Transformation of Fisheries
👤 Li, Haohui; Li, Sihang; Tu, Yubo; Zhong, Changbiao 📅 2026-10-30
Using panel data from 30 Chinese provinces spanning 2011–2024, this study assesses the digitalization and green transformation of fisheries using the entropy-weighted TOPSIS method and the super-efficiency epsilon-based measure (EBM) model, respectively. A two-way fixed-effects model is then employed to examine the relationship between the digitalization and green transformation of fisheries. The results reveal a positive association that persists across a series of robustness and endogeneity checks. Analysis of potential mediating channels indicates positive indirect associations through aquaculture intensification, aquatic product processing efficiency, and fishery industrial structure upgrading, whereas the fishing efficiency channel is not statistically supported. Heterogeneity analysis shows that the positive association is stronger in Eastern China and becomes more pronounced as regional innovation and human capital levels increase. Spatial analysis further reveals spatial dependence in the green transformation of fisheries and a positive association between local fishery digitalization and the green transformation of fisheries in neighboring regions.

Inland fisheries market feasibility of sharptooth catfish (Clarias gariepinus) in the Eastern Cape, South Africa
👤 J Pegg; C Welgemoed; OLF Weyl 📅 2026-10-30
Despite increasing interest in inland fisheries as a means of supporting food security, livelihoods, and rural development, inland fisheries in South Africa remain poorly integrated into food systems, with significant uncertainty regarding supply chains, consumer demand, and product quality. We assessed the market feasibility of sharptooth catfish (Clarias gariepinus) harvested through the Darlington Alien Fish Harvest Project in the Eastern Cape, South Africa. Over a 9-month trial, 761 kg of catfish were procured, processed into retail products (fillets, burgers, smoked portions, and offcuts), and tested in both formal and informal markets in Makhanda, Eastern Cape. High fat content and visible trematode cysts rendered 32% of fish unsuitable for retail. Of the fish considered fit for consumption, processed products such as burgers and fillets sold moderately well in formal outlets, while offcuts were the most successful product in township markets. The main challenge to the viability of the project, however, was not quality but economics. Whole catfish were purchased at 8–10 ZAR/kg, but erratic supply and transport costs inflated landed prices by more than 50%, reducing competitiveness against established products such as hake (Merluccius capensis) and carpenter (Argyrozona argyrozona). This case study revealed two key findings: First, it provides evidence that new consumer markets can be developed for fish products from South African inland fisheries. Second, successful fishery development requires attention not only to harvesting, but also to supply chains, processing, and market development. These findings provide practical evidence to support implementation of South Africa's inland fisheries policy.
