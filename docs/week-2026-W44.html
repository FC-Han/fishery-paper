<b>Characterization of low-cost aquaculture systems in three municipalities of the mountainous region of Veracruz, Mexico</b>
👤 Campos Páez, Edgardo; Cabal Prieto, Adán; Becerra-Ferniza, A... 📅 2026-10-30
Objective: To characterize small-scale aquaculture systems and identify their key productive, technical, commercial, and social conditions in three municipalities of the mountainous region of Veracruz. Design/methodology/approach: A survey was conducted on eight aquaculture farms using the snowball sampling technique. The questionnaire was structured in two phases: the first collected general information about the interviewees, whereas the second focused on obtaining information regarding the production systems and the implementation of Good Management Practices (GMP) in aquaculture. The data obtained were analyzed using descriptive statistics, specifically absolute frequencies and percentages, with the Microsoft 365 software package. Results: In the mountainous region of Veracruz, semi-intensive systems predominate for the production of rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) and tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), with male (62.5%) and female (37.5%) participation. Aquaculture is integrated as a complementary activity to conventional agricultural practices. Farms with less than five years of operation exhibited limitations in technical knowledge, insufficient water quality monitoring, and a lack of production records and sanitary practices. Despite the government support received by more than 50% of producers, production levels ranged from 50 to 1,000 kg per cycle, with an average fish weight of 500 ± 265 g. Production was intended for both household consumption (0.5–5 kg/week/family) and commercialization. Limitations on study/implications: Survey implementation was limited within the mountainous region; therefore, applying the instrument to a larger number of aquaculture farms and/or producers would provide a more comprehensive characterization of these production systems. Findings/conclusions: Aquaculture systems in the mountainous region of Veracruz constitute an emerging activity that is highly accepted among small-scale producers because of their contribution to the availability of animal protein within local communities. However, continuous technical training is required to enhance sustainability, production efficiency, and integration with complementary production systems such as aquaponics.

Identification and Characterization of a Cathepsin B Protease in the Marine Fish Parasite Cryptocaryon irritans
👤 Chi, Hongshu; Pan, Ying; Liu, Xiaodong; Lin, Nengfeng; Jiang... 📅 2026-10-30
Cryptocaryon irritans, an obligate ectoparasite, causes cryptocaryoniasis and heavy economic losses to marine aquaculture. Cathepsin B (CatB) is highly expressed in C. irritans theronts and hypothesized to participate in parasite host invasion, yet its biochemical properties and pathogenic roles remain poorly defined. In this study, we performed bioinformatic analysis of the CiCatB gene. Following codon optimization, CiCatB was heterologously expressed in Pichia pastoris, and the biologically active recombinant CiCatB protein was purified. Subcellular localization analysis revealed that CiCatB predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of theronts, with partial signals detected at the cell membrane and in the extracellular space. Enzymatic assays showed that yCiCatB hydrolyzed gelatin and type I collagen from large yellow croaker, with optimum activity at pH 5.0 and stable performance from 28 to 37 °C. Mg2+ increased enzyme activity, while Cu2+ almost completely abolished catalytic activity. Both E-64 and anti-CiCatB serum inhibited enzyme activity. Functional blocking using inhibitor or antiserum markedly reduced parasite recovery following challenge. Our results suggest that CiCatB may contribute to host invasion by degrading host structural proteins, advancing our understanding of C. irritans pathogenicity. CiCatB represents a potential target for exploring control strategies against cryptocaryoniasis.

Fish Responses to Total Dissolved Gas Supersaturation: From the Perspective of Historical Experimental Data in the Upper Yangtze River
👤 Rao, Jianing; Pan, Rongxuan; Wang, Yuanming; Feng, Jingjie; ... 📅 2026-10-29
During discharge from high dams, the high-velocity water flows mix with air, causing downstream total dissolved gas (TDG) supersaturation, threatening fish survival. However, a systematic understanding on fish response patterns under the coupled influence of multiple environmental and biological factors remains limited. This study synthesized historical experimental data to explore fish tolerance and avoidance in response to TDG supersaturation in the upper Yangtze River. Economic fish species demonstrated overall stronger tolerance than the rare fish species under all TDG levels. Significant interspecific differences in economic fish tolerance were observed with increasing TDG levels. Aristichthys nobilis demonstrated stronger tolerance than Hypophthalmichthys molitrix. For the rare fish, Procypris rabaudi showed the weakest tolerance. Exposure time, TDG level, species and water temperature were identified as the primary factors influencing fish mortality. Based on these, mortality prediction formulas for rare and economic fish were established, achieving an R2 over 0.59. The avoidance ability of fish increased significantly with increasing TDG levels and reached 50% at 130% TDG. Significant interspecific differences in fish avoidance were observed at a 140% TDG level or above, and Schizothorax prenanti showed the weakest avoidance ability. TDG level was the dominant factor influencing fish avoidance ability, followed by body length. Accordingly, a prediction formula for fish avoidance rates was proposed with an R2 of 0.88. The findings offer critical insights for ecological risk management and the development of fish conservation strategies downstream of high dams.

Identifying Strategic Drivers and Development Priorities for Sustainable Pearl Lobster Grow-Out Aquaculture: An Integrated MICMAC-AHP Framework
👤 Haj, Muhammad Hairul; Baso, Aris; Adhawati, Sri Suro 📅 2026-10-29
The sustainable farming of pearl lobsters (Panulirus ornatus) demands strategic measures that account for institutional, economic, ecological, technological, and social factors. This study identifies the main strategic drivers of sustainable grow-out aquaculture and prioritizes development interventions in South Sulawesi, Indonesia. Data were obtained from 54 key informants representing government agencies, researchers, extension officers, exporters, collectors, aquaculture workers, and coastal communities through interviews, focus-group discussions, observations, and structured questionnaires. The Matrix of Cross-Impact Multiplications Applied to Classification (MICMAC) was used to identify structural drivers, while the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was used to establish relative development priorities. The direct MICMAC analysis identified Regulatory Framework as linkage variables, while Multi-Actor Coordination and Aquaculture Innovation occupied the linkage position. The AHP results (CR = 0.047) assigned the highest criterion weight to institutional factors (0.339), followed by economic (0.242), technological (0.154), ecological (0.154), and social (0.111) factors. The highest global priorities were Multi-Actor Coordination (0.176), Regulatory Framework (0.163), Production Cost (0.109), and Aquaculture Innovation (0.100). The integrated MICMAC–AHP framework indicates that governance capacity, coordination, and technological innovation are important leverage points for sustainable pearl lobster grow-out aquaculture in South Sulawesi.

Integrated technology for processing salmonid fish milt to obtain biologically active DNA and hyaluronidase
👤 Gerasimchik, Georgii; Mukhametshina, Ekaterina; Kuprina, Ele... 📅 2026-10-30
Introduction: Fish milt is currently used predominantly as a culinary product, despite containing numerous biologically active compounds with high value-added potential, including nucleoproteins, enzymes, and proteins. Its integrated processing creates an opportunity to recover biologically valuable compounds, including DNA and hyaluronidase, within a single technological cycle. Objective: To develop an integrated technology for the processing of fish milt with the sequential recovery of DNA and hyaluronidase while preserving their target properties. Materials and Methods: Frozen fish milt was mechanically homogenized and extracted at reduced temperature with acetic acid using a raw material-to-extractant ratio of 1:2. Following centrifugation, the soluble fraction containing hyaluronidase was clarified and concentrated by ultrafiltration. The precipitate containing the DNA–protein complex was hydrolyzed with trypsin at a water-to-precipitate-to-trypsin ratio of 2:1:0.3 and subsequently centrifuged to isolate the DNA fraction. Hyaluronidase activity was determined using a pharmacopeial method. The enzyme-containing fraction was additionally characterized by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy. DNA quality was assessed by spectrophotometry; residual protein in the DNA fraction was quantified using the micro-biuret method, and the preparation was additionally characterized by infrared spectroscopy. The yields of DNA and the hyaluronidase-containing fraction were determined using a rapid drying method at 170 °C. Results: The developed technological scheme enabled recovery of the target products from a single raw material. The DNA yield was 11.3% of the initial raw material mass. The final fraction contained 60–65% DNA, and the A260/A280 ratio ranged from 1.80 to 1.90, indicating relatively high purity. The yield of the hyaluronidase-containing fraction was 4.8% of the initial raw material mass on a dry-weight basis. Ultrafiltration increased specific activity of hyaluronidase-containing fraction 1.77-fold, from 1375 to 2437 IU/mg. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy confirmed the proteinaceous nature of the enzyme-containing preparation. Novelty of the Study: This study proposes an integrated technological scheme for fish milt processing that enables the sequential recovery of a hyaluronidase-containing fraction and a DNA fraction from a single raw material. The use of ultrafiltration as a concentration step increases enzyme activity without requiring expensive chromatographic purification, thereby improving the practical feasibility of the process. Conclusions: The proposed technology enables rational utilization of fish milt as a secondary biological raw material and allows recovery of biologically valuable DNA and a hyaluronidase-containing fraction within a single production cycle. The developed approach is technologically feasible and promising for further application in biotechnology, food processing, and pharmaceutical production. Keywords: fish milt, DNA, hyaluronidase, ultrafiltration, integrated processing, bioactive compounds.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN AQUACULTURE: APPLICATIONS, RESEARCH GAPS, AND IMPLICATIONS FOR PROFESSIONAL EDUCATION AND GOVERNANCE — A SYSTEMATIC LITERATURE REVIEW
👤 Marinho-Pereira, Thiago; Cavero, Bruno; Brandão, Lian Valent... 📅 2026-11-01
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly used in aquaculture to support monitoring, prediction, automation, and production management, while also creating new requirements for professional competence, ethical oversight, and governance. This systematic literature review, conducted under an integrative approach and reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020, examined practical applications of AI and their implications for professional education and governance in aquaculture. Searches in Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and Google Scholar identified 877 records, of which 97 met the eligibility criteria and were included in the qualitative synthesis. The evidence was concentrated on technical applications, particularly fish detection and biomass estimation, water and environmental monitoring, automated production systems, health and biosecurity, and feeding and growth optimization. Direct educational evidence was scarce: only one study examined aquaculture professionals' knowledge and interest in AI, and no included study evaluated formal curricular interventions or learning outcomes in Fisheries Engineering. The synthesis also identified concerns involving data quality, algorithmic bias, technological exclusion, privacy, standardization, and accountability. These findings indicate that responsible adoption requires locally representative data, transparent validation, digital and data literacy, human oversight, and coordinated governance. AI has substantial potential to support aquaculture, but its contribution to professional education remains an important and insufficiently investigated research field.
