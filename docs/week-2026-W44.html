水产养殖多组学与智能监测研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录8篇渔业相关论文，研究方向涵盖渔业治理与政策（采购问责、鲑鱼养殖标签与追溯）、渔业生态学（温盐锋对饵料鱼营养生态的影响、鳟鱼捕获放流的能量代价）、水产养殖工程与微生物学（RAS生物滤器低温运行、鱼类加工废弃物堆肥）、水产病害快速检测（*Perkinsus olseni* RPA-PfAgo检测）以及海洋生物技术（深度学习辅助抗菌肽发现）。整体来看，本周论文以应用导向为主，治理法规类与病害检测类研究占比偏高，基础生态学研究相对有限；跨学科方法（深度学习、等温扩增）向水产领域的渗透是值得关注的趋势。本周无高影响力综合期刊论文发表。

  1. Energetic costs of catch and release angling in Brook Trout at two temperatures（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 将C&R的生理胁迫量化为恢复期的能量支出与摄食停滞代价，并考察温度的双重调节作用。该研究把休闲渔业管理中的"放流即无害"假设置于能量学检验之下，对变暖背景下C&R规范制定有直接意义。

The influence of accountability on procurement performance: a case study ministry of blue economy and fisheries Zanzibar
👤 Manzi, Abdillah; Sama, Hamisi 📅 2026-10-26
Accountability is a foundational pillar of procurement governance, yet quantitative evidence on how accountability mechanisms specifically shape procurement outcomes within individual government ministries in Zanzibar remains scarce. This study examined the influence of accountability on procurement performance at the Ministry of Blue Economy and Fisheries (MoBEF), Zanzibar. Guided by Institutional Theory and attached in a positivist research philosophy, study adopted a descriptive and explanatory quantitative design where a structured, five-point Likert-scale questionnaire was administered to 85 respondents drawn from a target population of 108 procurement officers, tender board members, finance officers, user department representatives, internal auditors, and registered suppliers, selected through stratified random sampling using Yamane’s (1967) formula. Data were analyzed descriptively and inferentially using IBM SPSS version 23, incorporating reliability testing, correlation, and multiple linear regressions. Descriptive results revealed that accountability practices were generally well established at MoBEF, with mean scores ranging from 3.90 to 4.12. Review and approval procedures and documentation practices attracted the strongest endorsement, while disciplinary enforcement for procurement irregularities recorded both the lowest mean (M = 3.90) and the greatest variability (SD = 0.89), indicating a structural enforcement gap within the Ministry’s accountability system. Multiple regression analysis confirmed that accountability significantly and positively predicted procurement performance (β = 0.27, SE = 0.07, t = 3.57, p = .001), alongside fairness (β = 0.31, p < .001) and transparency (β = 0.29, p = .002); the overall model was statistically significant (F(3, 81) = 28.45, p < .001, R² = 0.520). The null hypothesis was rejected. These findings confirm that while formal accountability structures are well established within MoBEF, inconsistent disciplinary enforcement limits their full effect on procurement performance. The study recommends strengthening audit follow-through, enforcing consistent sanctions for non-compliance, and institutionalizing structured monitoring systems.

India and the WTO Fisheries Subsidies Agreement: The Unfinished Equity Question
👤 Acharya, Kamlesh Kumar; Sirohi, Smita 📅 2026-10-26
India formally accepted the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies in July 2026, but this did not bring the negotiations to an end. The Agreement bans subsidies tied to illegal and unregulated fishing and overfished stocks, but broader subsidy rules contributing to overcapacity and overfishing remain unsettled. This article highlights India’s evolution from prioritizing the protection of small-scale and artisanal fisheries to currently challenging the use of aggregate subsidy levels for differentiation. Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) data from 2020 to 2022 indicate that while total support figures for OECD members and non-members appear similar, the average support per fisher significantly differs, at $5,722 for members and $283 for non-members. This comparison supports India’s argument, but environmental harm depends on subsidy type, fleet capacity, fishing location, stock condition and fisheries management rather than support per fisher alone. India’s acceptance endorses current agreements and raises concerns about equitable burden distribution for further regulations among countries with varied fishing sectors and historical subsidies. Comparison of three sperm-FISH protocols to optimize population-scale screening in cattle
👤 Kowal-Mierzwa, Wiktoria; Parma, Pietro; Battelli, Marco; Ian... 📅 2026-10-26
Abstract Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) is widely used in chromosome structure and organization studies, playing a key role in diagnosing chromosomal aberrations in somatic and germ cells. A critical step in preparing spermatozoa for sperm-FISH analysis is effective nuclear chromatin decondensation. Sperm chromatin is highly condensed, posing a barrier to the proper hybridization of molecular probes. Insufficient or excessive decondensation can result in unclear fluorescent signals, reducing the reliability of analysis. Since optimizing decondensation is essential for achieving precise and reproducible results, this study aimed to optimize chromatin decondensation in domestic cattle spermatozoa using three different sperm-FISH protocols, to identify a protocol suitable for future population-scale sperm-FISH analyses. The research material consisted of semen from 10 bulls obtained in collaboration with the Malopolska Biotechnic Center in Krasne. Three separate protocols were applied: Protocol 1 involved decondensation in 1 M DTT in PBS; Protocol 2 used a papain–DTT solution; and Protocol 3 involved treatment with a 3 M NaOH solution. All three protocols resulted in a percentage of properly decondensed sperm, but Protocol 3 yielded a statistically higher proportion of this type. It provided well-decondensed sperm chromatin, clear fluorescent signals specific to the studied chromosomes, and allowed reliable detection of normal and abnormal signals. Alkaline denaturation may provide more stable chromatin decondensation in bovine spermatozoa, thereby improving hybridization efficiency and signal clarity.

👤 Suryana, Asep Agus Handaka; Millatina, Nabilah Sani; Andrian... 📅 2026-10-26
This study examines the application of Floating Net Karamba (KJA) technology in aquaculture in Cirata Reservoir, particularly in Margalaksana Village, Cipeundeuy District, West Bandung Regency. It focuses on KJA construction, water-quality conditions and cultivators' technological adaptations. The case study was conducted from August 2024 to April 2025 in three KJA blocks (Gandasoli, Cikuda and Cipicung) and involved 70 purposively selected respondents cultivating red tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) and common carp (Cyprinus carpio). Overall, aquaculture in Cirata Reservoir continues to face water-quality challenges, particularly concerning DO. The three-spot seahorse (Hippocampus trimaculatus Leach) is among the most heavily harvested seahorse species in Vietnam. Due to overexploitation and habitat degradation, H. trimaculatus is listed in Appendix II of CITES (2014), the IUCN Red List (IUCN, 2025), and the Vietnam Red List of Threatened Species (Vietnam Academy of Science and Technology, 2024). The potential for large-scale genome editing in finfish aquaculture via germline stem cell culture and transplantation
👤 Raudstein, Mari; Datsomor, Alex Kojo; Stewart, Robert; McGre... 📅 2026-10-26
Genome editing has emerged as a promising tool to complement selective breeding programmes and accelerate genetic gain in aquaculture. Although CRISPR-based genome editing has been successfully applied in several economically important fish species, its application at an industry-relevant scale remains challenging due to limitations of current delivery methods, which primarily rely on manual embryo microinjection. This approach is labour-intensive, technically demanding, and frequently results in high levels of mosaicism in founder individuals, necessitating additional generations of breeding to establish stable genotypes. In this mini review, we discuss the potential of combining germline stem cell (GSC) culture and transplantation approaches as an alternative platform for further improved genome editing in fish. By enabling genetic changes to be introduced, validated, and expanded in vitro prior to germline transmission, this approach has the potential to improve scalability, reduce mosaicism, and shorten breeding timelines. Although several technical challenges remain, continued progress in GSC culture and transplantation methodologies could facilitate the practical implementation of tailored genome editing for improved genetic gain, fish health, and welfare in aquaculture.

Proteomic signatures of vaccine-induced immunity in teleost fish: from antigen discovery to correlates of protection
👤 Rao, Shreesha Sadashiva; Prasad, T. S. Keshava 📅 2026-10-26
Fish-vaccine efficacy in teleosts is still established primarily through pathogen-challenge experiments, which determine whether a formulation protects but provide limited insight into the antigens and immune mechanisms responsible. Proteomics addresses part of this limitation by measuring pathogen proteins and host responses induced by vaccination. This critical narrative review evaluates pathogen-focused and host-response proteomics from antigen discovery through biomarker validation and translational decision-making. We assess quantitative mass spectrometry, immunoproteomics, surfaceome and secretome profiling, and their integration with reverse and systems vaccinology. Across the limited teleost evidence base, vaccination-associated changes involve complement and acute-phase pathways, antigen processing, redox regulation, hemostasis, tissue repair and exosome-associated antigen presentation. Cross-study replication remains weak, and the reported proteins largely represent study-specific vaccine-responsive profiles. More importantly, most studies do not test whether pre-challenge signatures predict subsequent survival, pathogen control or tissue protection. Within the literature reviewed here, no candidate proteomic signature has yet been prospectively replicated in an independent teleost vaccination cohort; therefore, none currently meets the requirements of a validated, transferable correlate of protection. The main obstacles include heterogeneous study designs, incomplete reference proteomes, teleost-specific protein-inference challenges, small cohorts, limited longitudinal sampling and scarce external validation. Environment-adaptive Acoustic–Optical sensing for fish abundance estimation in complex aquaculture waters
👤 Wang, Yao; Cheng, Xiangyu; Lu, Yuchen; Zhao, Qinglong; Zhu, ... 📅 2026-10-26
Abstract Reliable fish abundance estimation is essential for the operation and management of aquaculture systems, but remains challenging under turbidity, bubble interference, target occlusion, and acoustic reverberation. This study develops an Acoustic–Optical fusion sensing framework for robust fish monitoring in complex aquaculture waters. An environment-aware geometric fusion network, termed EAGF-Net, is proposed to integrate optical appearance information with sonar-derived geometric features. The network employs modality-specific feature extraction with an orthogonality constraint, sonar-guided spatial compensation, and adaptive gating to regulate modality contributions according to environmental conditions. Asymmetric cross-attention is further introduced to reduce semantic inconsistency and suppress unreliable acoustic or optical responses. For densely distributed fish, a keypoint-based topological association method uses fish-eye features and geometric constraints to separate overlapping individuals. The resulting observations are incorporated into Kalman-filter-based trajectory management to reduce missed and repeated counts. Fish abundance is then inferred by combining trajectory flux with the effective sensing volume determined by the Acoustic–Optical field of view and survey path. Experiments covering clear water, dense schooling, turbidity, and bubble disturbance show that the proposed fusion network achieves an F-measure of 0.924 and an average counting accuracy of 96.40%. Field-scale tests in eight aquaculture ponds estimate 1,939 fish against a reference count of 1,975, corresponding to a relative error of 1.80%. These results demonstrate the potential of environment-adaptive Acoustic–Optical sensing for non-invasive fish abundance monitoring in aquaculture engineering applications.

Policy Support and Farmers’ Ecological Aquaculture Behavior: The Mediating Role of Ecological Awareness
👤 Jiang, Shuyang; Hu, Yanfu 📅 2026-10-27
Promoting farmers’ adoption of ecological aquaculture practices is important for the environmental sustainability and green transformation of aquaculture. Using survey data from 278 aquaculture farmers in Zhangzhou, Fujian Province, China, this study examines the relationship between policy support and farmers’ ecological aquaculture behavior. Policy support is measured across three dimensions—financial and credit support, agricultural insurance support, and technical and knowledge support—and the potential mediating role of ecological awareness is further examined. The results show that policy support is significantly and positively associated with farmers’ ecological aquaculture behavior, and each of the three dimensions also exhibits a significant positive association when examined separately. Further analysis indicates that policy support is significantly and positively associated with ecological awareness, which plays a significant partial mediating role in the relationship between policy support and farmers’ ecological aquaculture behavior. These findings suggest that promoting the environmental sustainability of aquaculture requires attention to farmers’ financial, risk-related, and technical constraints, as well as to the role of ecological awareness in linking policy support to actual production behavior.

A Fish-Oriented Heavy Metal Pollution Index for the Danube River
👤 Bezdan, Atila; Bezdan, Jovana 📅 2026-10-27
The Heavy Metal Pollution Index (HPI) usually includes the metals that have a regulatory standard rather than those most relevant to fish. Its usual weights also give most influence to the metals with the strictest standards. This study developed a fish-oriented HPI for the Danube River. A data-coverage screen and an expert evaluation with the PAPRIKA method selected eight metals: Cd, Pb, Hg, Cu, Ni, Zn, As and Cr. They were combined with equal weights so that each selected metal contributes to the index. The index was calculated for 6467 sampling events at 43 stations in eight countries (1998–2022), and values below detection limits were estimated by multiple imputation. A generalised additive mixed model showed that the index increased about fourfold from the upper to the lower Danube and tended to decline over time. The downstream increase persisted under most alternative methods. In a Serbian reach surveyed for fish in 2022, the index in 2020–2022 was in the upper third of the basin, mainly because of Ni and Cu, and the fish community was dominated by eurytopic and non-native species. Selecting metals by their relevance to fish, and weighting them so that this selection matters, offers a transferable approach for fish-relevant contamination indices in large rivers.
