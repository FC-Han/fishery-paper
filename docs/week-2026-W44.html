Aquaculture extension and outreach professionals in the Northeastern United States are working in a sector shaped by evolving industry needs, workforce turnover, and complex position requirements. The influence of accountability on procurement performance: a case study ministry of blue economy and fisheries Zanzibar
👤 Manzi, Abdillah; Sama, Hamisi 📅 2026-10-26
Accountability is a foundational pillar of procurement governance, yet quantitative evidence on how accountability mechanisms specifically shape procurement outcomes within individual government ministries in Zanzibar remains scarce. This study examined the influence of accountability on procurement performance at the Ministry of Blue Economy and Fisheries (MoBEF), Zanzibar. Guided by Institutional Theory and attached in a positivist research philosophy, study adopted a descriptive and explanatory quantitative design where a structured, five-point Likert-scale questionnaire was administered to 85 respondents drawn from a target population of 108 procurement officers, tender board members, finance officers, user department representatives, internal auditors, and registered suppliers, selected through stratified random sampling using Yamane’s (1967) formula. Data were analyzed descriptively and inferentially using IBM SPSS version 23, incorporating reliability testing, correlation, and multiple linear regressions. Descriptive results revealed that accountability practices were generally well established at MoBEF, with mean scores ranging from 3.90 to 4.12. Review and approval procedures and documentation practices attracted the strongest endorsement, while disciplinary enforcement for procurement irregularities recorded both the lowest mean (M = 3.90) and the greatest variability (SD = 0.89), indicating a structural enforcement gap within the Ministry’s accountability system. Multiple regression analysis confirmed that accountability significantly and positively predicted procurement performance (β = 0.27, SE = 0.07, t = 3.57, p = .001), alongside fairness (β = 0.31, p < .001) and transparency (β = 0.29, p = .002); the overall model was statistically significant (F(3, 81) = 28.45, p < .001, R² = 0.520). The null hypothesis was rejected. These findings confirm that while formal accountability structures are well established within MoBEF, inconsistent disciplinary enforcement limits their full effect on procurement performance. The study recommends strengthening audit follow-through, enforcing consistent sanctions for non-compliance, and institutionalizing structured monitoring systems.

👤 Acharya, Kamlesh Kumar; Sirohi, Smita 📅 2026-10-26
India formally accepted the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies in July 2026, but this did not bring the negotiations to an end. The Agreement bans subsidies tied to illegal and unregulated fishing and overfished stocks, but broader subsidy rules contributing to overcapacity and overfishing remain unsettled. This article highlights India’s evolution from prioritizing the protection of small-scale and artisanal fisheries to currently challenging the use of aggregate subsidy levels for differentiation. Comparison of three sperm-FISH protocols to optimize population-scale screening in cattle
👤 Kowal-Mierzwa, Wiktoria; Parma, Pietro; Battelli, Marco; Ian... 📅 2026-10-26
Abstract Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) is widely used in chromosome structure and organization studies, playing a key role in diagnosing chromosomal aberrations in somatic and germ cells. A critical step in preparing spermatozoa for sperm-FISH analysis is effective nuclear chromatin decondensation. Sperm chromatin is highly condensed, posing a barrier to the proper hybridization of molecular probes. Insufficient or excessive decondensation can result in unclear fluorescent signals, reducing the reliability of analysis. Since optimizing decondensation is essential for achieving precise and reproducible results, this study aimed to optimize chromatin decondensation in domestic cattle spermatozoa using three different sperm-FISH protocols, to identify a protocol suitable for future population-scale sperm-FISH analyses. The research material consisted of semen from 10 bulls obtained in collaboration with the Malopolska Biotechnic Center in Krasne. Three separate protocols were applied: Protocol 1 involved decondensation in 1 M DTT in PBS; Protocol 2 used a papain–DTT solution; and Protocol 3 involved treatment with a 3 M NaOH solution. All three protocols resulted in a percentage of properly decondensed sperm, but Protocol 3 yielded a statistically higher proportion of this type. It provided well-decondensed sperm chromatin, clear fluorescent signals specific to the studied chromosomes, and allowed reliable detection of normal and abnormal signals. Alkaline denaturation may provide more stable chromatin decondensation in bovine spermatozoa, thereby improving hybridization efficiency and signal clarity.

👤 Spreitzenbarth, Stefan; Alder, Al; MacKechnie, Fiona; McKay,... 📅 2026-10-28
ABSTRACT A key bottleneck in the development of octopus aquaculture is the absence of formulated feeds that are readily consumed and fulfil their nutritional requirements. Until efficacious formulated feeds are developed, sustainably‐sourced natural seafood diets provide a potential viable alternative for octopus aquaculture. To investigate this alternative, this study measured feed intake, growth and biochemical composition of juvenile Octopus tetricus when fed bivalves discarded from shellfish aquaculture. Three dietary treatments were tested over 56 days: (1) whole thawed bivalves (natural feed), (2) bound pellet made from chopped bivalves bound with gelatine and transglutaminase (GO pellet) and (3) bound pellet made from chopped bivalves and krill meal, bound with gelatine and transglutaminase (GOK pellet). Overall, the mean specific feeding rate (wet weight) among octopus was highest in the natural feed (12.1 ± 1.5% BW day −1 ) regardless of time, indicating inferior palatability in the two bound pelleted feeds. Mean specific growth rates were higher from Day 0–28 (1.8 ± 0.2% BW day −1 ) than from Day 29–56 (1.0 ± 0.2% BW day −1 ) independently of dietary treatment. Mean feed efficiency (23.3 ± 3.3%) and feed conversion (5.5 ± 0.9) were similar among treatments for the whole experiment. The biochemical profile of octopuses was similar among treatment aside from a 30% increase in lipid content in arm tissue for octopuses fed with the natural feed. The results show that discarded bivalves from commercial aquaculture are an effective feed for juvenile O . tetricus providing fast growth and good feed conversion. While consumption and growth were reduced with the bound pelleted feeds, these wet seafood‐based feeds hold promise as a route to begin to develop formulated feeds for octopuses while utilising an otherwise wasted resource, especially if the palatability of these bound pelleted feeds can be improved.

👤 Schriener, William; Wuellner, Melissa R.; Koupal, Keith D. 📅 2026-10-28
ABSTRACT Physical structures, constructed from either natural or artificial materials, are added to aquatic systems to provide habitat for fish. Attraction to new structures is well documented, but few studies have compared attraction on added structures to existing or bare areas in multiple reservoirs or species. Thus, the objective of this study was to compare species richness and diversity and abundances of target taxa over newly added structures constructed of different materials to those over nearest existing available habitat and an open site with no apparent habitat structures within three reservoirs where sampling was conducted across seasons and years. Cedar brush piles and Georgia cube complexes were placed in into three reservoirs in October 2020. Existing physical habitats and areas devoid of any physical structure were also selected near added structures. Sampling was conducted in spring, summer, and fall 2021 and 2022 using boat‐mounted electrofishing. Richness and diversity were generally higher over existing or added structure than over open habitat. However, none of our target taxa showed consistent association to a single habitat type between reservoirs, and seasonal differences in habitat use were common. Our results point towards the need to understand the inherent factors, such as existing habitat availability, the recipient fish community, and abiotic factors on the outcomes of adding physical habitat structure in reservoirs. 👤 Hauser-Davis, Rachel Ann; Maciel, Oswaldo Luiz de C. 📅 2026-10-27
Seafood is an important component of human nutrition worldwide but also represents a major dietary pathway of arsenic (As) exposure. Assessing seafood-related health risks remains challenging because As toxicity depends strongly on chemical speciation rather than total concentration alone. Although seafood often contains high total arsenic (tAs), most occurs as organic compounds such as arsenobetaine, generally regarded as low in toxicity and rapidly excreted in humans. In contrast, inorganic arsenic (iAs), primarily arsenite [As(III)] and arsenate [As(V)], is highly toxic and carcinogenic. Emerging evidence further challenges the traditional assumption that organic arsenicals are uniformly harmless, as methylated intermediates have demonstrated cytotoxic and genotoxic properties, and some arsenolipid subclasses, particularly arsenic-containing hydrocarbons, have demonstrated cytotoxicity. Despite the importance of As speciation assessments, routine species-specific analysis remains limited in seafood monitoring due to the limited availability of validated methods and certified reference materials, as well as relatively high operational costs, and limited laboratory accessibility. Consequently, many exposure assessments rely on tAs concentrations and generalized assumptions regarding iAs fractions, despite substantial variability among seafood taxa, habitats, and geographic regions. Human exposure assessments are further complicated by marked differences in seafood consumption patterns and demographic susceptibility, with coastal and fishing-dependent communities often experiencing substantially greater dietary exposure than population averages suggest. This review synthesizes current knowledge on As occurrence and speciation in seafood, analytical approaches, toxicological implications of different arsenic species, and human exposure and risk assessment frameworks, including Estimated Daily Intake (EDI), Estimated Weekly Intake (EWI), Target Hazard Quotient (THQ), Hazard Index (HI), and Target Cancer Risk (TCR). Particular emphasis is placed on taxonomic variability in iAs occurrence, localized seafood consumption, demographic heterogeneity, and emerging toxicological evidence. Collectively, the evidence supports a transition from tAs-based screening toward more realistic, speciation-informed and context-dependent seafood risk assessment.
