渔业污染与生态风险：从汞蓄积到毒性预测研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文7篇，研究主题集中于鱼类入侵生态学、营养生态学、渔业海洋学及水产养殖基因组学等方向。其中，入侵生物学占据较大比重（3篇），涵盖全球尺度入侵风险评估、深海aphotic zone入侵记录及外来与本土鱼类的营养生态位重叠分析；此外，渔业资源变动与环境驱动因子的关联研究、鱼类黏膜免疫活性物质开发以及龙虾基因组资源综述亦各有涉及。整体来看，本周研究呈现出从传统资源评估向多学科交叉（机器学习、分子生物学、遥感技术）拓展的趋势，且对生态入侵与气候变化背景下渔业资源响应的关注度显著提升。

高影响力期刊亮点

本周暂无发表于Nature、Science、PNAS、Nature Climate Change、Nature Ecology & Evolution、Nature Sustainability、Nature Communications、Science Advances等高水平期刊的论文。

创新性研究

  1. Assessing Invasion Risk and Associated Economic Loss of Fish Species Across Global Watersheds Using Machine Learning Algorithms（*Water*）— 该研究将机器学习算法引入全球流域尺度的鱼类入侵风险评估，并首次将经济损失量化纳入预测框架，实现了生态风险与经济影响的耦合分析，为入侵物种管理提供了决策支持新工具。
  1. Invasives in the dark: An established population of the arrow bulleye *Priacanthus sagittarius* on the aphotic continental slope of the eastern Mediterranean（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究首次记录鱼类入侵至地中海aphotic zone（无光层），打破了此前入侵记录几乎全部局限于沿岸及上陆架生境的认知，提示深海生态系统同样面临生物入侵威胁，拓展了入侵生态学的研究边界。
  1. Trophic niche overlap between native and non-native fishes in central Anatolian lakes, Türkiye（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究系统比较了安纳托利亚中部湖泊中8种本土与5种外来鱼类的食性组成、猎物选择及种间营养重叠，揭示了外来种与高特有性本土鱼类之间的竞争格局，为生物多样性热点区域的入侵管理提供了精细化生态学依据。
  1. Tracking Eastward Displacement of Pacific Saury Fishing Grounds Using Nighttime Fishing Radiance: The Role of Early-Season Kuroshio Extension Variability（*Fisheries Oceanography*）— 该研究创新性地利用夜间渔船灯光遥感数据追踪秋刀鱼渔场东移过程，并将其与黑潮延伸体早期季节变异建立关联，为理解气候变化背景下小型中上层鱼类资源再分布提供了新的观测手段与分析视角。

Dietary parsley seed powder attenuates acrylamide‐waterborne toxicity hazards on growth, redox status, immunity and gene regulations in Oreochromis niloticus
👤 Naiel, Mohammed A. E.; Zaki, Eman M.; Abdelhakim, Taghrid M.... 📅 2026-08-09
Abstract This study evaluated the protective role of dietary parsley seed powder (PSP) against acrylamide (ACR) toxicity in Nile tilapia ( Oreochromis niloticus ). A total of 200 juvenile fish (15.98 ± 0.01 g) were randomly assigned to four dietary groups (0.0, 2.5, 5.0 and 10.0 g PSP/kg diet) and exposed for 56 days to a sublethal ACR concentration (3.46 mg/L). Throughout the experiment, growth performance, blood biochemical indices, immune responses, oxidative stress biomarkers, digestive enzyme activities, histopathology and hepatic gene expression were thoroughly assessed. The results revealed that PSP supplementation, particularly at 10.0 g/kg, significantly improved final body weight, body weight gained, daily weight gain and survival rate ( p ≤ 0.05), while reducing feed conversion ratio significantly at 2.5 g/kg ( p ≤ 0.001). Moreover, blood AST activity was significantly decreased in the 5.0 and 10.0 g/kg groups ( p ≤ 0.01), accompanied by significantly elevated total protein and albumin ( p < 0.01), and reduced uric acid and creatinine levels ( p ≤ 0.001), indicating hepatoprotective and nephroprotective effects. Additionally, PSP exerted anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant benefits, as evidenced by significantly reduced serum tumour necrosis factor‐α (TNF‐α) levels ( p ≤ 0.001) and increased glutathione (GSH) concentration ( p ≤ 0.05), especially in the 10.0 g/kg group. Digestive enzymes (amylase and pepsin) exhibited a significant increase at all PSP levels ( p ≤ 0.05 or 0.01), with the highest levels observed in the DP 3 group (10.0 g PSP/kg diet). Histopathological evaluation indicated a dose‐dependent attenuation of intestinal and hepatopancreatic alterations with increasing PSP levels, as evidenced by reduced lesion severity, decreased necrotic changes and better preservation of tissue architecture. Furthermore, gene expression analysis revealed significant downregulation of cysteine–aspartic acid protease‐3 ( caspase‐3 ) and tumour necrosis factor‐alpha 2 ( tnf‐2α ) genes ( p ≤ 0.01) and upregulation of insulin‐like growth factor 1 ( igf‐1 ) and growth hormone ( gh ) ( p ≤ 0.01) genes in the 10.0 g/kg group, indicating enhanced growth and cellular resilience. In conclusion, dietary PSP at 10.0 g/kg significantly alleviates ACR toxicity by enhancing physiological, biochemical, immunological and molecular responses in Nile tilapia.

Mercury (Hg) accumulation in the organs and muscle tissue of commercial fish species in water reservoirs of the Middle Volga
👤 Revukhin, A. A.; Shurganova, G. V.; Kudrin, I. A.; Voronin, ... 📅 2026-08-09
The paper provides a comparative analysis of mercury (Hg) accumulation in the muscle tissue and organs of commercial fish in the Gor’ky and Cheboksary reservoirs. The research material consisted of 120 specimens of four fish species, namely: Blicca bjoerkna , Abramis brama , Rutilus rutilus , and Perca fluviatilis . The mercury concentrations in the muscles, liver, gills, and gonads were analyzed for each species group. The results showed a pronounced species specificity of accumulation, namely: the highest Hg concentrations were recorded in the muscle tissue and liver, the lowest it in the gills and gonads. Predatory perch is characterized by a higher mercury level, which reflects the influence of its trophic position. Correlation and covariance analysis revealed a significant dependence of mercury concentration in muscles on body length in most fish species, whereas this dependence was less pronounced and variable for the liver, gills, and gonads. The data obtained confirm the informative value of muscle tissue as an indicator of long-term mercury pollution and emphasize the need to take into account the species composition and size-age structure in the environmental monitoring of reservoirs.

Isolation and identification of Pseudomonas fluoeroscens and evaluation of its antimicrobial sensitivity from fish in Lake Haramaya, East Hararghe Zone, Oromia Regional State, Ethiopia
👤 Feleke, Melaku Getahun; Edao, Mustefa Hinika; Yilhal, Getach... 📅 2026-08-09
Abstract Pseudomonas fluorescens ( P. fluorescens ) is an opportunistic Gram-negative bacterium associated with disease in freshwater and marine fish, resulting in economic losses to aquaculture. This study aimed to isolate and identify P. fluorescens from wild Nile tilapia ( Oreochromis niloticus ) and African catfish ( Clarias gariepinus ) collected from Lake Haramaya, Ethiopia, and to evaluate the antimicrobial susceptibility profile of the isolates. A cross-sectional study was conducted from December 2022 to May 2023. A total of 50 fish (32 Nile tilapia and 18 African catfish) were sampled, and 250 swab specimens were collected from the skin, gills, intestine, liver, and kidney. Isolation and identification were performed using conventional cultural, morphological, and biochemical methods. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was conducted on 10 representative presumptive isolates using the Kirby–Bauer disk diffusion method according to Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines. Presumptive P. fluorescens was detected in 21 of 50 (42.0%) wild fish examined. A total of 27 presumptive isolates were recovered from the sampled organs based on their cultural, morphological, and biochemical characteristics. The highest isolate-level occurrence was observed in the skin (20%), followed by the intestine (14%), gills (12%), liver (6%), and kidney (2%). Bacterial occurrence differed significantly among organs ( P < 0.05), whereas no significant association was observed with fish species or sex ( P > 0.05). Among the 10 representative isolates tested, all were resistant to tetracycline and ciprofloxacin, whereas 90% and 70% were susceptible to kanamycin and gentamicin, respectively. These findings provide preliminary evidence of the occurrence of presumptive P. fluorescens and antimicrobial-resistant isolates in wild fish from Lake Haramaya. Molecular confirmation of species identity and comprehensive antimicrobial susceptibility testing using a larger number of isolates are needed, as identification was based solely on phenotypic methods and antimicrobial susceptibility testing was limited to 10 representative isolates. The findings establish baseline information for future surveillance of P. fluorescens and antimicrobial resistance in Ethiopian freshwater ecosystems within a One Health framework.
