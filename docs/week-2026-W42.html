水产养殖可持续发展与鱼类生理适应研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录4篇渔业相关论文，全部发表于 *Journal of Fish Biology*，研究方向涵盖分子生态学方法、物种杂交与保护遗传学、气候变化下的鱼类分布变动以及水产养殖免疫机制。整体来看，本周研究呈现出两个鲜明特征：一是方法学导向的研究（如DNA保存方案优化）为后续生态-遗传数据整合提供技术支撑；二是应用导向的研究（如杂交监测、北极鳕鱼分布扩展、细菌感染免疫应答）直接回应保护管理与养殖生产的现实需求。值得注意的是，多篇论文均涉及人为活动（如气候变暖、栖息地改造）对鱼类种群的影响，反映出当前渔业科学对全球变化背景下种群响应机制的高度关注。

创新性研究

  1. Active nuclease inhibition over passive freezing secures the genomic archive of fish eye lenses（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新点在于系统比较了主动核酸酶抑制与被动冷冻两种策略对鱼眼晶状体基因组DNA保存效果的影响，并首次为这一兼具生态与遗传信息价值的组织提出了标准化保存方案。鱼眼晶状体可提供个体生活史的时间序列档案，但死后核酸酶降解长期制约着生态与遗传数据的整合。该方法的建立有望推动鱼类个体水平“生态-基因组”联合研究在野外和博物馆样本中的广泛应用。

  1. Widespread hybridization in sympatric populations of allis shad *Alosa alosa* and twaite shad *Alosa fallax*: A transnational perspective for conservation and management（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究以跨国尺度揭示了同域分布的西鲱（allis shad）与芬塔西鲱（twaite shad）之间存在广泛的杂交现象，创新性地将杂交格局与人为环境改造联系起来。研究结果对两种鲱鱼的保护单元界定和渔业管理策略提出了直接挑战，提示在制定保护措施时需充分考虑杂交对遗传完整性的潜在侵蚀。

  1. Seasonal presence, movements and behaviour of Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) in Isfjorden, Svalbard（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究聚焦北极化（Atlantification）背景下大西洋鳕鱼向斯瓦尔巴群岛峡湾的北向扩张，通过监测其季节性出现、移动和行为模式，提供了 boreal 物种入侵北极生态系统的直接行为学证据。其创新之处在于将气候变化驱动的海洋环境变动与鱼类个体行为响应相衔接，为预测北极渔业资源重组提供了关键基线数据。

  1. Transcriptomic analysis reveals the immune response mechanisms of *Acrossocheilus fasciatus* against *Citrobacter freundii* infection（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究结合组织学观察与RNA-seq技术，系统解析了光唇鱼（*Acrossocheilus fasciatus*）肠道和脾脏对弗氏柠檬酸杆菌感染的免疫应答机制。其创新点在于首次为该经济鱼种提供了细菌感染的转录组水平免疫图谱，为水产养殖中该病的防控和抗病育种提供了候选基因资源。

👤 Marques Chagas, Paulo Augusto; Campanharo, Sarah Chagas; Pin... 📅 2026-10-17
Abstract Aquaculture plays an increasingly important role in global food production, but intensive freshwater fish aquaculture still faces major challenges related to infectious diseases and the use of antimicrobials. Antimicrobials are commonly administered orally through medicated feed, a practice often compromised by drug leaching before ingestion, heterogeneous feed intake, and temperature- and species-dependent pharmacokinetic variability. These factors can generate heterogeneous antimicrobial exposure in fish, ranging from subtherapeutic concentrations to compensatory overdosing practices while simultaneously increasing the entry and persistence of antimicrobial residues in water and sediments, thereby creating selective pressure that favors the emergence and maintenance of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquatic microbial communities. This integrative review focuses primarily on intensive freshwater fish aquaculture systems and examines how antimicrobial use contributes to AMR through interconnected pharmacological, microbiological, and environmental pathways. The review discusses how water temperature, fish physiology, feeding behavior, and dosing inconsistencies influence antimicrobial absorption, disposition, therapeutic performance, residue persistence, and resistance selection. Intrinsic, acquired, and adaptive resistance mechanisms are also discussed with emphasis on the role of environmental contamination in maintaining resistance reservoirs. Within this framework, the review analyzes nanoenabled strategies designed to address these bottlenecks, including drug delivery systems that improve control over drug administration, gastrointestinal stability, bioavailability, and release, as well as nanotechnologies for residue removal and environmental monitoring. By integrating host- and environment-oriented perspectives, this Review highlights how nanotechnology may help reduce antimicrobial dependence, limit selective pressure, and support more sustainable fish aquaculture within a One Health framework.

A Fish Journey through Data: Long-Term Monitoring and Red Mullet Stock Status
👤 Georgieva, Yoana; Klayn, Stefania 📅 2026-10-12
Red mullet ( Mullus barbatus ) is one of the most valuable demersal fish species in the Black Sea, supporting important commercial fisheries while playing a key ecological role in benthic food webs. Due to its short life span (4-6 years), rapid growth, and sensitivity to environmental changes and fishing pressure, the species serves as an effective indicator of the states of the coastal ecosystems. Hence, biological long-term monitoring became essential for evaluating population dynamics and supporting sustainable fisheries management. This study implement fisheries-dependent long term monitoring data (2015–2025) to evaluate changes in the status of red mullet stock along the Bulgarian Black Sea coast. In the Black Sea, length-based indicators are crucial since the age determination remains affected by inconsistencies in otolith interpretation among laboratories, whereas length measurements are standardized, routinely collected, and highly reproducible. Annual analyses included total length, body weight, condition factor, and cumulative length-frequency distributions. These analyses were complemented by long-term ecosystem projections from an Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) model to evaluate future biomass and catch trajectories under unchanged fishing pressure (F status quo). Mean total length (10.9–12.6 cm) and body weight (15.5–23.7 g) showed clear inter annual variability, while condition factor remained consistently stable, indicating good individual condition throughout the study period. Length-frequency distributions revealed generally stable population size structure but also marked year-to-year differences, with 2017 and 2023 characterized by a greater proportion of small individuals and 2024 by larger fish. Approximately 35% of the catch consisted of fish below the estimated size at first maturity (11.2 cm TL), indicating persistent harvesting of immature individuals despite the relative stability of the stock structure. Ecosystem model projections further suggest that, if current fishing mortality is maintained, both biomass and catches may decline by approximately 13% by 2050. The red mullet stock in the Black Sea remains overexploited (GFCM 2024). These results demonstrate the importance of long-term ecological monitoring for detecting gradual changes in exploited populations that may not be detected by the short-term data. Although the overall length structure remained relatively stable, the persistent occurrence of immature individuals in the catch indicates ongoing recruitment overfishing. Integrating long-term time series with ecosystem modelling provides a robust scientific basis for adaptive fisheries management and supports precautionary measures, including revision of the minimum conservation reference size, to improve the resilience of red mullet populations under future environmental and fishing pressures.

Hydrodynamics, Sensing, and Control in Schooling Fish
👤 Kanso, Eva 📅 2026-10-12
Fish schools are among the most spectacular examples of collective behavior in nature. Individual fish coordinate their motion with neighbors primarily through vision and flow sensing. Each fish generates flow disturbances that can influence the motion and energetics of nearby swimmers. Yet fundamental questions remain unresolved: To what extent can schooling be explained by passive hydrodynamic interactions, and where are individual-level sensing and control required to maintain group cohesion? This review examines collective swimming through the lens of fluid mechanics. By synthesizing experimental, theoretical, and computational studies spanning pairs of fish to large schools, I discuss how hydrodynamic interactions affect coordination, stability, and energetic cost. Flow interactions can yield robust pairwise coordination and locomotor benefits, but they can also drive collective instabilities and unequal benefit distributions in larger groups. These findings point to a fundamental interplay between hydrodynamics and sensory–motor feedback in the formation and maintenance of fish schools. More broadly, they suggest that hydrodynamics shapes collective swimming not only through energetic costs and benefits but also through the physical constraints it imposes on coordination, stability, and information flow within groups.

👤 Cala-Delgado, Daniel Leonardo; Duarte-Rodriguez, Luz Zoraya;... 📅 2026-10-13
Accidental petroleum contamination can affect fish health and food safety in aquaculture, yet evidence from commercial production systems remains limited. This study evaluated the effects of an accidental diesel spill on a rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) farm operating under a continuous flow-through system in Santander, Colombia. Water and fish muscle were analyzed for petroleum hydrocarbons, and exposed fish were examined by histopathology and immunohistochemistry. Following the rollover of a diesel tanker truck, the producer reported a diesel-like odor, an oily surface film, and reduced feed intake, although no spill-related mortality was observed and water quality remained within acceptable ranges for trout culture. These findings indicate that accidental diesel exposure in a commercial flow-through rainbow trout farm can cause detectable PAH contamination and multisystemic tissue injury without acute mortality. Integrated chemical and histopathological monitoring is therefore advisable for aquaculture toxicology and food safety surveillance.

Abstract Lake Titicaca is a continental centre for ichthyological biodiversity, with over 90% of native species being endemic. However, these species face significant threats from invasive species, particularly from invasive salmonids and invasive Argentine pejerrey. While ecologists have known that these invasive species have had deleterious effects on the ichthyofauna of Lake Titicaca for decades, whether this is a consequence of competitive exclusion or direct predation remains unknown. We tested this using morphological data, testing whether the closeness of native species to invasives in morphospace was predictive of their current conservation status, and whether smaller species were likelier to be at risk than larger species. It could be used by conservationists in other regions to triage conservation efforts towards more vulnerable species and to predict which species are likelier to be harmed when a new invasive species is identified.

This review provides a theoretical basis for assessing MNPs health risks and developing practical management strategies for sustainable aquaculture.
