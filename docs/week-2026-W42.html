鱼类免疫、遗传与生态适应研究新进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录4篇渔业相关论文，全部发表于 *Journal of Fish Biology*，研究方向涵盖分子生态学方法、物种杂交与保护遗传学、气候变化下的鱼类分布变动以及水产养殖免疫机制。整体来看，本周研究呈现出两个鲜明特征：一是方法学导向的研究（如DNA保存方案优化）为后续生态-遗传数据整合提供技术支撑；二是应用导向的研究（如杂交监测、北极鳕鱼分布扩展、细菌感染免疫应答）直接回应保护管理与养殖生产的现实需求。值得注意的是，多篇论文均涉及人为活动（如气候变暖、栖息地改造）对鱼类种群的影响，反映出当前渔业科学对全球变化背景下种群响应机制的高度关注。

高影响力期刊亮点

本周暂无发表于 Nature、Science、PNAS、Nature Climate Change、Nature Ecology & Evolution、Nature Sustainability、Nature Communications、Science Advances 等高影响力期刊的渔业相关论文。

创新性研究

  1. Active nuclease inhibition over passive freezing secures the genomic archive of fish eye lenses（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新点在于系统比较了主动核酸酶抑制与被动冷冻两种策略对鱼眼晶状体基因组DNA保存效果的影响，并首次为这一兼具生态与遗传信息价值的组织提出了标准化保存方案。鱼眼晶状体可提供个体生活史的时间序列档案，但死后核酸酶降解长期制约着生态与遗传数据的整合。该方法的建立有望推动鱼类个体水平“生态-基因组”联合研究在野外和博物馆样本中的广泛应用。

  1. Widespread hybridization in sympatric populations of allis shad *Alosa alosa* and twaite shad *Alosa fallax*: A transnational perspective for conservation and management（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究以跨国尺度揭示了同域分布的西鲱（allis shad）与芬塔西鲱（twaite shad）之间存在广泛的杂交现象，创新性地将杂交格局与人为环境改造联系起来。研究结果对两种鲱鱼的保护单元界定和渔业管理策略提出了直接挑战，提示在制定保护措施时需充分考虑杂交对遗传完整性的潜在侵蚀。

  1. Seasonal presence, movements and behaviour of Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) in Isfjorden, Svalbard（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究聚焦北极化（Atlantification）背景下大西洋鳕鱼向斯瓦尔巴群岛峡湾的北向扩张，通过监测其季节性出现、移动和行为模式，提供了 boreal 物种入侵北极生态系统的直接行为学证据。其创新之处在于将气候变化驱动的海洋环境变动与鱼类个体行为响应相衔接，为预测北极渔业资源重组提供了关键基线数据。

  1. Transcriptomic analysis reveals the immune response mechanisms of *Acrossocheilus fasciatus* against *Citrobacter freundii* infection（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究结合组织学观察与RNA-seq技术，系统解析了光唇鱼（*Acrossocheilus fasciatus*）肠道和脾脏对弗氏柠檬酸杆菌感染的免疫应答机制。其创新点在于首次为该经济鱼种提供了细菌感染的转录组水平免疫图谱，为水产养殖中该病的防控和抗病育种提供了候选基因资源。

A Fish Journey through Data: Long-Term Monitoring and Red Mullet Stock Status
👤 Georgieva, Yoana; Klayn, Stefania 📅 2026-10-12
Red mullet ( Mullus barbatus ) is one of the most valuable demersal fish species in the Black Sea, supporting important commercial fisheries while playing a key ecological role in benthic food webs. Due to its short life span (4-6 years), rapid growth, and sensitivity to environmental changes and fishing pressure, the species serves as an effective indicator of the states of the coastal ecosystems. Hence, biological long-term monitoring became essential for evaluating population dynamics and supporting sustainable fisheries management. This study implement fisheries-dependent long term monitoring data (2015–2025) to evaluate changes in the status of red mullet stock along the Bulgarian Black Sea coast. In the Black Sea, length-based indicators are crucial since the age determination remains affected by inconsistencies in otolith interpretation among laboratories, whereas length measurements are standardized, routinely collected, and highly reproducible. Annual analyses included total length, body weight, condition factor, and cumulative length-frequency distributions. These analyses were complemented by long-term ecosystem projections from an Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) model to evaluate future biomass and catch trajectories under unchanged fishing pressure (F status quo). Mean total length (10.9–12.6 cm) and body weight (15.5–23.7 g) showed clear inter annual variability, while condition factor remained consistently stable, indicating good individual condition throughout the study period. Length-frequency distributions revealed generally stable population size structure but also marked year-to-year differences, with 2017 and 2023 characterized by a greater proportion of small individuals and 2024 by larger fish. Approximately 35% of the catch consisted of fish below the estimated size at first maturity (11.2 cm TL), indicating persistent harvesting of immature individuals despite the relative stability of the stock structure. Ecosystem model projections further suggest that, if current fishing mortality is maintained, both biomass and catches may decline by approximately 13% by 2050. The red mullet stock in the Black Sea remains overexploited (GFCM 2024). These results demonstrate the importance of long-term ecological monitoring for detecting gradual changes in exploited populations that may not be detected by the short-term data. Although the overall length structure remained relatively stable, the persistent occurrence of immature individuals in the catch indicates ongoing recruitment overfishing. Integrating long-term time series with ecosystem modelling provides a robust scientific basis for adaptive fisheries management and supports precautionary measures, including revision of the minimum conservation reference size, to improve the resilience of red mullet populations under future environmental and fishing pressures.

Hydrodynamics, Sensing, and Control in Schooling Fish
👤 Kanso, Eva 📅 2026-10-12
Fish schools are among the most spectacular examples of collective behavior in nature. Individual fish coordinate their motion with neighbors primarily through vision and flow sensing. Each fish generates flow disturbances that can influence the motion and energetics of nearby swimmers. Yet fundamental questions remain unresolved: To what extent can schooling be explained by passive hydrodynamic interactions, and where are individual-level sensing and control required to maintain group cohesion? This review examines collective swimming through the lens of fluid mechanics. By synthesizing experimental, theoretical, and computational studies spanning pairs of fish to large schools, I discuss how hydrodynamic interactions affect coordination, stability, and energetic cost. Flow interactions can yield robust pairwise coordination and locomotor benefits, but they can also drive collective instabilities and unequal benefit distributions in larger groups. These findings point to a fundamental interplay between hydrodynamics and sensory–motor feedback in the formation and maintenance of fish schools. More broadly, they suggest that hydrodynamics shapes collective swimming not only through energetic costs and benefits but also through the physical constraints it imposes on coordination, stability, and information flow within groups.

From Sequence to Structure: AlphaFold and the AI Revolution in Aquaculture Drug Discovery and Vaccine Design
👤 Üstüner, Enes; Öz, Mustafa 📅 2026-10-12
ABSTRACT Global aquaculture faces an escalating disease and antimicrobial resistance (AMR) crisis, exacerbated by climate‐driven physiochemical shifts and intensive farming practices. The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and structural biology, spearheaded by AlphaFold, represents a paradigm shift in aquatic pharmacology, transitioning the field from empirical trial‐and‐error to rational in silico design. This review critically evaluates the application of AI‐driven tools in aquaculture drug discovery and vaccine development. We examine the evolution of AlphaFold in predicting complex host–pathogen interactions and facilitating de novo drug design. Concurrently, we highlight critical limitations stemming from the reliance on multiple sequence alignment (MSA) depth rather than mere PDB taxonomic composition, the unreliability of direct molecular docking due to apo conformations, and the dynamic nature of psychrophilic adaptations in aquatic environments. Furthermore, we assess next‐generation structural vaccinology, including reverse vaccinology and pre‐fusion antigen stabilization, alongside the integration of nanomaterial‐based delivery systems to overcome aquatic pharmacokinetic barriers. The synergy of AI with CRISPR/Cas technologies and multimodal large language models (MM‐LLMs) for disease management is also explored. Despite unprecedented computational capabilities, significant bottlenecks remain, including a severe lack of species‐specific omics data, the indispensability of in vivo wet‐lab validation, and lagging regulatory frameworks. Ultimately, realizing the potential of precision aquatic medicine requires developing aquaculture‐specific databases, addressing the ecological risks of open‐water deployment, and democratizing these technologies for small‐scale farmers to ensure sustainable, equitable, and resilient global aquaculture.

Consumer Substitution Behavior and Price Elasticity of Demand for Fish Products in Coastal Nigeria: Empirical Evidence of Bayelsa State
👤 Achor, Prince Christopher 📅 2026-10-12
This study investigated the responses of fish consumption patterns of households in Bayelsa State, Nigeria to price volatility. The sampling technique used was mixed cross sectional method, where four Local Government Areas (LGAs) were purposively selected due to their nearness to fish activities and high fish consumption pattern. The respondents were selected by systematic random sampling. The number of questionnaires distributed was 400 of which 308 were recovered and the analyses were based on the 308 responded questionnaires. A structured questionnaire (Effect of Price Change on Fish Consumption Patterns (EPCFCP)). Cronbach's Alpha was used to determine the reliability of the instrument and was found to have an acceptable internal consistency. Data were statistically analyzed with descriptive statistics, Spearman's Rank Correlation and Ordinal Logistic Regression. The results indicated that price changes of fish were statistically significant and affected household fish consumption trend leading to a decrease in fish consumption and an increase in substitution with other protein sources. The Spearman's Rank Correlation indicated that there was a moderate positive and statistically significant correlation between changes in fish prices and fish consumption behaviour of household members (r = 0.547, p < 0.05). But there was very little and statistically insignificant negative relationship between responsiveness to fish price changes and the household income (r = −0.099, p = 0.083). The results of the Ordinal Logistic Regression also showed that the overall model was statistically significant and that the price change of the fish, consumption pattern, household income and gender were significantly influencing fish consumption behaviour. Price changes in fish proved to be best predictors. The analysis shows that the prices of fish have a significant effect on household consumption decisions. It suggests the need for investment in aquaculture, subsidy for fish producers and availability of cheap and affordable credit, development of adequate storage and distribution system, and measures to support the purchasing power of the fish household to improve food security and nutritional status.

Strengthening Chitin and Chitosan Downstreaming to Enhance Indonesia Fisheries Export Competitiveness
👤 Pujiastuti, Agustina 📅 2026-10-12
Indonesia has substantial potential to develop a downstream chitin and chitosan industry because shrimp-processing activities generate shell by-products that can be converted into higher-value biomaterials. However, the utilization of these by-products for industrial chitin and chitosan remains constrained by fragmented raw-material supply, limited commercialization of research and technology, inadequate standardization and quality assurance, limited industrial capacity, and weak institutional coordination. This study aims to assess the current status of downstream chitin and chitosan development in Indonesia, identify the structural factors constraining value-chain development, analyze policy gaps, and formulate a phased policy roadmap. A qualitative descriptive approach was applied using secondary data from regulations, policy documents, official statistics, scientific publications, and institutional reports. The analysis consisted of current-status assessment, root-cause analysis, problem prioritization, policy-gap analysis, policy-alternative assessment, and roadmap formulation. The findings indicate that the principal constraint is not the availability of shrimp-shell resources but the weak integration of the downstream value chain. A four-phase roadmap is proposed covering governance and value-chain integration, industrial development and technology commercialization, international competitiveness, and innovation and sustainability. The framework provides a policy basis for increasing value added and strengthening fisheries export competitiveness through circular utilization of fisheries by-products.performance

Hydrodynamic Analysis of Aquaculture Net Cages Under Coupled Netting-Fish-Sediment Interactions
👤 Liu, Shuang; Cao, Zuwei; Lyu, Chao 📅 2026-10-12
To address the limitations of existing studies that neglect sediment feedback and thus underestimate the complexity of the flow field, this study develops a three-dimensional hydrodynamic model for aquaculture cages that couples a porous-media representation of netting with a two-way discrete-phase model for sediment transport. Unlike traditional single-factor simulations, this work systematically evaluates three interaction scenarios: netting-only, netting-fish coupling, and netting-fish-sediment multi-factor coupling, thereby revealing key nonlinear hydrodynamic mechanisms that have not been reported before. The results demonstrate that sediment particles induce a distinct momentum-feedback-driven “local re-acceleration zone” downstream of the cage, increasing turbulent kinetic energy by 13.2% and reducing wake velocity attenuation by 6-8%. These findings confirm that sediment–fish–netting interactions significantly modify wake shear structures and must be accounted for to ensure accurate flow prediction. This study provides new mechanistic insights into multi-factor hydrodynamics and a refined modeling framework to optimize cage design, enhance water exchange, and support sediment management in intensive mariculture.

Mud Crab Aquaculture: Biological Foundations, Production Bottlenecks and Sustainability Pathways in Scylla Farming
👤 Patil, Atul R.; Chadha, N. K.; Jain, Amita; Prakash, Patekar... 📅 2026-10-12
Mud crabs of the genus Scylla support high-value fisheries and aquaculture across the tropical Indo-Pacific, yet the sector remains technologically uneven and closely coupled to wild resources. This critical narrative review evaluates the biological and production evidence underpinning Scylla aquaculture, with emphasis on species identity, broodstock and hatchery management, larviculture, nursery and grow-out systems, nutrition, environmental physiology, health and biosecurity, domestication, genetics, economics, and sustainability. Literature published from 1998 to 5 August 2026 was identified through agriculture, biomedical, multidisciplinary, and open scholarly indexes, supplemented by citation searching and authoritative institutional sources. Evidence was appraised for species verification, life stage, experimental design, replication, husbandry control, outcome validity, field relevance, and transferability. The literature shows that the principal constraint is not a single husbandry variable but a weakly integrated production chain. Hatchery survival remains inconsistent; nurseries are constrained by cannibalism and size heterogeneity; grow-out performance depends strongly on stocking design, shelter, feed quality, environmental stability, and disease control; and many farming systems still depend on wild broodstock, crablets, or subadult crabs. Species-level differences further limit the validity of transferring results among S. serrata, S. olivacea, S. paramamosain, and S. tranquebarica. Nutrition research demonstrates clear stage- and species-specific responses, but commercially validated formulated feeds remain limited. Disease reports increasingly implicate viral, bacterial, parasitic, and shell-associated syndromes, reinforcing the need for preventive biosecurity rather than reactive treatment. Domestication and genomic tools are advancing, particularly for S. paramamosain and S. serrata, but multi-generation selection, genetic management, welfare assessment, and independent economic validation remain sparse.
