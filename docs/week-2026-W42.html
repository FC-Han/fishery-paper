渔业研究多元热点：从养殖技术到生态保护

本周研究概览

本周共收录4篇渔业相关论文，全部发表于 *Journal of Fish Biology*，研究方向涵盖分子生态学方法、物种杂交与保护遗传学、气候变化下的鱼类分布变动以及水产养殖免疫机制。整体来看，本周研究呈现出两个鲜明特征：一是方法学导向的研究（如DNA保存方案优化）为后续生态-遗传数据整合提供技术支撑；二是应用导向的研究（如杂交监测、北极鳕鱼分布扩展、细菌感染免疫应答）直接回应保护管理与养殖生产的现实需求。值得注意的是，多篇论文均涉及人为活动（如气候变暖、栖息地改造）对鱼类种群的影响，反映出当前渔业科学对全球变化背景下种群响应机制的高度关注。

创新性研究

  1. Active nuclease inhibition over passive freezing secures the genomic archive of fish eye lenses（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新点在于系统比较了主动核酸酶抑制与被动冷冻两种策略对鱼眼晶状体基因组DNA保存效果的影响，并首次为这一兼具生态与遗传信息价值的组织提出了标准化保存方案。鱼眼晶状体可提供个体生活史的时间序列档案，但死后核酸酶降解长期制约着生态与遗传数据的整合。该方法的建立有望推动鱼类个体水平“生态-基因组”联合研究在野外和博物馆样本中的广泛应用。

  1. Widespread hybridization in sympatric populations of allis shad *Alosa alosa* and twaite shad *Alosa fallax*: A transnational perspective for conservation and management（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究以跨国尺度揭示了同域分布的西鲱（allis shad）与芬塔西鲱（twaite shad）之间存在广泛的杂交现象，创新性地将杂交格局与人为环境改造联系起来。研究结果对两种鲱鱼的保护单元界定和渔业管理策略提出了直接挑战，提示在制定保护措施时需充分考虑杂交对遗传完整性的潜在侵蚀。

  1. Seasonal presence, movements and behaviour of Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) in Isfjorden, Svalbard（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究聚焦北极化（Atlantification）背景下大西洋鳕鱼向斯瓦尔巴群岛峡湾的北向扩张，通过监测其季节性出现、移动和行为模式，提供了 boreal 物种入侵北极生态系统的直接行为学证据。其创新之处在于将气候变化驱动的海洋环境变动与鱼类个体行为响应相衔接，为预测北极渔业资源重组提供了关键基线数据。

  1. Transcriptomic analysis reveals the immune response mechanisms of *Acrossocheilus fasciatus* against *Citrobacter freundii* infection（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究结合组织学观察与RNA-seq技术，系统解析了光唇鱼（*Acrossocheilus fasciatus*）肠道和脾脏对弗氏柠檬酸杆菌感染的免疫应答机制。其创新点在于首次为该经济鱼种提供了细菌感染的转录组水平免疫图谱，为水产养殖中该病的防控和抗病育种提供了候选基因资源。

Nanotechnology Strategies to Mitigate Antimicrobial Resistance in Fish Aquaculture Systems
👤 Marques Chagas, Paulo Augusto; Campanharo, Sarah Chagas; Pin... 📅 2026-10-17
Abstract Aquaculture plays an increasingly important role in global food production, but intensive freshwater fish aquaculture still faces major challenges related to infectious diseases and the use of antimicrobials. Antimicrobials are commonly administered orally through medicated feed, a practice often compromised by drug leaching before ingestion, heterogeneous feed intake, and temperature- and species-dependent pharmacokinetic variability. These factors can generate heterogeneous antimicrobial exposure in fish, ranging from subtherapeutic concentrations to compensatory overdosing practices while simultaneously increasing the entry and persistence of antimicrobial residues in water and sediments, thereby creating selective pressure that favors the emergence and maintenance of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquatic microbial communities. This integrative review focuses primarily on intensive freshwater fish aquaculture systems and examines how antimicrobial use contributes to AMR through interconnected pharmacological, microbiological, and environmental pathways. The review discusses how water temperature, fish physiology, feeding behavior, and dosing inconsistencies influence antimicrobial absorption, disposition, therapeutic performance, residue persistence, and resistance selection. Intrinsic, acquired, and adaptive resistance mechanisms are also discussed with emphasis on the role of environmental contamination in maintaining resistance reservoirs. Within this framework, the review analyzes nanoenabled strategies designed to address these bottlenecks, including drug delivery systems that improve control over drug administration, gastrointestinal stability, bioavailability, and release, as well as nanotechnologies for residue removal and environmental monitoring. By integrating host- and environment-oriented perspectives, this Review highlights how nanotechnology may help reduce antimicrobial dependence, limit selective pressure, and support more sustainable fish aquaculture within a One Health framework.

Hybrid Wind–Solar-Powered Smart Fish Feeding For Sustainable Aquaculture
👤 M Habib Aulia; Dewi, Tresna; Bow, Yohandri 📅 2026-10-18
This research evaluated the performance of a hybrid photovoltaic (PV) and vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) energy system to power a smart fish feeding system and support sustainable aquaculture. The study used a quantitative experimental approach based on a Design and Development (D&D) methodology, collecting data over a 20-day period from April 28 to May 17, 2026, between 10:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m. Western Indonesian Time (WIB) in Lubuk Bakung, Palembang, South Sumatra, Indonesia. The measured parameters included solar irradiance, wind speed, PV power, wind turbine power, hybrid system input power, output power, battery performance, and operational reliability. The results showed that an average solar irradiance of 611.26 W/m² produced an average PV power of 195.60 W, while an average wind speed of 2.64 m/s produced an average wind turbine power of 65.78 W. The hybrid system produced an average input power of 256.52 W, with an average system efficiency of 2.76%. The battery had a usable energy capacity of 192 Wh and provided approximately 6.40 hours of theoretical operational autonomy. During the experimental period, all 40 scheduled feeding operations were successfully completed, resulting in a 100% operational reliability rate. These findings show that integrating PV and VAWT technologies can provide an adequate, reliable standalone renewable energy supply for automatic fish feeding systems and support energy-efficient, sustainable aquaculture operations. 👤 Kartu, S. C.; Lende, S. R.; Tandel, D. C.; Vala, F. D.; Pith... 📅 2026-10-17
The present study assessed fish consumption patterns, food preferences, and socioeconomic characteristics among school-going children in tribal areas of Tapi District, Gujarat. A total of 100 children aged 8–13 years from two schools in Bavali and Pipalkuwa villages of Ukai Taluka were surveyed using a structured interview schedule and Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) during September 2026. The study population comprised 52% boys and 48% girls. Agriculture was the predominant occupation among parents, accounting for 80% of fathers and 70% of mothers, while fishing was reported as the occupation of 10% of fathers. The findings indicate that fish constituted an important component of non-vegetarian food choices among the surveyed children despite the predominance of agricultural livelihoods and relatively low household income. One Rule to Rule them All: Hybrid Harvest Control Rules Improve Bio-Economic Outcomes in an MSE Framework
👤 Zahner, Joshua A; Goethel, Daniel R.; Cunningham, Curry Jame... 📅 2026-10-17
Ramped threshold harvest control rules (HCRs), which reduce fishing pressure as a stock declines, are generally resilient to environmental stochasticity, but can struggle to provide good management advice when confronted with abrupt shifts in productivity. For instance, long-lived species with spasmodic recruitment present management dilemmas due to long periods of low recruitment followed by short bursts of extreme productivity, potentially causing market saturation and poor economic conditions. Using management strategy evaluation, we evaluate the performance of a “hybrid” HCR that couples ramped threshold HCRs with stability constraints to limit quota variability and harvest caps to mitigate excessive quota increases, with the goal of identifying whether multiple simple modifications can improve HCR performance for spasmodic species. For long-lived species like Alaskan sablefish, we demonstrate that the hybrid HCR can improve economic value, stabilize catches, and increase population size and resilience compared to a ramped threshold HCR or application of either constraint in isolation. Thus, the hybrid HCR exhibits potential to improve population resilience and harvest opportunity for species with environmentally-driven dynamic recruitment.

Trans‐Basin Climate Events Modify Distribution and Catchability of Large and Small Size Classes of Bigeye Tuna ( Thunnus obesus ) in the Indian Ocean
👤 Xu, Zhenqi; Liu, Yang 📅 2026-10-18
ABSTRACT The sustainability of global tuna fisheries exhibits regional divergence, with some stocks showing significant recovery while Indian Ocean bigeye tuna ( Thunnus obesus ) remains overfished. Particularly against the backdrop of intensifying global climate change, both large and small size classes of bigeye tuna are experiencing shifts in distribution and catch rate patterns influenced by trans‐basin climate events. This study focuses on the effects of trans‐basin climate change on the resource dynamics of bigeye tuna in the Indian Ocean, comprehensively considering the differing responses of large and small size classes to environmental changes. We systematically examined the causal relationship between trans‐basin climate events and tuna resource fluctuations by integrating fishery and climate data using the Convergent Cross‐Mapping (CCM) method. Concurrently, the Climate Events Synergy Model (CESM) was employed to analyze the interactions between these climate factors and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) in shaping tuna habitat suitability. Results indicate that different trans‐basin climate events exert heterogeneous impacts on large and small size classes of bigeye tuna by altering water temperature, nutrient transport, and circulation patterns, and these events affect the resource dynamics in conjunction with the IOD. This study provides scientific evidence for elucidating the mechanisms underlying tuna resource fluctuations under multi‐scale climate drivers in the Indian Ocean, holding significant implications for formulating climate‐specific fisheries management strategies and addressing climate change challenges.

Design and Thermal Performance Analysis of a Multilayer Coolbox with Polyurethane as the Primary Insulation for Portable Fish-Hold Applications
👤 Kamal, Mustopa 📅 2026-10-17
Post-harvest handling of fresh fish by small-scale fishermen is highly dependent on effective temperature control to minimize quality deterioration during storage and transportation. This study aimed to design, fabricate, and evaluate the thermal performance of a 70-L portable coolbox using rigid polyurethane foam as the primary thermal insulation material. The coolbox was constructed using a 3-cm-thick multilayer composite wall consisting of fiberglass (1.0 cm), meranti plywood (0.5 cm), aluminum foil (0.5 cm), and rigid polyurethane foam (1.0 cm). A rubber sealing strip and clamp locks were incorporated to reduce heat infiltration through the lid interface. Thermal performance was evaluated experimentally over 168 h under two loading conditions: 30 kg of block ice and a combination of 30 kg of block ice and 20 kg of fresh fish. A one-dimensional steady-state heat conduction model was also applied to estimate the thermal resistance and equivalent thermal conductivity of the composite wall. The results yielded a total thermal resistance of 0.4291 K/W and an equivalent thermal conductivity of 0.0606 W/(m·K). Polyurethane contributed approximately 84.15% of the total thermal resistance, indicating that it was the dominant insulating layer in the composite structure. During the first 72 h, the internal temperature remained within approximately 0–4 °C under both loading conditions. After 72 h, the internal temperature gradually increased as the ice load decreased, reaching 18–19 °C by the seventh day. The total material cost for fabricating one prototype was IDR 1,445,000. These findings indicate that the multilayer polyurethane-based coolbox can provide effective passive thermal insulation for short-term chilled fish storage in small-scale fishing operations.

Fish symbolism in the Indus Civilisation: an ideographic reading of the fish sign as the soulof the deceased.
👤 Ramasami, Jeyakumar 📅 2026-10-18
After the jar sign, the fish sign is the most frequently attested element in the known corpus of Indus inscriptions; together with its graphic variants, it accounts for an estimated 10 percent of all recorded signs. Despite this prominence, no consensus exists on its meaning, and the widely repeated reading of the sign as 'star' is contested. (1) It leaves unexplained why such a complex pictogram would render such a common and abstract idea. This article develops an alternative ideographic hypothesis: that the fish sign denotes the soul of a deceased person, and more specifically the ancestral soul (Sanskrit piṭṛ, 'forefather'). The argument draws on three independent strands of evidence: Indus seal iconography; comparative funerary symbolism in Mesopotamia, Egypt, Syria, and the Greek world; and the fish iconography of the Mesolithic–Neolithic site of Lepenski Vir in the Danube Gorges. This paper offers a proposed reading of an Indus seal depicting a ritual fish-pit, using it as a case study. It argues that a funerary reading explains the sign's high frequency and its association with aquatic and ancestral imagery more economically than a stellar reading.

👤 Ma, Xiaoli; Su, Baofeng; Ye, Anxin; Wan, Renyu; Zhao, Keyan;... 📅 2026-10-18
Abstract Largemouth bass ( Micropterus salmoides ) is a globally significant freshwater aquaculture species known for its rapid growth, high‐quality flesh and excellent nutritional value. Given the increasing incidence of hypoxia in its aquaculture setting, understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying gill adaptation to hypoxic stress is critical for improving resilience in this species. To achieve this, we employed full‐length transcript sequencing using PacBio Iso‐Seq and high‐throughput Illumina RNA‐Seq to construct a comprehensive transcriptome profile. Histological analyses revealed apparent structural alterations in gill tissues under hypoxic stress (dissolved oxygen: 2.0 ± 0.5 mg/L, duration: 96 h), including curved and swollen gill filaments, apoptosis and epithelial cell vacuolation. Comparative transcriptomic analysis revealed pronounced shifts in the expression of genes involved in energy metabolism, angiogenesis, immune responses, cell proliferation and apoptosis. Notably, the down‐regulation of genes associated with cell cycle and proliferation suggests that M. salmoides may conserve energy by suppressing cell division under hypoxic conditions. In contrast, the increased expression of apoptosis‐related genes, including casp7 , indicates that apoptosis may play a protective role in maintain tissue homeostasis under hypoxia. Furthermore, the suppression of chemokine‐mediated immune pathways implies that hypoxia redirects energy toward essential metabolic processes at the cost of local immune responses. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses identified TNF‐α and chemokine‐mediated signalling pathways as key pathways involved in tissue homeostasis and adaptive responses to hypoxic stress. These findings enhance our understanding of the mechanisms underlying hypoxia adaptation and provide valuable genetic resources for breeding strategies aimed at enhancing hypoxia tolerance in M. salmoides .

Enhancing the hardness of low-carbon steel by pack carburizing using shellfish shell powder as a calcium carbonate activator
👤 Adri, Junil; Parmanoan, Durain; Refdinal, Refdinal; Jasman, ... 📅 2026-10-17
This study investigates the potential of shellfish shell powder as a natural source of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) for improving the pack carburizing of AISI 1020 low-carbon steel. AISI 1020 Low-carbon steel is commonly used in engineering applications because of its favorable mechanical properties and machinability; however, its relatively low surface hardness may limit its wear resistance. Carburization was performed at 950 °C for 6 h using three conditions: an untreated control, charcoal only, and a mixture of charcoal shellfish shell powder. Surface characterization was conducted using FE-SEM/EDS, while hardness was evaluated using the Brinell Hardness test. The charcoal-shellfish shell powder specimen achieved the highest average hardness (481.44 BHN), which was significantly higher than those of the charcoal specimen (387.40 BHN) and the control specimen (216.77 BHN) (p < 0.001). FE-SEM observations showed that the charcoal–shellfish shell powder specimen exhibited distinct surface morphology, while EDS analysis revealed a higher surface carbon concentration and the presence of calcium, indicating a more effective carburizing environment than the charcoal-only treatment. These findings indicate that the shellfish shell powder effectively enhanced hardness, resulting in the formation of a carbon-enriched surface. The novelty of this study lies in the use of shellfish shell powder derived from shell waste as an alternative natural activator, and in the quantitative validation its effectiveness through statistical analysis, demonstrating its potential as a cost-effective and environmentally sustainable substitute for conventional carburizing activators.
