Otolith Morphological Characteristics of Major Schizothoracinae Fish Species in Tibet and Genus-Level Morphological Discrimination（Animals）

该研究的创新点在于将耳石形态学方法系统应用于青藏高原裂腹鱼亚科这一形态高度趋同的类群，尝试解决传统外部形态难以区分近缘属的鉴定难题。裂腹鱼亚科物种多样性丰富但外部形态极为相似，长期以来给分类学和渔业资源调查带来困扰，而耳石作为硬组织具有种属特异性形态，为判别提供了新途径。研究聚焦属级判别而非种级，这一策略在近缘种外部形态趋同、但属间耳石特征可能更稳定的前提下具有合理性，对高原鱼类资源监测和分类实践有潜在应用价值。不过，属级判别能否进一步下推至种级、以及样本量与地理覆盖范围是否充分，仍有待后续研究检验。

Nutritional Profiling of Dried Puntius sophore (Hamilton, 1822), a Principal Raw Material for the Traditional Fermented Fish Product Ngari in Manipur, India
👤 Bipinchandra, Urikhimbam; Singh, Pebam Bidyananda; Devi, Tho... 📅 2026-09-19
The small indigenous fish species Puntius sophore is an important raw material used in the preparation of Ngari, a traditional fermented fish product of Manipur, India. Fresh adult P. sophore were collected from Loktak Lake, Manipur, during August to September 2025 and processed into dried fish for nutritional evaluation. The fish were cleaned, washed, sun-dried, pulverised and stored before laboratory analysis. This study analysed the proximate composition, fatty acid profile and amino acid composition of dried P. sophore to generate baseline information relevant to its use in traditional fermentation. Analyses were performed in triplicate, and the results were expressed as mean±standard deviation. The dried sample had a low moisture content of 3.34±0.12%, indicating effective dehydration. Crude protein and crude fat contents were 40.03±0.24% and 30.15±0.008%, respectively, while ash and carbohydrate contents were 14.42±0.02% and 12.06±0.024%, respectively. Fatty acid analysis showed saturated fatty acids at 13.25±0.04%, monounsaturated fatty acids at 12.66±0.03% and polyunsaturated fatty acids at 4.24±0.016%, with no trans fat detected. Amino acid profiling confirmed the presence of both essential and non-essential amino acids. Glycine (3.14±0.01%), glutamic acid (3.06±0.01%), proline (2.39±0.008%), leucine (2.72±0.03%) and phenylalanine (1.88±0.01%) were the major constituents recorded, whereas histidine and lysine were not detected in the sample. Overall, the findings indicate that dried P. sophore is a protein- and lipid-rich fish material with appreciable ash content and a diverse amino acid profile. The study provides preliminary nutritional data that may support quality assessment of dried fish used for Ngari production and related traditional fermentation systems in Manipur.

Determination of Total Mercury in Selected Seafood Samples Using Cold Vapor Atomic Absorption Spectrometry (CVAAS)
👤 Omema Khalid Atia; Safa Abd Alwahhab Albakkoush 📅 2026-09-19
Mercury (Hg) is a persistent environmental contaminant that can accumulate in aquatic organisms and enter the human diet through seafood consumption. Monitoring total mercury concentrations in seafood is therefore important for food-safety assessment and environmental surveillance. This study aimed to determine and compare total mercury concentrations in selected seafood samples, including squid, prawn, fresh fish (mackerel), and canned fish, using cold vapor atomic absorption spectrometry (CVAAS) following microwave-assisted acid digestion. The analytical procedure was also assessed in terms of calibration linearity, recovery, and repeatability. Homogenized seafood samples (approximately 0.20 g) were digested with concentrated nitric acid using a closed-vessel microwave digestion system. Mercury was measured using a NIC RA-3000 Mercury Analyzer, with the standard addition method used to minimize matrix effects. The calibration curves demonstrated good linearity, with coefficients of determination (R²) ranging from 0.9883 to 0.9956. The measured total mercury concentrations were 1.74 ppb in squid, 1.22 ppb in prawn, 5.69 ppb in fresh fish, and 6.92 ppb in canned fish. Recovery values ranged from 65% to 102%, while repeatability assessment for canned fish produced an RSD of 5.57%. Total mercury concentrations varied among the investigated seafood samples, with the highest measured value in canned fish and the lowest in prawn. However, the limited sampling design prevents statistical generalization, and the higher value in canned fish cannot be attributed to canning. Further studies should include larger representative samples, independent biological replicates, comprehensive analytical validation, and mercury speciation to better evaluate contamination and potential dietary exposure.

Defining and finding the profit for seaweed secondary extensive aquaculture
👤 Froehlich, Halley E.; Campbell, Rose S.; Miller, Kathy Ann; ... 📅 2026-09-18
Seaweed aquaculture is expanding globally, yet small-scale producers, such as those emerging in the United States, face challenges in achieving economic viability. We introduce and formalize the concept of secondary extensive aquaculture (SEA): the non-target cultivation, harvest, and use of extractive species (e.g., seaweeds) that naturally recruit and grow on existing aquaculture infrastructure designed for a different primary species (e.g., oysters). To explore the economic viability of SEA, we employed a scenario-based bioeconomic modeling approach, coupling logistic seaweed growth and sustainable harvest with a simple profit function. The model was parameterized with literature-derived ranges for seaweed growth and carrying capacity, as well as data from a 2025 pilot harvest on a 65 ha oyster farm in California. Using simulations, we evaluated key ecological and economic combinations under which seaweed SEA may offset the estimated 5-10% production losses associated with typical, unharvested biofouling. We showed that profitability increased with farm size, growing season length, and intrinsic growth rate ( r ) of seaweed, with viable scenarios typically requiring &gt; 50-100 ha and &gt; 60 growing days under conservative market assumptions ($400 t -1 wet weight). Intrinsic growth rate exerted a stronger influence on profitability than carrying capacity ( K ), indicating that faster-growing seaweed species (e.g., Ulva spp., Gracilaria spp., and Pyropia spp.) are more suitable candidates for SEA. In fact, modal ecological conditions on a 65 ha farm may offset biofouling costs. Labor intensity, however, emerged as a major bottleneck, with pilot-informed labor estimates (~140-200 hrs. t -1 including processing) far exceeding the ca. 10 hrs. t -1 needed to breakeven at minimum wage, but could be achieved with realistic improved efficiency. Although our pilot SEA yields fell near the lower bound of global seaweed aquaculture production, they remain within plausible commercial ranges. Overall, SEA represents a promising complementary strategy for diversifying revenue streams and enhancing circularity in aquaculture systems, but its realization will depend on sufficient scale, favorable biological productivity, labor efficiency improvements, and supportive policy frameworks.

A computational intelligence framework for multimodal oceanic data analysis and predictive modeling in sustainable marine resource management
👤 Liu, Xuan 📅 2026-09-18
Introduction The rapid expansion of ocean based economic activities and increasing anthropogenic pressures have made sustainable marine resource management a critical challenge in oceanic computing. Effective management of ocean ecosystems necessitates intelligent systems capable of integrating multimodal data, capturing spatiotemporal dynamics, and operating under uncertainty. Traditional approaches often rely on static optimization models or oversimplified biological assumptions, limiting their ability to address real world complexities such as ecological feedback, regulatory compliance, and conflicts among user groups. Methods To overcome these limitations, we propose a computational intelligence framework that integrates high resolution ecological modeling with dynamic decision making and decentralized governance mechanisms. Central to this framework is the ECO STORM model (Ecologically Constrained Optimization via Stochastic Temporal Resource Management), which employs a stochastic dynamic programming approach to unify biomass evolution, habitat conditions, and enforcement factors. Neural function approximators are utilized for value iteration, while softmax parametrized policies enable adaptive effort allocation at the regional level. Complementing this, we introduce AQUA GOV (Adaptive Quota and Uncertainty Aware Governance), a multi agent governance strategy that allocates quotas under ecological and regulatory constraints, incorporating user specific preferences and compliance risks. AQUA GOV features fairness aware quota adjustments, stochastic monitoring, and a reinforcement loop that integrates stakeholder feedback. This closed loop system effectively models, forecasts, and manages marine resources under uncertainty. Results and Discussion Experiments conducted in simulated marine environments demonstrate the framework’s ability to maintain ecological thresholds, ensure policy compliance, and optimize resource distribution among diverse user groups. This work provides a scalable and intelligent solution for advancing marine governance in data rich, heterogeneous, and ecologically sensitive seascapes.

Local baselines reveal elevated groundwater nutrients, localised coastal enrichment, and broadly oligotrophic reef conditions in Laamu Atoll, Maldives
👤 Arnull, Jessica; Hashim, Aminath Shaha; Ganeshram, Raja S.; ... 📅 2026-09-18
Introduction Nutrient enrichment in coral reef systems is increasingly recognised as a major driver of ecological change, yet integrated assessments across groundwater, coastal, and reef environments remain limited in small island developing states (SIDS). Methods We assessed dissolved inorganic nutrients (total oxidised nitrogen, ammonium and phosphate), N:P ratios, and isotope-based tracers (δ 15 N, C:N) across groundwater, coastal-surface, and reef domains of Laamu Atoll, Maldives, between 2022 and 2024 to characterise spatial nutrient patterns and establish a baseline for environmental monitoring. Results Groundwater contained substantially higher nutrient concentrations – typically one to two orders of magnitude – than coastal-surface and reef waters, with several wells exceeding national drinking-water standards for ammonium. Coastal-surface waters showed localised nitrogen enrichment at several inhabited and poorly flushed shorelines, while reef waters remained characteristically oligotrophic with phosphate frequently below detection limits across most sites. N:P patterns suggested predominant, though not uniform, phosphorus limitation in groundwater, and more variable nutrient limitation across coastal and reef environments that differed by island and use-type context. Exploratory δ 15 N analysis in seagrass and macroalgal tissue showed weak group-level differences but localised enrichment near fish-processing and relative depletion near agricultural zones, while C:N ratios differed most strongly by species, with a secondary, weaker signal by land-use type. Discussion Together, these cross-domain nutrient gradients demonstrate clear spatial differences among groundwater, coastal-surface, and reef environments, consistent with localised nutrient enrichment in some nearshore settings and strong hydrodynamic control across the wider atoll. This study provides the first integrated nutrient baseline for Laamu Atoll, complemented by exploratory isotope data from a subset of islands, and contributes to the limited body of nutrient monitoring data available for coral reef systems in SIDS, supporting future monitoring and management of nutrient pressures associated with wastewater and agriculture.

Functional distinctions in feeding behavior of two herbivorous reef fish lineages
👤 Luenenborg, A; Mihalitsis, M; Wainwright, P C 📅 2026-09-19
Abstract Understanding the mechanisms and variation of how herbivorous fishes feed is key to understanding the dynamics of herbivory on coral reefs. In our comparative study of closely related nominally herbivorous fishes, we investigate the use of ten distinct behaviors used to support feeding on algae. We found that species within Siganidae share 5 out of the 10 behaviors with Acanthuridae while also exhibiting two additional family-specific behaviors: presence of nibbling and extra jaw closure. Siganids predominantly use movements of the jaws whereas acanthurids also employ body and head movements while detaching algae from the substrate. A comparison among siganid species revealed an axis of diversity in the frequency and extent of their feeding behaviors, with the main axis of variation being strongly associated with sociality (schooling vs. pairing) and morphology (short-snouted vs. long-snouted). Overall, we show that siganids and acanthurids display distinct behaviors during feeding. Thus, our results suggest potential niche differences between two major groups of herbivorous reef fishes.
