水产养殖可持续发展与鱼类资源管理研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文10篇，研究方向涵盖水产养殖营养与饲料替代、鱼类寄生虫分类学、鱼类生殖生物学与种群遗传学、渔业可持续性评估、鱼类病毒性疾病防控以及微藻水处理技术等多个领域。整体来看，水产养殖的可持续发展是本周的核心议题，多篇论文围绕饲料替代、天然活性物质应用和水质净化展开研究。与此同时，分类学与生态学基础研究亦有新发现，包括泰国淡水鱼类寄生虫新种和台湾吻虾虎鱼隐存种的描述。研究方法上，稳定同位素分析、中性粒模糊评价框架等新方法的应用值得关注。

创新性研究

  1. β-myrcene: A nature-identical compound for sustainable aquaculture（Journal of the World Aquaculture Society）

该研究提出β-月桂烯（β-myrcene）作为一种天然等同化合物在水产养殖中的可持续应用潜力。其创新之处在于将植物源天然化合物系统引入水产养殖的病害防控与生长促进领域，为减少化学药物依赖提供了新思路，对推动绿色水产养殖具有潜在影响。

  1. Assessing the Sustainability Performance of Fishing Harbors Under Uncertainty: A Gaussian-Neutrosophic Framework（Sustainable Development）

该研究首次将高斯中性模糊集（Gaussian-Neutrosophic）框架引入渔港可持续性评估，有效处理了评价过程中的不确定性问题。其方法论创新为渔业基础设施的可持续管理提供了更稳健的决策工具，有望推广至其他渔业管理场景。

  1. A new cryptic species of Rhinogobius from northern Taiwan（Journal of Fish Biology）

该研究描述了采自台湾翡翠水库的吻虾虎鱼隐存种——*Rhinogobius feitsuiensis* sp. nov.，并对*R. similis*种复合体内的隐存多样性进行了讨论。隐存种的发现提示该物种复合体的多样性可能被严重低估，对台湾及东亚淡水鱼类生物多样性保护与分类学修订具有重要意义。

  1. Antiviral activity of chlorogenic acid against largemouth bass virus（Journal of Fish Biology）

该研究首次系统评估了绿原酸（chlorogenic acid, CGA）对大口黑鲈病毒（LMBV）的体内外抗病毒活性。将天然多酚化合物应用于鱼类病毒病防控，为水产养殖中抗病毒药物的开发提供了候选分子，也为替代传统化学抗病毒策略开辟了新路径。

  1. Identification of Microalgae, Evaluation of Water Purification Efficiency, and Optimization of Large-Scale Production Conditions Based on RAS（Plants）

该研究筛选了四种微藻对循环水养殖系统（RAS）废水中氨氮和亚硝态氮的去除能力，并优化了大规模生产条件。其创新在于将微藻水处理与RAS实际工况相结合，为养殖尾水的生物净化提供了可规模化实施的方案。

ABSTRACT The morphology of the olfactory organ of fish exhibits considerable variability, which reflects taxonomical position and also adaptations to the diverse environmental conditions in which these animals live. The findings of studies on the representative of the Mastacembelidae family, Macrognathus aculeatus , provide a further illustration of this phenomenon. This species is a carnivorous, bottom‐dwelling fish that frequently buries itself in the substrate. The olfactory canal, situated posterior to the tubular incurrent nostril, is lined with non‐sensory squamous epithelium. 👤 Nakib, Nirjhar; Alam, Mohammed Shahidul; Islam, Md Rakibul; ... 📅 2026-09-29
ABSTRACT Ompok bimaculatus , a silurid catfish, is a commercially valuable and endangered species in Bangladesh. Kaptai Lake is a critical habitat for this species, yet growing market demand and ecological pressures jeopardize stock sustainability. Therefore, this study employed a length‐based approach integrating LB‐SPR and aLBI to conduct the baseline stock assessment of the species in the lake. The LB‐SPR suggests that the stock may be experiencing overfishing with a reduced spawning potential ratio ( SPR = 0.30) and unsustainable fishing pressure ( F / M = 1.32). Furthermore, the length at 50% selectivity ( SL 50 = 19.67 cm) fell below the length at 50% maturity ( L 50 = 21.02 cm), indicating potential growth overfishing. Phytoplankton Dynamics in Contrasting Coastal Embayments of South Australia: Implications for Bivalve Aquaculture
👤 Rahman, Maziidah A.; Henderson, Shaun; van Ruth, Paul D.; Li... 📅 2026-09-29
This study investigated seasonal phytoplankton communities in two contrasting oyster-growing embayments, Coffin Bay and Franklin Harbour (Eyre Peninsula, South Australia), to assess how environmental conditions influence food availability across the full phytoplankton size spectrum. In both embayments, nutrient availability was a major driver of phytoplankton dynamics during summer, while seasonal changes in physical conditions coincided with a shift in the phytoplankton community from oligotrophic to mesotrophic assemblages. In Coffin Bay, the community was dominated by picoplankton (<2 µm), accounting for more than 50% of total biomass for over 8 months of the year. In contrast, Franklin Harbour was dominated by larger phytoplankton (>20 µm). Productivity in outer Coffin Bay was primarily supported by inflowing shelf waters, which sustained peak picoeukaryote biomass (44.27 µg C L−1), and was further enhanced by a late-summer upwelling event in the eastern Great Australian Bight. However, productivity in Franklin Harbour was primarily generated internally, with the highest picoeukaryote biomass (68.15 µg C L−1) in the southern end. This study provides new insights into phytoplankton dynamics across the full spectrum in two contrasting embayments. The findings demonstrate how variations across the phytoplankton size spectrum can inform assessments of coastal productivity and carrying capacity, with implications for the sustainable management of bivalve aquaculture in temperate coastal regions.

The Impact of Fisheries and Aquaculture Production on Carbon Emissions: The Role of Natural Resource Rents
👤 Atılgan, Dilek 📅 2026-09-28
This study investigates the long-run relationships between aquaculture production (AP), fisheries production (FP), natural resource rents (NRR), economic growth, and CO₂ emissions in China, India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and Malaysia over the period 1990–2021. Long-run relationships are examined using the LM bootstrap co-integration test, while the Common Correlated Effects Mean Group (CCEMG) estimator is employed for long-run coefficient estimation. The Augmented Mean Group (AMG) estimator and additional sensitivity analyses are also used to assess the robustness of the findings. The baseline CCEMG results do not reveal statistically significant panel-level effects of AP, FP, NRR, or GDP. However, the Augmented Mean Group (AMG) results indicate that AP is negatively associated with CO₂ emissions, whereas FP and GDP have positive effects, while NRR remains statistically insignificant. Country-specific estimates reveal substantial heterogeneity, with AP reducing CO₂ emissions in China, India, and Malaysia, while GDP increases emissions in India, Thailand, and Malaysia. NRR also exhibits heterogeneous effects, increasing emissions in India while reducing them in Vietnam. Sensitivity analyses further indicate that the estimated effect of FP varies across model specifications, while the main findings are not solely driven by China. Overall, the results highlight the importance of country-specific production structures, resource management practices, and economic conditions in shaping the environmental effects of aquatic food production. The findings support country-specific policies promoting energy-efficient and environmentally sustainable aquatic food systems.

A survey of the Lake Superior cisco complex using integrated morphological and genetic tools reveals a presumed extinct species, Shortnose Cisco (Coregonus reighardi)
👤 Gorman, Owen T.; Ackiss, Amanda; Pratt, Thomas C; Lyons, Jos... 📅 2026-09-29
Throughout the 19th and 20th centuries, anthropogenic activities resulted in losses of ciscoes in the Laurentian Great Lakes (LGL) and only Lake Superior was left with a relatively intact complex. In 1929, W. Koelz described six cisco species from Lake Superior: Coregonus artedi, C. hoyi, C. kiyi, C. zenithicus, C. reighardi, and C. nigripinnis. Of these, artedi, hoyi and kiyi remain abundant in Lake Superior, zenithicus is considered extirpated throughout the LGL except in Lake Superior, and reighardi and nigripinnis are considered extinct. We present results reaffirming the presence of artedi, hoyi, and kiyi, and the presence of the presumed extinct reighardi. Our analysis did not confirm the presence of zenithicus and nigripinnis. The principal morphological characters that distinguish artedi, hoyi, kiyi, and reighardi reflect habitat and trophic specialization: eye size, gillraker length and gillraker spacing. The discovery of reighardi is expected to stimulate conservation and restoration of rare ciscoes in the LGL, and our integrated morphological and genetic approach can provide insights into the evolution, diversity, and ecology of ciscoes.

👤 Wei, Songlin; Lin, Jingmin; Xue, Ruichao; Xu, Wei 📅 2026-09-29
Vibrio alginolyticus is a halophilic, Gram-negative marine bacterium that has emerged as a significant opportunistic pathogen affecting both marine animals and humans. As one of the most abundant vibrios in coastal and estuarine environments worldwide, V. alginolyticus causes substantial economic losses in aquaculture through vibriosis in fish, shrimp, and shellfish, while also being responsible for wound infections, otitis externa, and occasionally gastroenteritis in humans following seawater exposure or seafood consumption. The pathogenesis of V. alginolyticus is multifactorial, involving an arsenal of virulence determinants including secretion systems (type III, type VI, and type II), haemolysins, adhesins, biofilm formation, quorum sensing-regulated factors, and the recently recognized contributions of non-coding RNAs and outer membrane vesicles. Recent genomic analyses have revealed considerable genetic diversity among isolates and identified a ubiquitous presence of β-lactam resistance genes, raising concerns about natural resistance and the potential for horizontal gene transfer. The interplay between rising seawater temperatures, intensive aquaculture practices, and increasing antimicrobial resistance has positioned V. alginolyticus as an emerging pathogen of growing concern. This Review synthesizes current knowledge on the epidemiology, genomics, virulence mechanisms, host interactions, and control strategies of V. alginolyticus , with an emphasis on the molecular determinants of pathogenesis, including the regulatory networks governing adhesion, biofilm formation, and metabolic adaptation, and the challenges posed by antimicrobial resistance. We also discuss promising alternative control approaches, including phage therapy, vaccination, and probiotic strategies, and identify key knowledge gaps that warrant future investigation.

Discovery of Post‐Translationally Modified Epitopes in Autoimmunity: Someone Has to Fish so Everyone Can Eat
👤 Arshad, Sanya; Cameron, Benjamin M.; Joglekar, Alok V. 📅 2026-09-28
ABSTRACT Post‐translational modifications (PTMs) chemically diversify the ~20,000 genomically encoded proteins into millions of functional variants, and this diversity has profound consequences for T cell‐mediated immune responses. PTM‐reactive T cells have been identified across numerous autoimmune diseases and, in several cases, implicated directly as pathogenic drivers. Mechanistically, PTMs promote autoimmunity through two intertwined routes: by altering peptide–MHC and TCR binding to favor recognition of self‐epitopes, and by generating neoepitopes in peripheral and/or inflamed tissues that are absent from the thymus, allowing PTM‐reactive T cells to escape central tolerance. This cascade is amplified by inflammatory cytokines, upregulated antigen‐processing machinery, disease‐associated HLA alleles, and, in some cases, exogenous triggers such as diet. We use three well‐characterized examples—citrullination in multiple autoimmune disorders, hybrid insulin peptide (HIP) formation in type 1 diabetes, and gluten deamidation in celiac disease—to illustrate how distinct PTM biology, tissue specificity, and genetic encodability shape the antigenic landscape and dictate feasible discovery strategies. We then review four complementary approaches for identifying PTM‐specific T cell responses: mass spectrometry‐based proteomics and immunopeptidomics, computational MHC‐binding prediction, antigen‐directed methods, and high‐throughput T cell receptor (TCR)‐directed library screening methods. For each, we discuss underlying principles, strengths, and limitations, with particular attention to the challenge posed by non‐canonical, PTM‐bearing residues. By comparing citrullination, HIPs, and gluten deamidation across these methodologies, we highlight how biochemical alterations, tissue accessibility, and mouse‐to‐human conservation determine which discovery approach is most tractable. We conclude that while synthetic peptide‐based methods remain the standard for validating known epitopes, emerging high‐throughput, cell‐based platforms are rapidly expanding the accessibility of the PTM antigenic landscape for both known and undiscovered autoimmune epitopes.

The country is endowed with various aquaculture technologies and opportunities for job creation, youth involvement, and development of aquaculture.

Dietary flexibility differs between wild- and domesticated-origin Eurasian perch larvae from contrasting broodstock histories: implications for aquaculture of emerging species
👤 Palińska-Żarska, Katarzyna; Niewiadomski, Piotr; Król, Jaros... 📅 2026-09-29
Abstract The domestication of fish is a key step in aquaculture, shaping traits required for adaptation to captive rearing conditions. Previous studies suggested that domesticated Eurasian perch ( Perca fluviatilis ) larvae perform well on formulated feeds, whereas wild-origin larvae exhibit greater dietary flexibility. We hypothesized that larvae originating from wild broodstock would better cope with alternative protein sources than larvae from domesticated broodstock. To test this, we compared the zootechnical performance, digestive enzyme activity, and digestive gene expression of wild- and domesticated-origin larvae fed diets containing soybean meal, poultry meal, or their combination, using a commercial high-fishmeal diet as the control. Wild-origin larvae generally performed better on alternative protein diets, whereas domesticated-origin larvae achieved the best growth on the control diet, indicating a diet-dependent performance trade-off rather than an overall advantage of either group. These findings should be interpreted with caution because the wild and domesticated broodstocks originated from different geographic populations and did not share a common ancestral background. Nevertheless, the observed differences are consistent with the hypothesis that broodstock history and previous nutritional experience may influence the dietary adaptability of offspring. Our results suggest that maintaining traits associated with dietary flexibility may facilitate the transition toward more sustainable aquafeeds and should be considered when developing future breeding and domestication strategies for Eurasian perch.

This study evaluated the microbiological quality and antibiotic resistance profiles of bacteria isolated from street-vended smoked fish sold in Owo, Ondo State, Nigeria. Nine smoked fish samples were obtained from three vending locations and analysed using standard microbiological procedures. Bacterial isolates were identified through conventional biochemical methods, while antimicrobial susceptibility was assessed using the Kirby–Bauer disc diffusion method. Improved hygienic handling, routine microbiological surveillance and antimicrobial-resistance monitoring are therefore important for strengthening the safety of smoked fish sold to consumers.

Nevertheless, these systems are undergoing accelerated biodiversity loss, driven by intensifying anthropogenic pressures that often surpass the impacts observed in terrestrial and marine environments. Protected areas are central to biodiversity conservation, but their effectiveness in freshwater systems is limited by hydrological connectivity and the mismatch between fixed boundaries and dynamic ecological processes. In this study, we assessed the ecological and fisheries performance of Special Fish Habitats (SFHs) in Serbia by comparing 25 SFH and 19 non-SFH localities across the Ibar and Nišava River basins, using field surveys conducted through standardized electrofishing from 2022 to 2025 and fish stock monitoring records covering 2016–2021.
