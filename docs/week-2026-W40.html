鱼类多样性与可持续水产养殖研究进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文10篇，研究方向涵盖水产养殖营养与饲料替代、鱼类寄生虫分类学、鱼类生殖生物学与种群遗传学、渔业可持续性评估、鱼类病毒性疾病防控以及微藻水处理技术等多个领域。整体来看，水产养殖的可持续发展是本周的核心议题，多篇论文围绕饲料替代、天然活性物质应用和水质净化展开研究。与此同时，分类学与生态学基础研究亦有新发现，包括泰国淡水鱼类寄生虫新种和台湾吻虾虎鱼隐存种的描述。研究方法上，稳定同位素分析、中性粒模糊评价框架等新方法的应用值得关注。

创新性研究

  1. β-myrcene: A nature-identical compound for sustainable aquaculture（Journal of the World Aquaculture Society）

该研究提出β-月桂烯（β-myrcene）作为一种天然等同化合物在水产养殖中的可持续应用潜力。其创新之处在于将植物源天然化合物系统引入水产养殖的病害防控与生长促进领域，为减少化学药物依赖提供了新思路，对推动绿色水产养殖具有潜在影响。

  1. Assessing the Sustainability Performance of Fishing Harbors Under Uncertainty: A Gaussian-Neutrosophic Framework（Sustainable Development）

该研究首次将高斯中性模糊集（Gaussian-Neutrosophic）框架引入渔港可持续性评估，有效处理了评价过程中的不确定性问题。其方法论创新为渔业基础设施的可持续管理提供了更稳健的决策工具，有望推广至其他渔业管理场景。

  1. A new cryptic species of Rhinogobius from northern Taiwan（Journal of Fish Biology）

该研究描述了采自台湾翡翠水库的吻虾虎鱼隐存种——*Rhinogobius feitsuiensis* sp. nov.，并对*R. similis*种复合体内的隐存多样性进行了讨论。隐存种的发现提示该物种复合体的多样性可能被严重低估，对台湾及东亚淡水鱼类生物多样性保护与分类学修订具有重要意义。

  1. Antiviral activity of chlorogenic acid against largemouth bass virus（Journal of Fish Biology）

该研究首次系统评估了绿原酸（chlorogenic acid, CGA）对大口黑鲈病毒（LMBV）的体内外抗病毒活性。将天然多酚化合物应用于鱼类病毒病防控，为水产养殖中抗病毒药物的开发提供了候选分子，也为替代传统化学抗病毒策略开辟了新路径。

  1. Identification of Microalgae, Evaluation of Water Purification Efficiency, and Optimization of Large-Scale Production Conditions Based on RAS（Plants）

该研究筛选了四种微藻对循环水养殖系统（RAS）废水中氨氮和亚硝态氮的去除能力，并优化了大规模生产条件。其创新在于将微藻水处理与RAS实际工况相结合，为养殖尾水的生物净化提供了可规模化实施的方案。

Morphological Variety of Fish Olfactory Organ in Terms of Phylogenetic Position and Adaptations to the Environment Illustrated by Lesser Spiny Eel Macrognathus aculeatus Bloch 1786 (Mastacembelidae, Teleostei) With Revision of Its Olfactory Organ Description
👤 Dymek, Jakub; Michalska, Hanna; Podkowa, Dagmara; Dymek, Ann... 📅 2026-09-29
ABSTRACT The morphology of the olfactory organ of fish exhibits considerable variability, which reflects taxonomical position and also adaptations to the diverse environmental conditions in which these animals live. The findings of studies on the representative of the Mastacembelidae family, Macrognathus aculeatus , provide a further illustration of this phenomenon. This species is a carnivorous, bottom‐dwelling fish that frequently buries itself in the substrate. The olfactory organ present in M. aculeatus consists of left and right elongated canals, each with an olfactory rosette. The olfactory canal, situated posterior to the tubular incurrent nostril, is lined with non‐sensory squamous epithelium. In the subsequent broad part of the canal, the epithelium forms an olfactory rosette. The structure is composed of a small median raphe and olfactory lamellae positioned on either side of the raphe. The olfactory epithelium, which lines the olfactory lamellae, contains several types of olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs). These include ciliated and crypt ORNs, as well as ORNs with long microvilli. On the level of the excurrent nostril, there is a vent of a voluminous ethmoid accessory nasal sac. We provide a revised characterization of the olfactory organ of M. aculeatus , complementing earlier descriptions.

The Impact of Fisheries and Aquaculture Production on Carbon Emissions: The Role of Natural Resource Rents
👤 Atılgan, Dilek 📅 2026-09-28
This study investigates the long-run relationships between aquaculture production (AP), fisheries production (FP), natural resource rents (NRR), economic growth, and CO₂ emissions in China, India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and Malaysia over the period 1990–2021. Long-run relationships are examined using the LM bootstrap co-integration test, while the Common Correlated Effects Mean Group (CCEMG) estimator is employed for long-run coefficient estimation. The Augmented Mean Group (AMG) estimator and additional sensitivity analyses are also used to assess the robustness of the findings. The baseline CCEMG results do not reveal statistically significant panel-level effects of AP, FP, NRR, or GDP. However, the Augmented Mean Group (AMG) results indicate that AP is negatively associated with CO₂ emissions, whereas FP and GDP have positive effects, while NRR remains statistically insignificant. Country-specific estimates reveal substantial heterogeneity, with AP reducing CO₂ emissions in China, India, and Malaysia, while GDP increases emissions in India, Thailand, and Malaysia. NRR also exhibits heterogeneous effects, increasing emissions in India while reducing them in Vietnam. Sensitivity analyses further indicate that the estimated effect of FP varies across model specifications, while the main findings are not solely driven by China. Overall, the results highlight the importance of country-specific production structures, resource management practices, and economic conditions in shaping the environmental effects of aquatic food production. The findings support country-specific policies promoting energy-efficient and environmentally sustainable aquatic food systems.

Onboard respirometry in a marine recreational fishery reveals predictors of post-capture recovery in adult coho salmon
👤 Van Wert, Jacey; Hendriks, Brian J; Johnston, Stephen; Zinn,... 📅 2026-09-28
Annually, billions of fish are captured and released under the assumption that they will recover and survive, which may not occur. Fishing-induced stressors require subsequent recovery, characterized by increased energy expenditure, greater vulnerability to predation and disease, and lost opportunities for critical activities such as feeding and migration. This study examined the physiological responses of adult coho salmon (Oncorhynchus kisutch) to interactions with fishing gear in coastal British Columbia, Canada. Collaborating with recreational fishers, we used novel onboard respirometers to measure metabolic recovery for 1 h, alongside blood sampling to assess physiological status. We examined how angling-induced injuries (scale loss, line wrap, fin and eye damage) and intrinsic and extrinsic factors (fork length, sex, preexisting wounds, temperature, capture duration) affect post-capture recovery. Fish with greater scale loss or line wrap incurred higher recovery costs and more acidic blood, respectively, while smaller fish and longer fight duration demonstrated greater acidosis and lactate accumulation. Comparisons with lab-based chase protocols on the same individuals revealed that standard lab methods underestimated aerobic capacity and short-term recovery, underscoring the value of the novel methodology for fisheries management and conservation.

Reduction in IgE reactivity of fish parvalbumin through heat denaturation
👤 Kobayashi, Yukihiro; Kubota, Hiroyuki; Ishikawa, Kenta; Kawa... 📅 2026-09-28
Abstract Parvalbumin is a prominent fish allergen with notable heat stability in structural and allergenic properties. However, high temperatures reduce immunoglobulin E (IgE) reactivity, likely due to the destruction of IgE-binding epitopes via heat-induced protein denaturation. This study investigated how heat treatment affects parvalbumin denaturation and IgE reactivity. IgE reactivity of patient sera was examined using Pacific chub mackerel extracts and purified parvalbumin by western blotting and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Parvalbumin IgE reactivity remained largely unchanged after 10 min at 100 °C, but declined significantly after 60 min. At 120 °C, IgE reactivity decreased rapidly. Denaturation of purified parvalbumin was assessed using phenylalanine-derived intrinsic fluorescence, hydrophobic region exposure with 1-anilinonaphthalene−8-sulfonic acid, and calcium-binding ability using Quin 2. The purified parvalbumin showed partial denaturation at 100 °C and complete denaturation at 120 °C. Western blotting of extracts from 20 fish species using a conformation-sensitive anti-parvalbumin antibody revealed slight reactivity after 60 min at 100 °C but complete loss after 60 min at 120 °C. ELISA showed slight IgE reactivity in many species after 60 min at 100 °C; however, all reactivities disappeared after 60 min at 120 °C. Parvalbumin from diverse fish species shows marked thermal stability, but heating at 120 °C induces denaturation and abolishes detectable IgE reactivity under the conditions tested.

Validation of an HPLC-FLD method for the determination of aflatoxins (B1, B2, G1, G2) in cereals, coffee and dried seafood and its application to the survey of Aflatoxin contamination in selected foods in Hanoi
👤 Vu Thi Kim, Oanh 📅 2026-09-28
This study aimed to establish the analytical parameters and validate a high-performance liquid chromatography with fluorescence detection (HPLC-FLD) method for the determination of aflatoxins (AFB1, AFB2, AFG1, and AFG2) in food samples. The validated method used to assess aflatoxin contamination in cereals, coffee products, and dried seafood collected in Hanoi. Positive samples were further confirmed by liquid chromatography&ndash;tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), and the quantitative results obtained by the two methods were compared. The validated method demonstrated good specificity, with limits of detection (LOD) and quantification (LOQ) of 0.5 and 1.0 &mu;g/kg, respectively, for each aflatoxin. Five samples (5.0%) were found to be contaminated with total aflatoxins at concentrations ranging from 9.04 to 726 &micro;g/kg. 👤 Huang, Xuhong; Zhang, Yixin; Lin, Jinghui; Zhan, Weixuan; Ch... 📅 2026-09-29
This study addresses path redundancy, excessive turning, and high hydrodynamic cost in full‐coverage path planning for biomimetic robotic fish operating in cluttered underwater environments. A three‐point‐crossover genetic algorithm with local refinement is proposed for fixed‐depth two‐dimensional coverage tasks. By using discrete grid modeling, coverage, path length, smoothness, and flow‐related propulsion energy are jointly optimized in a unified fitness function. The three‐point crossover is designed to retain useful contiguous subsequences from parent paths during recombination, while a diversity‐guided adaptive mutation strategy maintains exploration when the population begins to stagnate. Discovery of Post‐Translationally Modified Epitopes in Autoimmunity: Someone Has to Fish so Everyone Can Eat
👤 Arshad, Sanya; Cameron, Benjamin M.; Joglekar, Alok V. 📅 2026-09-28
ABSTRACT Post‐translational modifications (PTMs) chemically diversify the ~20,000 genomically encoded proteins into millions of functional variants, and this diversity has profound consequences for T cell‐mediated immune responses. PTM‐reactive T cells have been identified across numerous autoimmune diseases and, in several cases, implicated directly as pathogenic drivers. Mechanistically, PTMs promote autoimmunity through two intertwined routes: by altering peptide–MHC and TCR binding to favor recognition of self‐epitopes, and by generating neoepitopes in peripheral and/or inflamed tissues that are absent from the thymus, allowing PTM‐reactive T cells to escape central tolerance. This cascade is amplified by inflammatory cytokines, upregulated antigen‐processing machinery, disease‐associated HLA alleles, and, in some cases, exogenous triggers such as diet. We use three well‐characterized examples—citrullination in multiple autoimmune disorders, hybrid insulin peptide (HIP) formation in type 1 diabetes, and gluten deamidation in celiac disease—to illustrate how distinct PTM biology, tissue specificity, and genetic encodability shape the antigenic landscape and dictate feasible discovery strategies. We then review four complementary approaches for identifying PTM‐specific T cell responses: mass spectrometry‐based proteomics and immunopeptidomics, computational MHC‐binding prediction, antigen‐directed methods, and high‐throughput T cell receptor (TCR)‐directed library screening methods. For each, we discuss underlying principles, strengths, and limitations, with particular attention to the challenge posed by non‐canonical, PTM‐bearing residues. By comparing citrullination, HIPs, and gluten deamidation across these methodologies, we highlight how biochemical alterations, tissue accessibility, and mouse‐to‐human conservation determine which discovery approach is most tractable. We conclude that while synthetic peptide‐based methods remain the standard for validating known epitopes, emerging high‐throughput, cell‐based platforms are rapidly expanding the accessibility of the PTM antigenic landscape for both known and undiscovered autoimmune epitopes.

One Health and Antimicrobial Resistance in Aquaculture: Implications for Interdisciplinary Public Health Education
👤 Hidayatullah, M.Syarif; Muthia Cenderadewi 📅 2026-09-28
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquaculture creates public health risks through foodborne exposure, occupational contact, and environmental contamination. This literature review examined peer-reviewed evidence on the implications of aquaculture-related AMR for human and community health and evaluated the application of a One Health approach, while considering how the synthesis may inform interdisciplinary public health education. A systematic search was conducted using PubMed, ScienceDirect, and BMC Public Health for full-text articles published between 2010 and 2025 that linked One Health, AMR, and aquaculture or coastal communities. Five studies met the inclusion criteria and were synthesized thematically, supported by contextual literature. Four interrelated themes emerged: seafood-borne transmission of resistant bacteria; occupational exposure among aquaculture workers; environmental dissemination through aquatic systems and sediments; and gaps in cross-sector governance and surveillance. The findings indicate that aquaculture-related AMR extends beyond animal health and requires coordinated attention to food safety, worker protection, environmental health, antimicrobial stewardship, and integrated surveillance. Nevertheless, these systems are undergoing accelerated biodiversity loss, driven by intensifying anthropogenic pressures that often surpass the impacts observed in terrestrial and marine environments. Protected areas are central to biodiversity conservation, but their effectiveness in freshwater systems is limited by hydrological connectivity and the mismatch between fixed boundaries and dynamic ecological processes. In this study, we assessed the ecological and fisheries performance of Special Fish Habitats (SFHs) in Serbia by comparing 25 SFH and 19 non-SFH localities across the Ibar and Nišava River basins, using field surveys conducted through standardized electrofishing from 2022 to 2025 and fish stock monitoring records covering 2016–2021. Accordingly, assessing their sustainability performance has become an important concern for both policymakers, harbor operating organizations, and harbor managers. This study develops a decision support system (DSS) within a neutrosophic environment to evaluate the sustainability performance of small‐scale fishing harbors. Dactylostomum brayi n. sp. (Trematoda: Opecoelidae) from the Fish Hemibagrus nemurus (Bagridae) in Khun Thale Swamp, Thailand
👤 Kamchoo, Kanda; Ribas, Alexis; Gilardoni, Carmen; Poonlaphde... 📅 2026-09-28
During a parasitological survey of freshwater fishes from Khun Thale Swamp, Surat Thani Province, Thailand, a new species of opecoelid trematode, Dactylostomum brayi n. sp., was recovered from the intestine of the Asian redtail catfish Hemibagrus nemurus (Bagridae). Of the 1065 fishes examined, representing 33 species, 27 genera, and 16 families, the parasite was found exclusively in H. nemurus, with a prevalence of 27.27% and a mean intensity of 2.33 worms per infected host, suggesting a high degree of host specificity. The new species differs from all recognised freshwater congeners in the number of ventral sucker papillae, body proportions relative to the oral sucker, and the morphology of the reproductive organs. This represents the second record of Dactylostomum from Thailand, following specimens identified as Dactylostomum sp. by R. A. Bray from the same host and deposited in the Natural History Museum, London, in 1983. The present study also provides the first molecular characterisation (18S, ITS2, and 28S rRNA) for the genus Dactylostomum and reveals a close phylogenetic relationship with Dimerosaccus. These findings contribute to the taxonomy and distribution of Dactylostomum and provide a basis for future systematic and phylogenetic studies of this poorly known genus.

Effects of Replacing Fish Meal and Part of Soybean Meal with Enzymatically Hydrolyzed Soybean Meal on Growth Performance, Diarrhea Incidence, and Intestinal Health in Weaned Piglets
👤 Wang, Nan; Tang, Fu; Nie, Yuyilin; Zhang, Shanyuan; Li, Yuek... 📅 2026-09-28
The aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of substituting fish meal and part of soybean meal with enzymatically hydrolyzed soybean meal (EHSM) on growth performance, diarrhea incidence, antioxidant and immune status, and intestinal health in weaned piglets. Eighty 25-day-old piglets were distributed into two treatments with 8 replicate pens of 5 piglets each and fed a control diet or an EHSM diet for 42 days, with EHSM completely replacing fish meal and partially replacing soybean meal at inclusion levels of 9% and 4% during the pre-starter and starter phases, respectively. Compared with controls, EHSM increased day-42 body weight, average daily gain during days 28–42 and overall, and decreased feed conversion ratio during days 0–14 (p < 0.05). EHSM also reduced diarrhea incidence during days 0–14 (p = 0.050), decreased jejunal crypt depth, elevated jejunal villus height-to-crypt depth ratio, and increased NQO1 mRNA expression (p < 0.05). Plasma antioxidant and immunoglobulin indices were unaffected. Overall, EHSM improved growth and intestinal health, supporting its use as an alternative protein ingredient in weaned piglet diets. Additional research is warranted to clarify how EHSM influences intestinal function in weaned piglets.
