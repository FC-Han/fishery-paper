本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文27篇，研究覆盖面较为广泛，涉及水产养殖技术与管理、鱼类生理与遗传、渔业生态与栖息地、鱼类病害与食品安全、以及渔业社会经济等多个方向。从研究区域看，论文来自中国、韩国、印度尼西亚、尼日利亚、罗马尼亚、希腊、菲律宾、阿根廷等多个国家和地区，呈现出明显的国际化特征。整体研究趋势上，水产养殖的可持续性（替代饲料、生物材料利用、病害防控）与渔业生态连通性（eDNA、栖息地评估）构成本周的两大主线，同时应用型社区研究和技术方法创新也占有一定比例。

This study assessed whether two campus ponds at Politeknik Negeri Sriwijaya Banyuasin, Indonesia, are suitable for tilapia culture, using physical and chemical water-quality parameters and a descriptive quantitative design. Water was sampled at three stations per pond (inlet, center, outlet) on two occasions, with duplicate measurements. We reported temperature, total dissolved solids (TDS), dissolved oxygen (DO), pH, ammonia, phosphate, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), and chemical oxygen demand (COD). Both ponds therefore offer supportive oxygen conditions but are constrained by organic loading and nutrient enrichment. They should be regarded as usable only after water-quality management and confirmatory monitoring, including dawn-time DO, nitrite, alkalinity, and the analytical basis of ammonia and phosphate. Artificial Intelligence Applications in True Crabs (Brachyura) Across Aquaculture, Fisheries, Ecology, and Processing: A Scoping Review
👤 Bual, Cyrus Lawrence C.; Bual, Rachel C.; Vicerra, Ryan Rhay... 📅 2026-11-11
Artificial intelligence is increasingly applied to crab research, yet no prior review has mapped this work or its progress toward real-world use. This scoping review maps artificial intelligence applications in true crabs (Brachyura) across aquaculture, fisheries, ecology, and processing. Following the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews, five databases were searched for records published from 2016 to 14 June 2026, yielding 11,172 records, most from databases whose broad field coverage returned papers mentioning crabs or artificial intelligence only in passing. Of these, 88 peer-reviewed English-language primary studies applying artificial intelligence to brachyuran data and reporting at least one quantitative performance metric were included. Each study was charted for species, task domain, model architecture, dataset, performance, and deployment maturity (Technology Readiness Level). The literature is young and expanding rapidly, with nearly half published since 2025, and heavily concentrated, with China contributing most affiliations and three species accounting for more than two-thirds of studies. Twelve task domains were identified, dominated by quality assessment and sex classification. The field has converged on object detectors with attention modules, but accuracy is rarely comparable because datasets and protocols are proprietary. Most systems remain at laboratory validation. Reported accuracy has therefore advanced faster than demonstrated deployment.

Drone-Based IoT System for Spatial Monitoring of Pond Water Quality for Precision Aquaculture
👤 V. B. C. Sekararao, Kurada; Palacherla, Venkatrao 📅 2026-11-10
Aquaculture is an important source of food, employment and economic development, but maintaining appropriate water quality remains a major challenge for farmers. Parameters like temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), turbidity and salinity have significant effects on the growth, health, feeding efficiency and survival of aquatic organisms. Traditional monitoring relies mainly on manual sampling and periodic measurements, which is time-consuming and may not provide sufficient spatial and temporal information for precision aquaculture. This paper proposes an IoT-based mobile water-quality monitoring architecture with an unmanned aerial vehicle UAV (drone) integrated with an autonomous autopilot system. The system receives the registered GPS coordinates of an aquaculture pond and automatically navigates the UAV to the pond for measurement. When the UAV arrives at the pond, it visits several preselected sites and submerges a detachable multi-parameter sensing module to collect the water-quality parameters. An embedded controller processes the sensor data and transmits the measurements, GPS coordinates and time stamps to a cloud database via wireless communication. One mobile sensing unit can monitor multiple ponds and form a spatially indexed historical database of water quality. The architecture lays a foundation for AI-based anomaly detection, water-quality prediction, disease-risk assessment, and intelligent decision support for precision aquaculture.

The Wearing Layer Influences Long-Term Material Loss from Composite Floats for Oyster Aquaculture: An Accelerated-Weathering Comparison of Eight Candidate Materials
👤 Mun, So Youn; Jang, Pilseong; Jang, Jun Hwan 📅 2026-11-09
Expanded polystyrene floats have been identified as a significant source of marine microplastic in oyster aquaculture, yet no certification criterion assesses the material that the certified alternatives shed. Gravimetric mass loss, normalised to a common exposed area of 7200 mm2, is reported under accredited accelerated weathering to KS F 2274 for eight float materials: expanded polystyrene at three densities, expanded polypropylene, three injection-moulded polymers, and a continuous-fibre basalt-reinforced thermoplastic composite. Specimens were dried to constant mass before weighing, so the quantity measured is a net change: an upper bound on particulate release, not a measure of it. Mass exceeded the initial value in all three stabilised expanded polypropylene specimens. At 3000 h the loss spanned a factor of 54, and every foamed material exceeded every non-foamed one, while tripling the density of expanded polystyrene reduced loss by only 25% (n = 3; p < 0.0001 across the foamed groups). For ten injection-moulded grades the accelerated ranking reproduced that of 2.5 years outdoors (Spearman ρ = 0.89), though the ratio varied from 0.4 to 6.3. A wear model indicates that the pure-polymer barrier providing water tightness contains 905–1180 g L−1 of polymer against 578 g L−1 for the laminate beneath, so the wearing layer, not the bulk material, may govern release in service.

Towards Sustainable Shrimp Fisheries in a West Africa Lagoon: Gear Efficiency in the Nokoué Lagoon With Its Inlet
👤 Sintondji, Sèlomè Wilfried; Sohou, Zacharie; Vodounnou, Just... 📅 2026-11-11
ABSTRACT The continued use of prohibited fishing gear in inland waters around the world poses a direct threat to the sound management of global fish stocks. This is also the case in Benin, where there has been a drastic decline in shrimp production since 2002, particularly in the Nokoué lagoon. This study aimed to compare the capture capacity (in space and time) of two types of fishing gear (traps and nets) that are less destructive on shrimp catches in the Nokoué lagoon and its inlet complex (North, South, Centre, East and West), as part of a sustainable exploitation strategy. In total, two genera of shrimp were sampled: Macrobrachium Spence Bate, 1868 and Penaeus Fabricius, 1798. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) analyses and Tukey's post hoc tests revealed a pronounced seasonal pattern in catches, particularly for Macrobrachium , with a peak in September and a low‐catch period from March to June. Nets showed a prolonged period of low catches, whereas traps revealed four seasonal catch levels. For Penaeus , five seasonal catch levels were identified, corresponding to prolonged peaks in catches between January and June, followed by a gradual decline to the annual minimum in October and November. The spatial distribution of catches reflects significant heterogeneity between stations. For Macrobrachium , traps caught mainly at Western station, characterized by a high density of ‘Acadja’, whereas nets showed a peak at Eastern station. For Penaeus , Western station had the highest catches for both types of gear, whereas the other stations had intermediate or low yields, highlighting the importance of spatial configuration and artificial habitats in the distribution of populations. Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests confirmed that there were no significant differences between the seasonal effectiveness of traps and nets for both genera, indicating comparable overall performance. However, the chi‐square test revealed a moderate but significant difference in the size frequencies of Penaeus , with nets capturing proportionally larger individuals 150–180 mm, whereas traps favoured intermediate sizes 90–120 mm. For Macrobrachium , no significant difference was observed, reflecting a population dominated by juveniles and adults. Chi‐square residual analysis corroborated these observations, showing an overrepresentation of large Penaeus sizes in nets and intermediate sizes in traps. For Macrobrachium , differences in catch according to size class remain limited to individuals measuring 90–120 mm, mainly caught by traps. These results highlight the complex interaction between gear selectivity, seasonal dynamics, and spatial heterogeneity, underscoring the importance of these factors for the sustainable management of shrimp populations in the Nokoué lagoon.

Mopane Worm ( Gonimbrasia belina ) as an Alternative Protein Source in Aquaculture: A Review of Nutritional Value, Digestibility and Physiological Responses
👤 Macate, Isadola Eusebio; Muhala, Valdemiro; Vilanculos, Rena... 📅 2026-11-11
ABSTRACT The expansion of aquaculture has intensified the search for sustainable alternatives to fishmeal. The mopane worm, Gonimbrasia belina (syn. Imbrasia belina ), is a traditionally consumed southern African insect whose protein, lipid and micronutrient contents have prompted interest in its use as an aquafeed ingredient. This semi‐systematic review evaluates the nutritional value and digestibility of mopane worm meal and its effects on growth, feed utilisation and physiological responses in fish. Several experimental studies on mopane worm meal in fish diets, reviews and related insect‐meal research used to examine its nutritional potential were identified. The available evidence suggests species‐specific and inclusion‐dependent responses. Mozambique tilapia ( Oreochromis mossambicus ) tolerated partial fishmeal replacement and exhibited increased activity of some digestive enzymes, although the fishmeal‐based control achieved the highest growth. By contrast, increasing replacement levels reduced growth, protein utilisation and digestibility in African catfish ( Clarias gariepinus ) and were associated with greater hepatic alteration. Dusky kob ( Argyrosomus japonicus ) showed favourable growth and feed‐utilisation responses at moderate inclusion levels without major changes in digestive enzymes or haematological variables. Overall, the evidence supports mopane worm meal as a potentially valuable supplementary protein, particularly in locally formulated diets and when replaced in moderate proportions (18% for Dusky kob). Although there are studies suggesting replacements up to 60% for Mozambique tilapia, there is no scientific evidence regarding the optimal range. However, the small number of short‐term feeding trials, variability in ingredient composition and processing, incomplete digestibility data and uncertain sustainability of wild harvesting prevent broad commercial recommendations. Standardised, long‐term and independently replicated studies are required to establish species‐specific inclusion thresholds and evaluate physiological, economic and ecological outcomes.

Methods: A total of 40 fish representing five species were collected by angling between June and September 2025, after the legal fishing prohibition period. Eight tissues per fish (muscle, gills, liver, skin, bones, scales, eyes, and caudal fin) were analyzed by inductively coupled plasma–mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Contamination and exposure were evaluated using the pollution index (PI), metal contamination index (MCI), estimated daily intake (EDI), target hazard quotient (THQ), and hazard index (HI) for non-carcinogenic risk, and carcinogenic risk and total carcinogenic risk (TCR) for carcinogenic risk. Species and tissue patterns were examined by multivariate analysis, including linear discriminant analysis (LDA). Results: Multivariate statistical analysis revealed distinct species- and tissue-related patterns in elemental composition. LDA differentiated species with an initial classification accuracy of 82.5% and a leave-one-out cross-validated accuracy of 72.5%, with Cr, Mn, Pb, Sr, and Ni having the highest contribution. Tissue discrimination was lower (approximately 60%), with Sr, Zn, and Cu as main discrimination variable. THQ values for individual elements and HI were below 1, indicating no appreciable non-carcinogenic risk under the assumed exposure conditions. The highest CR values were associated with Ni (nase muscle, 2.26 × 10−6) and As (nase skin, 6.28 × 10−7). TCR ranged from 3.16 × 10−7 to 3.37 × 10−6 and remained below 1 × 10−4, within the acceptable range. Conclusions: Elemental contamination in fish from the Arieș River was generally low, and species and tissue type influenced elemental composition and dietary relevance. Continued monitoring is advised, with seasonal sampling, inorganic arsenic speciation, and attention to species such as nase when assessing consumption risk.
