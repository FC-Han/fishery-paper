全球渔业可持续发展与水产养殖创新研究热点

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文2篇，研究主题较为分散，分别涉及渔业管理政策的社会经济维度与鱼类分类学的基础生物学研究。前者聚焦西非加纳的小规模渔业燃料补贴政策及其对渔业可持续性的影响，属于渔业管理与政策评估领域；后者则报道了越南海域发现的一个盲鳗新属新种，属于渔业资源生物学与分类学范畴。整体来看，本周研究数量较少，但体现了渔业科学中“管理实践”与“基础认知”并行的学科特点，前者关注人类活动与资源利用的平衡，后者则致力于完善物种多样性的基础图谱。

创新性研究

  1. Small‐Scale Fisherfolk Perspectives on Premix Fuel Subsidies and Fisheries Sustainability in Ghana（*Natural Resources Forum*）— 该研究从小型渔民主体视角切入，评估加纳政府推行的premix燃油补贴政策对渔业可持续性的实际影响。其创新之处在于将政策效果评估从宏观统计转向微观行为主体感知，揭示了补贴政策在基层执行中的复杂反馈机制，为发展中国家渔业补贴改革提供了基于实地经验的决策参考。
  1. A new genus and species of hagfish from Vietnam: A transitional lineage between Rubicundinae and Myxininae（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究基于越南近海采集的两个标本，建立了盲鳗目（Myxiniformes）下的一个新属新种*Dolichostomus leucotaenia*。其创新性体现在两个层面：一是该物种具有细长椭圆形口裂等此前未见于盲鳗目的独有形态特征；二是系统发育分析显示其处于Rubicundinae与Myxininae两个亚科之间的过渡位置，为理解盲鳗类群的演化谱系提供了关键中间环节的形态与分子证据。

Global introduction and impacts of alien freshwater fish
👤 Chen, Xing; Wang, Tao; He, Fengzhi 📅 2026-08-14
Many freshwater fish species, including carps, salmonids, and catfishes, have been widely introduced outside their native ranges. These introductions can lead to profound ecological consequences. Here, we assessed the environmental impacts of alien freshwater fish, including their influence on abiotic variables, native species, and ecosystem function in the introduced regions. Following the Extended Environmental Impact Classification for Alien Taxa (EEICAT) framework and associated metrics, we analyzed the impact severity and mechanisms of the records related to alien freshwater fish in the InvaPact database, which used large language models to extract and categorize environmental impacts posed by alien species. We have identified over 8000 records, covering hundreds of alien freshwater fish species observed in all continents except for Antarctic. Various negative impacts were posed on native species by alien freshwater fish through a wide range of mechanisms, with predation being the most frequently reported mechanism, followed by herbivory and competition. Moreover, more than half of these species contributed to population decline or even local extinction of at least one native species. In addition, alien freshwater fish also caused alteration of abiotic environment and fundamental ecosystem functions, leading to water quality degradation and modified food webs. Our results demonstrate the necessity of shifting from species-level monitoring toward multi-dimensional assessments that capture the complex impacts posed by alien species, which can contribute to the development of holistic management strategies.

Integrating behavioural ecology into invasion biology: insights from freshwater fishes in Mexico
👤 Camacho-Cervantes, Morelia 📅 2026-08-14
Biological invasions are a major driver of biodiversity loss, with impacts emerging from individual interactions to ecosystem-level processes. Here, we synthesise findings from multiple published experimental and field studies on freshwater fishes in Mexico to evaluate how behavioural mechanisms influence invasion dynamics and their ecological consequences. Across laboratory behavioural experiments, mesocosm studies, and field surveys involving native and invasive poeciliid and goodeid fishes, we quantified behavioural traits including heterospecific sociability and behavioural plasticity, and assessed their effects on invasion success, native species persistence, and community structure. Experimental studies showed that invasive fishes consistently altered social behaviour, habitat use, and reproductive interactions of native species, including species involved in reintroduction programmes. Field-based community and functional diversity analyses further revealed reductions in functional richness and increasing biotic homogenisation in systems exposed to multiple invasions. Moreover, global change drivers, including climate warming and chemical pollution, amplify these effects by modifying behavioural responses and interaction outcomes. Our findings highlight that incorporating behavioural mechanisms into invasion ecology frameworks is essential to predict invasion trajectories and to design effective management and mitigation strategies.

Temporal invasion regimes modulate stability in native riverine fish communities
👤 Rocha, Barbbara; Arranz, Ignasi; Saito, Victor 📅 2026-08-14
Temporal stability is a fundamental property of ecological communities because it reflects their capacity to maintain structure and functioning under environmental change. A key mechanism expected to support this stability is interspecific asynchrony, in which declines in some populations are compensated by increases in others, buffering fluctuations at the community level. In freshwater fish assemblages, stability may also be reflected in the temporal consistency of community size structure, an important dimension of community organization. Understanding how these processes respond to biological invasions is essential for identifying mechanisms that sustain native communities in impacted ecosystems. Here, we evaluated the effects of invasion pressure and its temporal variability on stability in native fish communities and tested how asynchrony contributes to stability under invasion. We hypothesized that: communities exposed to higher invasion pressure would exhibit lower stability, particularly when invasion regimes were more pulsed than chronic; and asynchronous population dynamics would enhance native community stability under invaded conditions. communities exposed to higher invasion pressure would exhibit lower stability, particularly when invasion regimes were more pulsed than chronic; and asynchronous population dynamics would enhance native community stability under invaded conditions. To test these hypotheses, we analyzed 270 freshwater fish community time series from French rivers, each monitored for 10 years. We used linear models to assess relationships between invasion metrics and two response variables: community stability and the coefficient of variation of size-spectrum slopes over time. Our results showed that sites with higher mean relative abundance of non-native fishes had lower stability and greater variability in community size structure. Moreover, the negative effects of invasion pressure were stronger under more variable invasion regimes, indicating that fluctuations in non-native abundance can amplify the destabilizing effects of sustained invasion pressure. In contrast, asynchrony was positively associated with community stability, indicating that compensatory dynamics among native populations can buffer invasion effects. Together, these findings suggest that non-native fishes erode the temporal stability of native assemblages, while asynchronous dynamics help buffer community fluctuations in impacted freshwater ecosystems.

Eastern European–Asian Cooperation: Aquacultural and Cultural Contributions to Increased Fish Consumption in Laos
👤 Békefi, Emese Bozánné; Lénárt, István; Markovina, Irina; Brl... 📅 2026-08-14
Laos is one of the 32 landlocked developing countries (LLDCs) of the world, where isolation from the sea and the lack of a large-scale fishing industry have a direct effect on fish supply and fish consumption. Per capita fish consumption in LLDCs amounted to 5.1 kg/year in 2023, which is much lower than the world average (20.3 kg) as well as the Developing Regions average (20.0 kg). However, per capita fish consumption in Laos has reached 28.5 kg/year in 2023, representing the highest among the 32 LLDCs. A disease trap for wild and farmed salmon in British Columbia, Canada
👤 Krkošek, Martin 📅 2026-08-14
Recent theory has shown that ecological and economic interactions between wild and farmed fish can result in unstable tipping points between a fishery-dominated versus an aquaculture-dominated coastal sea. In this Perspective, I argue that the essential features needed for such bistability are present for wild and farmed salmon in British Columbia (BC), Canada. Specifically, a disease trap is created by a feedback loop involving fisheries decline, incentives for aquaculture growth, and parasite transmission. Under these conditions, theory predicts that wild salmon populations can become trapped in a collapsed state by the pathogen community maintained in coastal seas by salmon farms. Importantly, it is not needed for pathogen transmission to be involved in the original cause of fishery collapse, which is considered to be multifactorial for wild salmon in BC. The theory is relevant to recovery efforts for wild salmon and policy on salmon aquaculture in British Columbia, for which a path can be charted that either entrenches or avoids a disease trap.

Community Governance and Environmental Education as Social and Institutional Dimensions of Sustainability-Oriented Artisanal Fisheries Management: A Socio-Ecological Assessment of a Coastal Lagoon in Mexico
👤 Moreno Mendoza, Enrique; Saldaña Almazán, Mirella; Bedolla S... 📅 2026-08-14
Small-scale fisheries are socio-ecological systems in which resource users interact with environmental conditions, fishing regulations, community initiatives, and institutional actors. Understanding how fishers perceive and report these interactions provides information on the social context of fisheries management, although such perceptions cannot substitute for independent assessments of ecological sustainability, behavioural compliance, or governance effectiveness. This study characterized fishers’ environmental perceptions, self-reported fishing practices, participation in environmental education and conservation activities, and perceptions of community and institutional involvement in fisheries management in Tecomate Lagoon, Guerrero, Mexico. A cross-sectional quantitative design was used, and structured questionnaires were administered to 120 active artisanal fishers selected through simple random sampling. Data were analyzed descriptively using frequencies and percentages. Respondents primarily associated the lagoon with economic (49.17%) and food-related (25.83%) functions. Declining water levels (60.00%) and reduced species abundance (40.00%) were the environmental changes reported by respondents. Regarding fishing practices, 94.17% stated that they complied with fishing regulations, and 100% reported releasing organisms below the minimum legal catch size. Environmental training was reported by 95.00% of respondents, while 94.17% reported participation in conservation-related activities. CONAPESCA (80.83%) and UAGro (19.17%) were identified as institutions involved in fisheries-related initiatives. These findings characterize the perceptions and self-reported experiences of surveyed fishers and should not be interpreted as evidence of ecological sustainability, actual behavioural compliance, educational effectiveness, or governance performance. The contribution of the study lies in systematically documenting the resource-user perspective as a component of the social dimension of the fishery. This information provides a descriptive baseline that can complement future ecological, behavioural, institutional, and longitudinal assessments of small-scale fisheries in coastal lagoon systems.

Illegal unreported and unregulated fishing in Somalia a systematic review of fisheries governance and sustainable development
👤 Ahmed, Abdikarim; Osman, Mohamed 📅 2026-08-15
Abstract Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing poses a significant threat to fisheries sustainability, maritime governance, and coastal livelihoods in fragile maritime states such as Somalia. This study systematically reviews the literature on IUU fishing in Somali waters to examine its governance context, drivers, impacts, maritime security implications, and governance responses. Following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines, a systematic search of Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar identified relevant literature published between January 2001 and March 2025. After screening and full-text assessment, 25 studies met the eligibility criteria and were included in the final evidence synthesis. The findings indicate that weak governance, limited enforcement capacity, economic incentives, and transnational regulatory gaps are consistently identified as the principal drivers of IUU fishing. The review identifies substantial socio-economic and environmental impacts, including declining fish stocks, biodiversity degradation, revenue losses, and livelihood insecurity. Although links between IUU fishing and piracy remain contested, the evidence suggests that both are better understood as outcomes of broader governance failures than as a direct causal relationship. The included studies consistently propose strengthening fisheries institutions, improving maritime enforcement, enhancing regional cooperation, and promoting community participation as key governance responses. The review also identifies important research gaps, particularly the limited availability of field-based research and long-term fisheries monitoring, providing an evidence base for future research and policy development in Somalia and other fragile maritime states.

Socio-economic drivers and governance failures in the artisanal exploitation of marine turtles along the Mogadishu and Adale Coasts, Somalia
👤 Mohamud, Abdinajib Ismail; Ali, Hassan Kasim; Mohamed, Moham... 📅 2026-08-14
Empirical data on artisanal marine turtle extraction in Somalia remains exceptionally scarce. This study documents the socio-economic drivers, operational methods, and local perceptions surrounding the illicit capture of marine turtles at artisanal landing sites along the Mogadishu and Adale coasts. Using purposive and snowball sampling, semi-structured interviews were conducted with 25 active artisanal fishermen and 5 institutional informants (N = 30). Respondents described extraction not as incidental bycatch, but a targeted, commercialised activity primarily impacting Green ( Chelonia mydas ) and vulnerable Loggerhead ( Caretta caretta ) turtles. The economic incentive is substantial, with intact turtles commanding up to 120 USD. Demand is heavily influenced by ethnobiological beliefs regarding turtle derivatives treating tuberculosis and asthma—uses unsupported by clinical evidence that expose consumers to chelonitoxism and heavy metal bioaccumulation risks. Reported frequencies are presented as counts of the 25 fishermen respondents with percentages and 95% confidence intervals, and represent recalled self-report from a small non-probability sample rather than verified catch records. Fishers primarily use large-mesh Ildheer gillnets in nearshore seagrass beds. Despite federal prohibitions under the Somali Fisheries Law, local enforcement at artisanal landing sites is functionally absent, mirroring constraints in Somalia’s industrial fisheries. These findings suggest that purely top-down enforcement is insufficient in this fragile governance context. 👤 Salte, Live Aareskjold; Lekang, Odd Ivar; Strauch, Sebastian... 📅 2026-08-14
Heterotrophic denitrification is increasingly used in land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RASs) to remove nitrate and minimise new-water demand, but the carbon sources traditionally used—methanol and acetic acid—raise safety, corrosion, and cost concerns. This study evaluated glycerol as an alternative external carbon source in a commercial land-based Atlantic salmon RAS in Norway. Trials in two identical post-smolt systems with integrated denitrification bioreactors compared glycerol (80%) against acetic acid for denitrification performance, nitrite accumulation, pH stability, carbon-source consumption, cost, and hazard profile.
