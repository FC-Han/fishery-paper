本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文2篇，研究主题较为分散，分别涉及渔业管理政策的社会经济维度与鱼类分类学的基础生物学研究。前者聚焦西非加纳的小规模渔业燃料补贴政策及其对渔业可持续性的影响，属于渔业管理与政策评估领域；后者则报道了越南海域发现的一个盲鳗新属新种，属于渔业资源生物学与分类学范畴。整体来看，本周研究数量较少，但体现了渔业科学中“管理实践”与“基础认知”并行的学科特点，前者关注人类活动与资源利用的平衡，后者则致力于完善物种多样性的基础图谱。

Global introduction and impacts of alien freshwater fish
👤 Chen, Xing; Wang, Tao; He, Fengzhi 📅 2026-08-14
Many freshwater fish species, including carps, salmonids, and catfishes, have been widely introduced outside their native ranges. These introductions can lead to profound ecological consequences. Here, we assessed the environmental impacts of alien freshwater fish, including their influence on abiotic variables, native species, and ecosystem function in the introduced regions. Following the Extended Environmental Impact Classification for Alien Taxa (EEICAT) framework and associated metrics, we analyzed the impact severity and mechanisms of the records related to alien freshwater fish in the InvaPact database, which used large language models to extract and categorize environmental impacts posed by alien species. We have identified over 8000 records, covering hundreds of alien freshwater fish species observed in all continents except for Antarctic. Various negative impacts were posed on native species by alien freshwater fish through a wide range of mechanisms, with predation being the most frequently reported mechanism, followed by herbivory and competition. Moreover, more than half of these species contributed to population decline or even local extinction of at least one native species. In addition, alien freshwater fish also caused alteration of abiotic environment and fundamental ecosystem functions, leading to water quality degradation and modified food webs. Our results demonstrate the necessity of shifting from species-level monitoring toward multi-dimensional assessments that capture the complex impacts posed by alien species, which can contribute to the development of holistic management strategies.

Integrating behavioural ecology into invasion biology: insights from freshwater fishes in Mexico
👤 Camacho-Cervantes, Morelia 📅 2026-08-14
Biological invasions are a major driver of biodiversity loss, with impacts emerging from individual interactions to ecosystem-level processes. Here, we synthesise findings from multiple published experimental and field studies on freshwater fishes in Mexico to evaluate how behavioural mechanisms influence invasion dynamics and their ecological consequences. Across laboratory behavioural experiments, mesocosm studies, and field surveys involving native and invasive poeciliid and goodeid fishes, we quantified behavioural traits including heterospecific sociability and behavioural plasticity, and assessed their effects on invasion success, native species persistence, and community structure. Experimental studies showed that invasive fishes consistently altered social behaviour, habitat use, and reproductive interactions of native species, including species involved in reintroduction programmes. Field-based community and functional diversity analyses further revealed reductions in functional richness and increasing biotic homogenisation in systems exposed to multiple invasions. Moreover, global change drivers, including climate warming and chemical pollution, amplify these effects by modifying behavioural responses and interaction outcomes. Our findings highlight that incorporating behavioural mechanisms into invasion ecology frameworks is essential to predict invasion trajectories and to design effective management and mitigation strategies.

Temporal invasion regimes modulate stability in native riverine fish communities
👤 Rocha, Barbbara; Arranz, Ignasi; Saito, Victor 📅 2026-08-14
Temporal stability is a fundamental property of ecological communities because it reflects their capacity to maintain structure and functioning under environmental change. A key mechanism expected to support this stability is interspecific asynchrony, in which declines in some populations are compensated by increases in others, buffering fluctuations at the community level. In freshwater fish assemblages, stability may also be reflected in the temporal consistency of community size structure, an important dimension of community organization. Understanding how these processes respond to biological invasions is essential for identifying mechanisms that sustain native communities in impacted ecosystems. Here, we evaluated the effects of invasion pressure and its temporal variability on stability in native fish communities and tested how asynchrony contributes to stability under invasion. We hypothesized that: communities exposed to higher invasion pressure would exhibit lower stability, particularly when invasion regimes were more pulsed than chronic; and asynchronous population dynamics would enhance native community stability under invaded conditions. communities exposed to higher invasion pressure would exhibit lower stability, particularly when invasion regimes were more pulsed than chronic; and asynchronous population dynamics would enhance native community stability under invaded conditions. To test these hypotheses, we analyzed 270 freshwater fish community time series from French rivers, each monitored for 10 years. We used linear models to assess relationships between invasion metrics and two response variables: community stability and the coefficient of variation of size-spectrum slopes over time. Our results showed that sites with higher mean relative abundance of non-native fishes had lower stability and greater variability in community size structure. Moreover, the negative effects of invasion pressure were stronger under more variable invasion regimes, indicating that fluctuations in non-native abundance can amplify the destabilizing effects of sustained invasion pressure. In contrast, asynchrony was positively associated with community stability, indicating that compensatory dynamics among native populations can buffer invasion effects. Together, these findings suggest that non-native fishes erode the temporal stability of native assemblages, while asynchronous dynamics help buffer community fluctuations in impacted freshwater ecosystems.

Parasite diversity and community structure in invasive Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) in aquaculture systems of the Upper Tana River, Kenya
👤 Shigoley, Miriam; Antoine-Moussiaux, Nicolas; Jauniaux, Thie... 📅 2026-08-12
Aquaculture systems can influence how hosts and parasites interact, particularly when using widely introduced species such as Nile tilapia ( Oreochromis niloticus ). Although this species is farmed globally and hosts many parasite species, scientific understanding of the effects they have on fish, the environment and humans remains fragmented. In the Upper Tana River region of Kenya, O. niloticus is both a dominant aquaculture species and a well-established non-native fish. However, parasite communities in farmed systems in this region are still poorly described. This study investigated parasite diversity, community structure, the role of environmental conditions and production systems across small scale and large scale tilapia farms. We examined 157 fish for both internal and external parasites. We found diverse parasite taxa including monopisthocotylans, digeneans, copepods, nematodes, and acanthocephalans. Monopisthocotylans were the most prevalent taxa with high infection intensities. Parasite diversity differed significantly between farm types, with higher richness (p = 0.0118), Shannon diversity (p = 0.010), and Simpson diversity (p = 0.0152) in large-scale farms. Parasite community composition also differed between farm types (PERMANOVA: F = 3.22, R² = 0.21, p = 0.033). In contrast, environmental variables (temperature, pH, conductivity, and total dissolved solids) showed weak associations with parasite infection metrics. Histopathological analyses showed that infected fish had significantly higher gill lesion severity than non-infected individuals (p = 0.002). Lesion severity increased with parasite species richness and was generally higher in fish from large scale farms. These results suggest that production system characteristics play a stronger role than measured water quality variables in shaping parasite communities of farmed O. niloticus . They also show that parasite infections can affect fish health while remaining largely overlooked in farm management. Improving fish health therefore requires routine parasite monitoring and closer integration of ecological knowledge into farm-level practices. This is especially important in widely translocated, invasive species such as Nile tilapia, as baseline knowledge of the parasitological aspects of aquaculture-environment interactions is needed to assess the risks of parasite transmission between invasive and native species.

👤 Oparinde, Lawrence Olusola; Hess, Sebastian 📅 2026-08-12
ABSTRACT Climate change is having a devastating impact on artisanal fisheries communities in developing countries, including Nigeria. In Nigeria, multiple local conflicts have arisen as a result of climate‐related changes in coastal regions. This study investigates the impact of cooperatives and climate change adaptation on conflicts among artisanal fisherfolk in Nigeria. To capture climate change adaptation complexity, we employed the number of climate change adaptation strategies adopted as a count variable. We applied Endogenous Treatment Poisson Regression and Unconditional Quantile Regression models to analyze cross‐sectional data collected from artisanal fisherfolk in the coastal area of Nigeria. Findings from this research revealed that non‐fishing strategies are equally important as part of the climate change adaptation strategies with 43%, 22%, and 14% of the respondents adopting livelihood diversification, a community conflict resolution committee, and formation of local security outfits (vigilante), respectively. The results further showed that cooperatives play a significant role in increasing the number of climate change adaptation strategies adopted. Also, the study empirically established that cooperatives lessen the incidence, especially of the most severe conflicts among artisanal fisherfolk. Therefore, policy efforts should be geared towards strengthening and developing cooperative societies among artisanal fisherfolk in order to strengthen climate adaptation and reduce conflicts.

Evaluating Glycerol as an Alternative Carbon Source for Denitrification in Land-Based Salmon Recirculating Aquaculture Systems
👤 Salte, Live Aareskjold; Lekang, Odd Ivar; Strauch, Sebastian... 📅 2026-08-14
Heterotrophic denitrification is increasingly used in land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RASs) to remove nitrate and minimise new-water demand, but the carbon sources traditionally used—methanol and acetic acid—raise safety, corrosion, and cost concerns. This study evaluated glycerol as an alternative external carbon source in a commercial land-based Atlantic salmon RAS in Norway. Trials in two identical post-smolt systems with integrated denitrification bioreactors compared glycerol (80%) against acetic acid for denitrification performance, nitrite accumulation, pH stability, carbon-source consumption, cost, and hazard profile. Glycerol achieved similar or higher apparent total dissolved nitrogen (TDN) removal than acetic acid and, once the biofilm had acclimated, was completely consumed. It maintained a stable outlet pH (≥6.5), avoiding the enzyme inhibition seen when acetic acid depressed pH, and proved about 1.5 times more cost-effective (≈NOK 66 vs. 97 per kg TDN removed). A standardised hazard assessment ranked glycerol as the safest option for flammability, toxicity, and corrosiveness. TDN removal correlated strongly with dissolved nitrogen gas saturation at the bioreactor outlet (r2 = 0.79–0.82), indicating that total gas pressure monitoring is a promising, low-cost proxy for performance. Nitrite accumulation per unit of nitrogen removed was, however, about three times higher under glycerol (≈1 mg NO2-N per mg TDN) than under acetic acid (≈0.3 mg NO2-N per mg TDN), and the nine-day glycerol phase was too short to establish whether this resolves or persists as the biofilm matures. Subject to routine nitrite monitoring in the fish tanks, glycerol is a cost-effective, low-hazard, and pH-stable alternative to acetic acid for nitrate removal in salmon RASs.

Study on the Measurement and Enhancement Pathways of Ecological Efficiency of Marine Fisheries in China’s Coastal Areas
👤 Zhang, Xueqi; Zhu, Siyan 📅 2026-08-13
Marine fisheries play a vital role in ensuring food supply and sustaining livelihoods in coastal regions of China. However, the expansion of aquaculture has led to increasing carbon emissions and mounting pressure on resources and the environment, making the improvement of ecological efficiency a critical issue for the sustainable development of the industry. From the perspective of carbon emissions as undesirable output, this paper employs the DEA-SBM model and the GML index to measure the ecological efficiency of marine fisheries across nine coastal provinces in China from 2006 to 2023. Furthermore, using fixed-effects models, mediation-effect models, and grouped regression models, this study empirically examines the impacts of fishermen’s income and environmental regulations on the ecological efficiency of marine fisheries and their transmission mechanisms. The results indicate that ecological efficiency exhibits fluctuating trends across provinces, with significant inter-provincial disparities. Fishermen’s income has a significant positive effect on ecological efficiency, while environmental regulations show a significant negative effect. Digitalization level significantly promotes ecological efficiency, whereas fishery disaster losses significantly inhibit it. Technological adoption intention plays a partial mediating role in the pathways through which both fishermen’s income and environmental regulations affect ecological efficiency. Significant regional heterogeneity is observed, with the eastern coastal region exhibiting the strongest effects of various factors and the northern coastal region showing the weakest. Accordingly, this paper proposes differentiated enhancement pathways for ecological efficiency from four dimensions, including technological innovation-driven development, industrial structure optimization, environmental policy regulation, and regional coordinated governance, with the aim of providing theoretical foundations and policy references for the low-carbon transformation and sustainable development of marine fisheries in China’s coastal areas.
