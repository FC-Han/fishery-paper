可持续渔业与水产健康：多学科融合研究前沿

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文2篇，研究主题较为分散，分别涉及渔业管理政策的社会经济维度与鱼类分类学的基础生物学研究。前者聚焦西非加纳的小规模渔业燃料补贴政策及其对渔业可持续性的影响，属于渔业管理与政策评估领域；后者则报道了越南海域发现的一个盲鳗新属新种，属于渔业资源生物学与分类学范畴。整体来看，本周研究数量较少，但体现了渔业科学中“管理实践”与“基础认知”并行的学科特点，前者关注人类活动与资源利用的平衡，后者则致力于完善物种多样性的基础图谱。

高影响力期刊亮点

创新性研究

  1. Small‐Scale Fisherfolk Perspectives on Premix Fuel Subsidies and Fisheries Sustainability in Ghana（*Natural Resources Forum*）— 该研究从小型渔民主体视角切入，评估加纳政府推行的premix燃油补贴政策对渔业可持续性的实际影响。其创新之处在于将政策效果评估从宏观统计转向微观行为主体感知，揭示了补贴政策在基层执行中的复杂反馈机制，为发展中国家渔业补贴改革提供了基于实地经验的决策参考。
  1. A new genus and species of hagfish from Vietnam: A transitional lineage between Rubicundinae and Myxininae（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究基于越南近海采集的两个标本，建立了盲鳗目（Myxiniformes）下的一个新属新种*Dolichostomus leucotaenia*。其创新性体现在两个层面：一是该物种具有细长椭圆形口裂等此前未见于盲鳗目的独有形态特征；二是系统发育分析显示其处于Rubicundinae与Myxininae两个亚科之间的过渡位置，为理解盲鳗类群的演化谱系提供了关键中间环节的形态与分子证据。

Evolutionary diversification of pelagic larval duration in three reef fish families
👤 Huet, Emmanuele; Gillet, Amandine; Philippart, Claire; Lecch... 📅 2026-08-10
In many animal taxa, studies of trait diversification have mainly focused on adult phenotypes, while early life traits have been poorly studied from a macroevolutionary perspective. Reef fishes provide a good system for studying the evolution of animals with complex life cycles. Their biphasic life cycle – a pelagic larval stage and reef-associated juvenile/adult stages – and the fact that they are among the most diversified groups of vertebrates make them ideal candidates. This study aims to describe the patterns of diversification of an important larval trait, the pelagic larval duration (PLD), responsible for the dispersal ability of reef fishes in the ocean. Leveraging data available in the literature, we explored the diversification of PLD in three diverse families (Labridae, Pomacanthidae, and Pomacentridae). Our results show significant variation in PLD across the three fish families and across biogeographic regions, and reveal a strong phylogenetic signal in PLD. Our macroevolutionary analyses support that the tempo of PLD evolution is tightly associated with speciation events in Labridae and Pomacanthidae, sustaining the role of the pelagic larval phase in reef fish diversification. The hypothesis of a single optimal PLD value for all fishes – representing a trade-off between genetic mixing, colonisation of isolated reefs and mortality during the pelagic phase – was rejected by our comparisons of trait evolution models. Instead, we highlight multiple evolutionary shifts towards both short and long PLD durations. Finally, we reveal that PLD is linked to some adult ecological traits in Pomacanthidae but the same relationship is absent for Pomacentridae and Labridae. Our results strengthen the idea that PLD diversity is structured by phylogenetic relationships, and that the evolutionary dynamics of PLD are lineage-specific.

Widespread high prevalence of vaterite in pink salmon (Oncorhynchus gorbuscha) otoliths
👤 Pedersen, Frode; Berntsen, Henrik H; Thorstad, Eva B.; Skóra... 📅 2026-08-10
Pacific salmonids rely on precise sensory mechanisms, including the sagittal otolith, to support long-distance migrations. Vaterite replacement of otolith aragonite can impair sensory function, and compromise otolith-based reconstructions of environmental history. Otoliths from 1,116 native and invasive pink salmon across the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans were examined for vaterite prevalence, extent, and timing of formation. Visual assessment proved to be a reliable and efficient method for identifying otolith mineralogy, based on validation using X-ray diffraction. We document exceptionally high vaterite prevalence in pink salmon from both Atlantic (97%) and Pacific (94-96%) populations, with minimal geographic variation and no differences among native and invasive or wild and hatchery populations. Vaterite levels far exceeded those reported for other salmonids, including hatchery-reared populations. Our results indicate that vaterite formation can occur at any life stage, beginning as early as the juvenile freshwater stage and peaking early in the marine phase. Overall, our findings support an association between the unusually high prevalence of vaterite in pink salmon and a life-history strategy characterized by rapid growth.

Linear Alkylbenzenesulfonates in Polyurethane Soft Foam Products Drive Toxic Responses in Early Life Stage Fish
👤 He, Ying-Jie; Liao, Haolin; Wang, Jiazhen; Kang, Xinyuan; Ma... 📅 2026-08-10
Abstract Polyurethane soft foam (PSF), one of the most extensively used plastics in household products, presents both convenience and disposal challenges. The chemical makeup of PSF, particularly its released substances, remains unclear, as do the drivers of toxicity to aquatic organisms. Here, zebrafish (Danio rerio) were used as a model to evaluate the potential adverse effects of 47 PSF and similar-use products through swimming behavior and metabolomic profiling, and to identify the key toxic chemicals leached from these products by means of liquid chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry and correlation analysis. Among these, PSF leachates showed more severe adverse effects on larvae compared to latex and silicone leachates, with a higher number of chemical features and greater total abundance. Notably, linear alkylbenzenesulfonates (LAS), which accounted for nearly half of the chemicals significantly associated with toxicity, were predominantly found in PSF products. The concentrations of LAS in these products ranged from 5.6 to 4078.9 μg/g, with an average of 1015.6 μg/g. Of these, p-(3-dodecyl)benzenesulfonic acid was selected as the representative driver of LAS toxicity, which induced consistent behavioral and metabolic disruption in zebrafish similar to the effects observed with PSF leachates. This work suggests that LAS are potential adverse chemicals in PSF products and that their continued, widespread use raises concern for aquatic ecosystems.

The impacts of peracute underwater entrapment in fishing nets on marine mammal behaviour and welfare
👤 Dolman, Sarah; Barnett, James; Wessels, Mark 📅 2026-08-10
Fisheries interactions are widely recognised as being the leading anthropogenic cause of marine mammal mortality. One form of fisheries interaction is peracute underwater entrapment (PUE), the process of acute entanglement and forced submersion that occurs following interaction with fishing gear, where an individual is not able to break free or to reach the water’s surface to breathe. PUE results in a number of negative welfare impacts, including asphyxiation, injuries, physiological and psychological stress, social disruption and, ultimately, death impacting on the behaviour of affected individuals and conspecifics. Individually and collectively, the welfare implications of these events are severe and raise significant concerns. This review focuses on PUE and the known and hypothetical events associated with entrapment on the affected individual and on conspecifics including behaviour, the stress response and pathophysiology, the use of necropsy data to help provide a greater understanding of the events surrounding PUE and further research needed to improve our understanding of the welfare implications on individuals and populations. Case studies are provided for grey seal and common dolphin PUE cases in UK waters.

Tourism and coastal fisheries interaction: impacts and mechanism on social-ecological coastal ecosystems
👤 Ávila-Thieme, M. Isidora; Mora, Francisca; Ojeda, Danae; Rañ... 📅 2026-08-10
Tourism and coastal fisheries play key roles supporting livelihoods and shaping local development in coastal areas. Balancing social-economic benefits with ecological sustainability remains a significant challenge. Overexploitation of marine resources is one of the main drivers of biodiversity loss in marine ecosystems, and tourism has been proposed as a sustainable alternative to fisheries by supporting conservation goals while maintaining local livelihoods. Yet the direct influence of tourism on fishing pressure remains unclear, as most studies are highly context-specific, and provide limited generalizable insights. We conducted a scoping review of 317 articles to characterize the interaction between tourism and fisheries. Then, we selected a subset of 124 articles to explore the impacts and mechanisms underlying tourism–fisheries interactions. Research in the topic has increased over time, with most articles concentrated in ecology then fisheries and ocean journals. Most articles address both social and ecological dimensions. Only a small subset of studies explicitly evaluate the effects of tourism on fishing pressure. The effects of tourism on fishing depend on who participates in tourism, how livelihoods are reconfigured, and whether governance aligns tourism with fisheries management. Tourism could increase fishing pressure when driven by external and unconscious actors, leading to unsustainable demand for seafood and high-value species. Conversely, tourism could reduce fishing pressure when tourism engages with sustainable demand, or when fishers shift partially or entirely to tourism, decreasing time allocated to fishing or through the establishment of conservation areas. Sustainable marine coastal ecosystems require actively linking tourism development to fishing across governance and livelihood context.

Sex-related differences in the lipid and micronutrient profile, but not the proximate composition, of the Mediterranean mussel Mytilus galloprovincialis: a pilot study with implications for sex-targeted aquaculture
👤 Danesi, Francesca; D’Aniello, Enrico; Cappello, Tiziana; San... 📅 2026-08-10
Bivalves are a sustainable, low-carbon-footprint source of high-quality animal protein and polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA), particularly the long-chain (LC) n-3 forms eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). The Mediterranean mussel ( Mytilus galloprovincialis ) is among the most farmed and consumed bivalves in Europe, yet the role of sex in its nutritional profile at marketed-product level is poorly documented. M. galloprovincialis shows skewed sex-biased progenies, possibly influencing product nutrient content. In this pilot study, pooled soft tissues of farmed adults purchased as human food were sex-sorted into a female (F) and a male (M) pool (60 animals each) and analyzed for proximate composition, fatty acid profile, minerals, and vitamin B 12 . Data were summarized as absolute and relative differences and as log 2 (F/M). Moisture, protein, ash, carbohydrates, and energy were nearly superimposable between sexes. In contrast, the female pool has 25.6% more total lipids and ~five-fold higher PUFA fraction, driven by an order-of-magnitude higher EPA and DHA; total n-3 fatty acids were nearly six-fold higher, the n-3/n-6 ratio was more than twice, and EPA+DHA was more than ten times higher than in the male pool. Vitamin B 12 , iron, selenium, and iodine were higher in the female pool, whereas sodium and zinc were higher in the male pool. These data suggest that sex-targeted aquaculture could be explored as a route to differentiated, n-3 LC-PUFA and vitamin B 12 -enriched mussel products, providing a quantitative basis for properly replicated, season-resolved follow-up studies, and for breeding programs aimed at strengthening the nutritional and socio-economic value of mussel farming.

Fish Assemblages in an Urbanized Neotropical Basin: Influence of Environmental Factors Across Dry and Rainy Periods
👤 Alves, Natalia S.; Cavallari, Daiane E.; Smith, Welber S. 📅 2026-08-10
ABSTRACT This study investigated the effects of season and environmental disturbances on fish communities in neotropical rivers of the Sorocaba basin, a Sub‐Basin of the Paraná River in Southeast Brazil, based on sampling 1196 individuals from 49 species, including two non‐native and one threatened with extinction. Greater richness and abundance were recorded in the rainy season, but the community structure remained stable between periods, indicating resilience to climatic variations. The rivers showed marked differences: the Sorocaba River stood out for its high diversity and low dominance, while the Una River exhibited the lowest richness and highest dominance, reflecting a gradient of anthropogenic impact. Variables such as riparian vegetation, fast flow, and substrates exerted a greater influence on species distribution than seasonality. Larger rivers supported greater abundance, but habitat degradation, especially the loss of riparian vegetation, threatens local biodiversity. Despite advances in sanitation, problems such as pollution and the presence of invasive species persist. Conservation should prioritize protecting riparian vegetation, maintaining habitats with fast flow and diverse substrates, and managing non‐native species. The observed stability is maintained by generalist species and preserved riparian forest stretches, yet anthropogenic pressure remains the main risk to the fish fauna. Integrated management measures are urgently needed to reconcile anthropogenic use of water resources and the preservation of these neotropical rivers.

A new genus and species of hagfish from Vietnam: A transitional lineage between Rubicundinae and Myxininae (Craniata: Myxiniformes)
👤 Mok, Hin‐Kiu; Su, Yo; van Vo, Quang; Ho, Hsuan‐Ching 📅 2026-08-10
Abstract Dolichostomus leucotaenia gen. et sp. nov. is described from two specimens collected from off northern Vietnam. It is characterized by a long, oval mouth opening visible from the ventral view, a feature unique among the order Myxiniformes. It also possesses a single medial nasal‐sinus papilla and branchial slime pores positioned external to the gill‐aperture series. Moreover, it can be further distinguished by its slender body, a distinct white mid‐dorsal stripe, five pairs of gill apertures arranged in a straight line, and ventral aorta not bifurcated.
