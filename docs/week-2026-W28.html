本周研究概览

本周（2026-W28）共收录8篇渔业相关论文，研究主题涵盖渔业资源评估、水产养殖技术、鱼类生态学与生理学、以及渔业管理等多个方向。整体趋势表现为：一是人工智能与机器学习技术在渔业中的应用持续深化，从鱼体重量预测到鲸鲨识别追踪，再到渔业产量预报，体现了数据驱动方法对传统渔业研究的赋能；二是气候变化对渔业的影响及其归因分析成为热点，研究者开始将气候因子直接纳入资源评估模型；三是鱼类基础生物学研究依然活跃，涉及长寿机制、繁殖生物学和食物网结构等经典议题。

本周暂无发表在Nature、Science、PNAS等顶级综合期刊或Nature子刊上的论文。

创新性研究

  1. Climate Attribution in Fishery Stock Assessment: Case Studies From the Northeast U.S. Groundfish Stocks（*Fish and Fisheries*）— 该研究首次系统地将气候归因方法整合到渔业资源评估框架中，通过美国东北部底层鱼类案例展示了如何量化气候变化对种群动态的贡献。其创新之处在于突破了传统资源评估中仅考虑捕捞压力的局限，为气候适应性渔业管理提供了可操作的方法论。
  1. Explainable AI-Driven Machine Learning for Forecasting Marine Fisheries Production Using Environmental Predictors（*Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction*）— 该研究将可解释人工智能（XAI）引入渔业产量预测，不仅提升了预报精度，还揭示了环境因子对产量的非线性影响机制。其创新点在于克服了传统“黑箱”机器学习模型的可解释性缺陷，为区域渔业管理提供了透明、可信的决策支持工具。
  1. Potential factors contributing to extreme longevity in the Greenland shark（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究系统探讨了格陵兰鲨（*Somniosus microcephalus*）超长寿命（>400年）的潜在机制，从代谢率、DNA修复能力、抗氧化防御等多个维度提出假说。其创新性在于整合了比较基因组学与生理生态学视角，为理解脊椎动物极端长寿的进化机制提供了新线索。
  1. Egg and larval production of Eastern Baltic cod in captivity over one spawning season（*Journal of Fish Biology*）— 该研究首次在人工养殖条件下完整记录了东波罗的海鳕鱼（*Gadus morhua*）整个产卵季节的卵和幼体产量。其创新之处在于通过控制实验揭示了亲本大小与体况对繁殖成功率的直接影响，为解释该种群持续衰退的机制提供了关键实验证据。

推荐阅读

  1. Climate Attribution in Fishery Stock Assessment: Case Studies From the Northeast U.S. Groundfish Stocks — 开创性地将气候归因纳入资源评估，是气候适应性渔业管理的必读文献。
  2. Explainable AI-Driven Machine Learning for Forecasting Marine Fisheries Production Using Environmental Predictors — 展示了可解释AI在渔业预测中的前沿应用，方法新颖且实用价值高。
  3. Potential factors contributing to extreme longevity in the Greenland shark — 系统综述了脊椎动物最长寿物种的潜在机制，对理解衰老生物学具有跨学科意义。
  4. Automated whale shark recognition and tracking using drones and deep learning — 结合无人机与深度学习实现了濒危物种的非侵入性监测，技术路线具有推广价值。
  5. Seasonal Hydrology Restructures Basal Carbon Pathways in a Lower Yangtze River Fish Food Web: A Stable-Isotope Baseline for the Fishing-Ban Era — 为长江十年禁渔效果评估提供了关键的食物网基线数据，具有重要的管理参考价值。

Spatial and Temporal Variation in Habitat Use and Feeding Ecology Across Ontogeny of an Invasive Fish
👤 Zarini, Sina; Balshine, Sigal 📅 2026-07-12
The early life stage of animals shapes later survival and fitness, yet this period remains poorly understood for many fish species. We examined how early juvenile round gobies Neogobius melanostomus, a highly invasive benthic fish, use habitat and food resources during development. We quantified spatial and temporal distribution patterns of early juveniles (<25 mm standard length, SL) through field sampling and then analysed diet composition and feeding-related morphology across size classes in the laboratory. We documented a developmental progression involving shifts in habitat use, foraging behaviour, and functional morphology with smaller juveniles (10.79 ± 0.32 mm SL) using pelagic zones and surface waters at night and consuming a broad array of zooplankton prey. Larger juveniles (23.62 ± 0.23 mm SL) increasingly moved to benthic habitats and a more specialized diet comprised in part by benthic macroinvertebrates. Morphological shifts in mouth and eye position aligned with this transition toward benthic foraging. Together, these results reveal integrated behavioural, morphological, and trophic changes during early development that likely reduce intraspecific competition and enhance establishment success in novel environments.

Impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on fish consumption and sales in Muğla province: An evaluation for future outbreaks
👤 Metin Hacisa, Cansu; Şahin, Ekrem; Alparslan, Yunus 📅 2026-07-12
This study aimed to examine the changes in fish consumption preferences and fish sales in Muğla province before and after the COVID-19 pandemic. In this context, a survey was conducted with 302 participants consisting of consumers, fish markets, and fish restaurants operating in Muğla province. The questionnaires were administered both face-to-face and online. By analyzing the collected data, a general assessment of fish consumption and sales in the Muğla region during the pre- and post-pandemic periods was carried out. The findings revealed that the pandemic process affected fish consumption and sales patterns primarily through economic conditions, accessibility, and consumer preferences. Overall fish consumption decreased from 86% before the pandemic to 81% after the pandemic, while the proportion of non-consumers increased from 14% to 19%. Although there was an increased tendency toward durable products, a shift in dietary behavior was observed, including changes in meat consumption preferences and supplement use patterns. No significant change was observed in working arrangements, as 75–78% of participants continued working in offices before and after the pandemic. Despite the recognized importance of fish consumption for healthy nutrition, price and accessibility emerged as the main determining factors affecting consumption behavior, with price concerns increasing up to 38% and access difficulties up to 20% in the post-pandemic period. Fish markets remained the dominant purchasing location (approximately 48–51%), while fresh fish continued to be the most preferred product type (>93%) in both periods. Moreover, seafood businesses were significantly affected, as 63% reported that demand was met before the pandemic, whereas this rate dropped to 33% after the pandemic. Half of the enterprises reported a decrease in sales and customer numbers, and 76% indicated that their economic situation worsened. In conclusion, the study highlights the need to further develop fish consumption culture. It also emphasizes the importance of sustainable supply chains, hygiene practices, and consumer awareness during extraordinary circumstances, providing guidance for managing similar crises in the future.

IOT Based Bio-Floc Water Quality Optimization System for Sustainable Shrimp Aquaculture
👤 V, Muralidharan; P, Munipandeeswaran; Jenifer A, Agesta 📅 2026-07-12
Abstract Bio-floc technology (BFT) has emerged as a water-efficient and biosecure alternative to conventional pond-based shrimp and fish culture, relying on the in-situ conversion of nitrogenous waste into microbial protein through controlled carbon-to-nitrogen (C:N) ratio management. However, the productivity and stability of bio-floc systems depend on the continuous maintenance of narrow water quality bands for dissolved oxygen, pH, un-ionized ammonia, nitrite, alkalinity, and floc volume, a task that is difficult to sustain through manual testing alone, particularly in small and medium-scale coastal farms. This paper presents an IoT-Based Bio-Floc Water Quality Optimization System (IoT-BWQOS) that combines a low-power wireless sensor network, edge-level anomaly filtering, a cloud-hosted Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) forecasting model, and an automated actuation layer for aerators, paddle-wheels, and probiotic/carbon dosing pumps. The system was piloted on twelve bio-floc tanks (each of 30 m³ capacity) rearing Pacific white shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) at a coastal farm cluster in Nagapattinam district, Tamil Nadu, over a single 110-day culture cycle. Field results indicate a water quality parameter forecasting accuracy of 94.6%, an ammonia-spike early warning lead time of 4–9 hours, a 27% reduction in freshwater/make-up water usage, an 18% improvement in feed conversion ratio, and a 12.4% increase in shrimp survival rate compared to the farm's conventional manual-testing baseline from the preceding culture cycle. Keywords — Internet of Things, Bio-Floc Technology, Water Quality Monitoring, Aquaculture 4.0, LSTM, LoRaWAN, Shrimp Farming, Smart Sensors, Precision Aquaculture, Edge Computing.
