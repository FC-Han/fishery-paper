渔业资源监测与可持续养殖技术进展

本周研究概览

本周共收录6篇渔业相关论文，研究覆盖鱼类多样性保护、渔业资源评估与管理、鱼类分类学、渔业经济政策以及水产品加工与食品安全等多个方向。从研究区域看，非洲内陆水域（埃塞俄比亚Lake Tana、坦桑尼亚盐碱裂谷湖、尼日利亚渔业贸易）和南美洲（阿根廷Bermejo流域）成为本周的关注热点，反映出全球渔业研究对发展中国家内陆渔业可持续性的持续关切。方法学上，本周论文涵盖了传统形态分类与分子遗传学结合、时间序列计量经济分析、以及生物化学层面的污染物-蛋白互作机制等多种手段，整体呈现出渔业科学跨学科交叉的研究态势。

创新性研究

  1. Interaction Mechanism Between Grass Carp Fish Maw Collagen and Heavy-Metal Ions（*International Journal of Molecular Sciences*）

该研究首次系统表征了草鱼鱼鳔胶原蛋白（热溶性胶原HSC及另一组分）与常见重金属离子的结合行为与分子机制，填补了鱼鳔胶原-金属离子互作分子层面认识的空白。其创新点在于将水产品加工副产物的安全性问题与蛋白质-金属配位化学相结合，为鱼鳔类产品的重金属风险评估和脱除工艺开发提供了分子基础。

  1. Ageing imprecision compounds the effects of fishing selectivity, increasing bias in estimates of growth parameters（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新之处在于首次将 ageing error（年龄鉴定误差）与 fishing selectivity（捕捞选择性）的联合效应纳入生长参数估计偏差的量化框架，而非此前惯常的独立分析。这一发现对渔业资源评估中生长参数的可靠性具有直接影响，提示管理者在数据质量欠佳的情境下需重新审视基于长度频率的评估结论。

  1. A new species of *Pimelodella* from the upper Bermejo basin（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该文以形态学与遗传数据相结合的方式描述并命名了Bermejo流域上游的*Pimelodella*属新种，以椎骨数目（37–39）和短脂鳍基等特征与同属多数物种相区分。其意义在于丰富了La Plata basin鱼类区系记录，并为该属的系统分类与生物地理学研究提供了新的分类学依据。

  1. Fished to the Edge: Severe Overfishing Threatens Fish Stocks in Tanzania's Saline Rift Valley Lakes（*Fisheries Management and Ecology*）

该研究对坦桑尼亚Kitangiri和Singida两个盐碱裂谷湖的鱼类种群状况进行了系统评估，基于渔获量、物种组成和单位努力渔获量等指标揭示了过度捕捞的严重程度。其价值在于为数据匮乏的内陆小型渔业提供了可操作的资源状态诊断框架，对东非裂谷湖区渔业管理具有直接警示意义。

Impact of Selected Monetary Policy Instruments on Fish Trade Flow in Nigeria For The Period 1970-2024
👤 Jegede, R. O.; Coker, A. A. .A.; Baba, K. M.; Ojo, A. O. 📅 2026-11-16
Purpose: This study therefore examines the impact of selected MP instruments on FISTR flow in Nigeria over the period 1970–2024. Design/Methodology/Approach: The study adopts an ex-post facto research design and relies on annual time-series secondary data covering the period 1970–2024 in Nigeria. The dataset comprises 55 annual observations for the study variables, sourced from official publications of the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Statistical Bulletin and World Development Indicators (WDI) of the World Bank. The Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model is employed to estimate the short-run and long-run relationships among the variables. To assess the robustness of the ARDL estimates, Fully Modified Ordinary Least Squares (FMOLS), Dynamic Ordinary Least Squares (DOLS), and Canonical Cointegrating Regression (CCR) estimators are also employed. Research Limitation: The study focuses exclusively on five monetary policy variables, excluding other potentially relevant factors such as trade openness, fuel prices, fisheries infrastructure, government expenditure, climate variability and institutional quality. Findings: The ARDL bounds test confirms the existence of a long-run equilibrium relationship among the variables. The long-run estimates reveal that EXR and interest rate (INTR) significantly promote FISTR, whereas inflation (INF) and foreign reserves (LFREV) exert significant adverse effects, while money supply (MS) has a positive but insignificant influence. In the short run, EXR and INF initially depress FISTR, whereas INTR and MS stimulate trade after short lags. The error correction term indicates that approximately 74.2% of short-run disequilibrium is corrected annually. The FMOLS, DOLS and CCR estimators largely validate the ARDL results, confirming the robustness of the findings. Practical Implication: Directing monetary expansion towards productive fisheries financing and strategically utilising foreign reserves for infrastructure development can strengthen cold-chain facilities, storage, processing capacity, transportation, and export infrastructure. Social Implication: It will improve the well-being of fishing and processing communities and the affordability of fish as a source of animal protein. Originality/Value: Beyond methodological novelty, it demonstrates that fisheries trade performance is shaped not only by sector-specific factors but also by broader macroeconomic conditions. These findings provide new insights for designing monetary policies that support fisheries financing, trade competitiveness, and sustainable industry development.

Fish Species Diversity, Seasonal Dynamics and Land Use Land Cover Associations With Fish Assemblages in the Gulf of Lake Tana, Ethiopia
👤 Tsegaye, Birtukan; Mingist, Minwyelet; Getahun, Abebe; Mengi... 📅 2026-11-16
ABSTRACT Lake Tana, Ethiopia, harbours the world's only remaining cyprinid species flock, making it globally significant for conservation. Yet, these resources face mounting anthropogenic pressures from overfishing and land use changes. We assessed fish assemblage structure and seasonal dynamics in the Gulf of Lake Tana from September 2024 to August 2025 and land use land cover (LULC) changes from 2000 to 2025 using Landsat and Sentinel imagery. Fish specimens were sampled monthly from fishers at landing sites employing gillnets of varying mesh sizes. The specimens were identified to species level and analysed using Shannon diversity ( H ′), evenness ( J ), dominance ( C ) and Index of Relative Importance (%IRI). Exploratory multivariate analyses (non‐metric multidimensional scaling [NMDS] and principal component analysis [PCA] of four seasonal aggregates) were used to describe seasonal assemblage patterns. A total of 2764 specimens representing 18 species were recorded, dominated by endemic Labeobarbus spp. (81.8%), followed by Oreochromis niloticus (11.9%) and Clarias gariepinus (6.3%). Diversity was moderate ( H ′ = 2.60) and differed significantly among four hydrological seasons (individual‐based permutation test, 9999 permutations: p = 0.0024), being highest in the dry season ( H ′ = 2.596). After Bonferroni correction, dry‐season H ′ differed significantly from pre‐rainy (Hutcheson t ‐test, t = 3.84, adjusted p = 0.0006) and post‐rainy H ′ ( p = 0.036), but not from rainy‐season H ′ (adjusted p = 0.066). Evenness was high in all seasons ( J = 0.87–0.95). Sex ratios were female‐biased in the catches of 12 of 18 species ( χ 2 , p < 0.001). Between 2000 and 2025, cropland and settlements expanded to nearly 60% of the mapped area, whereas wetlands declined by 92.7%. Because, fish data cover a single year and no environmental covariates were measured, these LULC trends are presented only as landscape context that is associated with the current assemblage composition. The findings provide baseline data for further studies, testing these associations directly, and support the need for watershed rehabilitation, buffer zone protection and sustained fisheries monitoring to protect the lake's globally unique cyprinid flock.

Fish Species Composition, Relative Abundance and Diversity of Nworie River, Imo State, Nigeria
👤 Ukagwu, John I.; Nwosu, Chijioke Ralph; Nkulo, Uzoma U.; Eze... 📅 2026-11-16
This study assessed fish species composition, relative abundance and diversity in Nworie River, Imo State, Nigeria, using fish-catch records obtained between January and August 2026. The assessment aimed to establish a baseline for subsequent biodiversity monitoring and fisheries management. Records were drawn from three sampling stations representing the upstream (A), midstream (B) and downstream (C) sections of the river. Specimens were collected fortnightly using a gill net and a dugout canoe, and records were pooled across stations. Species abundance and relative abundance were summarised descriptively, while Simpson’s dominance, diversity and reciprocal indices were calculated to characterise the recorded assemblage. The dataset comprised 1,652 fish specimens assigned to 12 species across four families. Cichlidae accounted for 80.93% of the total catch. Haemichromis fasciatus was the most frequently recorded species (255 specimens; 15.44%), followed by Pelmatolapia marae (239; 14.47%), Oreochromis aureus (209; 12.65%), Sarotherodon galilaeus (187; 11.32%) and Oreochromis niloticus (179; 10.84%). Together, these five-species represented 64.71% of all recorded individuals. Haemichromis elongartus was the least abundant recorded species (13; 0.79%). Simpson’s dominance, diversity and reciprocal indices were 0.1075, 0.8925 and 9.30, respectively. The findings indicate unequal numerical representation among the recorded species and provide an initial fish assemblage inventory for Nworie River. Because the analysis concerns pooled catch records, the estimates should not be interpreted as absolute population abundances. Continued taxonomic verification and effort-standardised monitoring are recommended.

Atlantic salmon marine survival and distribution inferred from six decades of scale-derived growth: temporal trends and growth consistency among sea ages
👤 Breau, Cindy; Chaput, Gérald J; Friedland, Kevin D. 📅 2026-11-16
Across its range, the survival and maturation dynamics of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) are dependent on growth, energy reserves, and predation pressure. This study examined growth of one-sea-winter (1SW) and two-sea-winter (2SW) salmon using archived scales collected from the Miramichi River, Canada, spanning 59 smolt migration years (1958-2016). Fork length and nearly all scale-derived growth metrics exhibited significant long-term linear trends, however, no evidence was found for critical periods of reduced growth. Hence, growth during the post-smolt year showed no declines corresponding to periods of reduced marine survival, suggesting that variation in marine recruitment of North American Atlantic Salmon are not dependent on post-smolt growth. Growth patterns recorded on scales, including intercirculi spacing, were broadly similar between sea-age groups until the end of the first winter at sea, suggesting shared migration routes and overlapping marine distribution during this period. By identifying temporal shifts in growth and critical phases of growth similarity and divergence, this study improves understanding of salmon responses to environmental variability and refines inferences about the marine distribution of salmon from individual populations.

Hierarchical Multi-Objective Dispatch of a Multi-Use Floating Offshore Wind–Hydrogen–Aquaculture System with OpenFAST-Informed Environmental Modeling
👤 Lyu, Mao; Fu, Chunxiang; Zhou, Xiong; He, Huaijie; Yang, Lin... 📅 2026-11-17
Co-locating hydrogen production and aquaculture on floating offshore wind platforms requires dispatch policies that jointly optimize energy trading, electrolyzer longevity, and cage water quality—objectives that scalarized reinforcement learning struggles to reconcile. We develop a conservation-closed reduced-order model of a 20 MW-class floating wind system, driven by ERA5 North Sea reanalysis and cross-checked against OpenFAST, and cast dispatch as a Markov decision process. Scalarized twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) agents achieved nominally higher revenue than rule-based control (+0.033 M€; Hedges’ g = 0.94) but converged to a degenerate, hydrogen-abandoning equilibrium in 7% of independent runs (2/27). A hierarchical TD3 agent (H-TD3) with an annealed logarithmic Nash-bargaining reward showed no collapse across 20 seeds (Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.32), reached 95% of its final physical-objective level at the earliest observable episode in every seed, and exhibited an order-of-magnitude smaller plateau dispersion than scalarized baselines, with revenue statistically indistinguishable from rule-based control (1.216 ± 0.040 M€). Over 1000 h, H-TD3 increased cage biomass by 22.6% and yielded an electrolyzer-subsystem hydrogen cost of €3.05/kg (excluding electricity, wind-farm, and aquaculture costs), or €6.45/kg at €60/MWh wind electricity. These results frame multi-objective offshore dispatch as a risk-control problem: Nash-bargaining rewards produced fewer collapses than linear scalarization, though the difference was not significant.

Comparative Performance of Oxford Nanopore Duplex and Simplex 16S rRNA Reads for eDNA ‐Based Prediction of Salmon Aquaculture Benthic Impacts
👤 Stoeck, Thorsten; Damm, Lukas; Breiner, Hans‐Werner; Katzenm... 📅 2026-11-17
ABSTRACT Environmental monitoring of salmon aquaculture increasingly uses eDNA‐based bacterial community profiling, yet the practical value of Oxford Nanopore duplex sequencing remains uncertain due to low yield. We compared full‐length 16S rRNA simplex and duplex Nanopore reads for predicting benthic environmental quality (EQ) at 15 Scottish salmon farms. After stringent Q30‐filtering, samples contained far more high‐quality simplex than duplex reads. Random Forest models trained on simplex data perfectly classified samples as above or below the good‐moderate EQ boundary (accuracy 100%, κ = 1), while duplex models reached 97.6% (κ = 0.95). For four‐class EQ predictions (good, moderate, poor, bad), simplex reads again performed better (80.5% accuracy, κ = 0.67) than duplex reads (73.2%, κ = 0.53). Taxonomic profiles of high‐impact samples were highly concordant across read types, sharing indicator taxa such as Sulfurovum , Lutimonas and Psychrobacter . In contrast, low‐impact samples showed reduced concordance due to higher diversity and insufficient duplex coverage. Nanopore sequencing also detected strong bacterial bioindicators absent from Illumina‐based studies, highlighting its potential for uncovering previously undetected diversity. Overall, duplex sequencing provided no advantage for EQ assessment. Nanopore simplex sequencing is therefore a viable option for eDNA‐based benthic monitoring, though further optimization is needed to improve throughput and scalability.

Water Quality Parameters, Freshwater Fish Assemblages and Anthropogenic Disturbances in Looc Lake, Castillejos, Zambales, Philippines
👤 Ventura, Mikey B.; Corpuz, Mark Nell C. 📅 2026-11-16
Background: Looc Lake in Castillejos, Zambales, Philippines, supports local fisheries, but baseline information on its fish assemblages, water quality and anthropogenic pressures remains limited. Aims: The study provided a preliminary assessment of freshwater fish assemblages, evaluated water quality parameters, and identified potential anthropogenic threats in Looc Lake, Castillejos, Zambales, Philippines. Study Design: Spatial rapid bioassessment and environmental monitoring across five designated sampling stations covering the eastern, western, northern, southern and central regions of a shallow tropical floodplain lake. Place and Duration of Study: Looc Lake, Castillejos, Zambales, Luzon Island, Philippines. Sampling was conducted during daytime hours (06:00–10:00 h). Methodology: Fish specimens were collected using gill nets (mesh size 7–10 cm), cast nets, and fish traps deployed for four hours per station. Species were identified, counted, and analysed for relative abundance, diversity, dominance, evenness, catch per unit effort, and Bray-Curtis similarity. In-situ water quality parameters (pH, dissolved oxygen, water temperature, and Secchi disk visibility depth) were recorded alongside colourimetric tests for ammonia and nitrates. Anthropogenic activities were documented through observational assessments. Results: Water quality parameters indicated slightly alkaline, well-oxygenated, and temperatures within the reported range. A total of 1,611 individuals belonging to six families and eight non-native fish species were recorded, of which the fish assemblages were heavily dominated by the invasive black-chin tilapia (Sarotherodon melanotheron). All fish species were classified as non-native. Diversity indices revealed low species richness and high dominance. Based on the Bray-Curtis similarity analysis, fish assemblages exhibited high spatial homogeneity and minimal turnover across the lake stations, with similarity indices ranging from 0.60 to 0.89. However, the studied floodplain lake faces ecological pressures from solid waste dumping, macrophyte overgrowth, quarrying-induced siltation, and seasonal desiccation. Conclusion: Looc Lake exhibits low fish biodiversity dominated by invasive exotic cichlids, alongside environmental pressures from solid waste and nearby quarrying. Sustainable management policies and continuous monitoring are essential to conserve the lake's ecological integrity and local fishing livelihoods.
