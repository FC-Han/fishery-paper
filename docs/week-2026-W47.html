水产养殖智能监测与生态风险防控

本周研究概览

本周共收录6篇渔业相关论文，研究覆盖鱼类多样性保护、渔业资源评估与管理、鱼类分类学、渔业经济政策以及水产品加工与食品安全等多个方向。从研究区域看，非洲内陆水域（埃塞俄比亚Lake Tana、坦桑尼亚盐碱裂谷湖、尼日利亚渔业贸易）和南美洲（阿根廷Bermejo流域）成为本周的关注热点，反映出全球渔业研究对发展中国家内陆渔业可持续性的持续关切。方法学上，本周论文涵盖了传统形态分类与分子遗传学结合、时间序列计量经济分析、以及生物化学层面的污染物-蛋白互作机制等多种手段，整体呈现出渔业科学跨学科交叉的研究态势。

创新性研究

  1. Interaction Mechanism Between Grass Carp Fish Maw Collagen and Heavy-Metal Ions（*International Journal of Molecular Sciences*）

该研究首次系统表征了草鱼鱼鳔胶原蛋白（热溶性胶原HSC及另一组分）与常见重金属离子的结合行为与分子机制，填补了鱼鳔胶原-金属离子互作分子层面认识的空白。其创新点在于将水产品加工副产物的安全性问题与蛋白质-金属配位化学相结合，为鱼鳔类产品的重金属风险评估和脱除工艺开发提供了分子基础。

  1. Ageing imprecision compounds the effects of fishing selectivity, increasing bias in estimates of growth parameters（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新之处在于首次将 ageing error（年龄鉴定误差）与 fishing selectivity（捕捞选择性）的联合效应纳入生长参数估计偏差的量化框架，而非此前惯常的独立分析。这一发现对渔业资源评估中生长参数的可靠性具有直接影响，提示管理者在数据质量欠佳的情境下需重新审视基于长度频率的评估结论。

  1. A new species of *Pimelodella* from the upper Bermejo basin（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该文以形态学与遗传数据相结合的方式描述并命名了Bermejo流域上游的*Pimelodella*属新种，以椎骨数目（37–39）和短脂鳍基等特征与同属多数物种相区分。其意义在于丰富了La Plata basin鱼类区系记录，并为该属的系统分类与生物地理学研究提供了新的分类学依据。

  1. Fished to the Edge: Severe Overfishing Threatens Fish Stocks in Tanzania's Saline Rift Valley Lakes（*Fisheries Management and Ecology*）

该研究对坦桑尼亚Kitangiri和Singida两个盐碱裂谷湖的鱼类种群状况进行了系统评估，基于渔获量、物种组成和单位努力渔获量等指标揭示了过度捕捞的严重程度。其价值在于为数据匮乏的内陆小型渔业提供了可操作的资源状态诊断框架，对东非裂谷湖区渔业管理具有直接警示意义。

👤 Jegede, R. O.; Coker, A. A. .A.; Baba, K. M.; Ojo, A. O. 📅 2026-11-16
Purpose: This study therefore examines the impact of selected MP instruments on FISTR flow in Nigeria over the period 1970–2024. Design/Methodology/Approach: The study adopts an ex-post facto research design and relies on annual time-series secondary data covering the period 1970–2024 in Nigeria. The dataset comprises 55 annual observations for the study variables, sourced from official publications of the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Statistical Bulletin and World Development Indicators (WDI) of the World Bank. The Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model is employed to estimate the short-run and long-run relationships among the variables. To assess the robustness of the ARDL estimates, Fully Modified Ordinary Least Squares (FMOLS), Dynamic Ordinary Least Squares (DOLS), and Canonical Cointegrating Regression (CCR) estimators are also employed. Research Limitation: The study focuses exclusively on five monetary policy variables, excluding other potentially relevant factors such as trade openness, fuel prices, fisheries infrastructure, government expenditure, climate variability and institutional quality. Findings: The ARDL bounds test confirms the existence of a long-run equilibrium relationship among the variables. The long-run estimates reveal that EXR and interest rate (INTR) significantly promote FISTR, whereas inflation (INF) and foreign reserves (LFREV) exert significant adverse effects, while money supply (MS) has a positive but insignificant influence. In the short run, EXR and INF initially depress FISTR, whereas INTR and MS stimulate trade after short lags. The error correction term indicates that approximately 74.2% of short-run disequilibrium is corrected annually. The FMOLS, DOLS and CCR estimators largely validate the ARDL results, confirming the robustness of the findings. Practical Implication: Directing monetary expansion towards productive fisheries financing and strategically utilising foreign reserves for infrastructure development can strengthen cold-chain facilities, storage, processing capacity, transportation, and export infrastructure. Social Implication: It will improve the well-being of fishing and processing communities and the affordability of fish as a source of animal protein. Originality/Value: Beyond methodological novelty, it demonstrates that fisheries trade performance is shaped not only by sector-specific factors but also by broader macroeconomic conditions. These findings provide new insights for designing monetary policies that support fisheries financing, trade competitiveness, and sustainable industry development.

Elasmobranchs are ecologically and commercially important, but biological information remains limited for many species. This study investigated length–weight relationships (LWRs) of elasmobranchs caught as bycatch in a set-net fishery at Nishiki, Mie Prefecture, Japan, during 2018–2025. A total of 739 individuals representing 21 species were collected during 284 fishing operations, and 15 species with at least five individuals remaining after exclusion of damaged specimens and Cook’s distance outliers were analyzed. Assessment of Biomass, Maximum Sustainable Yield, Size Distribution and Biodiversity of Demersal Large-scale Fisheries in Lake Malawi
👤 Mulumpwa, M; Kaunda, E; Hara, M; Jere, W 📅 2026-11-18
This study was conducted to assess the fish biomass, biodiversity patterns and sustainable harvesting potential of demersal large-scale fisheries in Lake Malawi. This study aimed to determine appropriate fisheries output management target points (total allowable catch (TAC) and maximum sustainable yield (MSY)). The assessment was conducted in 2024 and was based on fish biomass survey data from 2020, which was conducted on Lake Malawi by the Department of Fisheries. A stern trawler (RV Ndunduma) was fitted with a bottom trawl net with a code-end mesh size of 38 mm. Biomass was estimated using the swept-area method and MSY was estimated using the Beddington and Cooke model. Shannon, Simpson and Pielou’s evenness indices were employed to assess biodiversity, while non-parametric statistical tests such as PERMANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis were used to determine differences in fish community composition and fish size distributions across the regions of the lake. The study estimated the total fish biomass and MSY of demersal fisheries in Lake Malawi at 261,297.8 tons and 130,648.9 tons per year, respectively. The southern region of the lake contributed the highest proportion of biomass and MSY, accounting for 55.6% of the total biomass. The study recorded high fish species richness (152), predominantly demersal fish species and very low densities of midwater fish species. The southern part of the lake recorded the highest fish species richness and abundance; however, it also showed a significant regional difference in community composition and fish size distribution. The findings further showed that the following commercially important fish species, Diplotaxodon, Lethrinops, and Rhamphochromis, in the southern part of the lake had smaller mean body sizes than in other regions, suggesting possible excessive fishing effort and growth overexploitation. The study also revealed limitations in the current fish biomass assessment approach, which is biased toward demersal species and may underestimate midwater fish stocks targeted by large-scale fishers. This study demonstrates that sustainable fishery management in Lake Malawi requires region-specific allocation of fishers, improved fish biomass assessment, stricter enforcement of fishery regulations and enhanced conservation measures. This will ensure that fish biodiversity is protected and that there is long-term fishery productivity. The findings provide scientific information for guiding fisheries policy, TAC allocation and ecosystem-based fisheries management in Malawi. Key words: Trawl fishery, fish biomass assessment, demersal fisheries and fisheries management

👤 Bhat, Showkat Ahmad; Sofi, Ishfaq Bashir; Huang, Nen-Fu 📅 2026-11-19
Reliable fish feeding assessment is essential for intelligent aquaculture, yet classification accuracy alone does not establish operational reliability. This structured critical review examines multimodal deep learning approaches, including selected unimodal deployment comparators, through five appraisal dimensions: label validity, data independence and adaptation, robustness to degraded or unavailable sensing, predictive reliability and calibration, and deployment and feeding-management evidence. A key concern is feature–label coupling, where motion-derived labels and model inputs share optical-flow representations, potentially limiting the biological interpretation of classification performance. 👤 Gangal, Mayuresh; Arthur, Rohan; Suri, Venkatachalam; Pandit... 📅 2026-11-20
Abstract Coastal fisheries are typically managed within bounds set by political jurisdictions of nations and their provinces. This approach often results in multiple states/provinces managing a single genetically distinct fish population autonomously and typically without coordination. In multispecies, decentralized, and data‐poor fisheries, this can lead to inadequate assessments and fisheries overharvests. We developed an integrative risk indicator for interjurisdictional stocks (IRIIS) to assess the overexploitation risk to interjurisdictional populations. IRIIS incorporates species‐specific vulnerability, distribution of genetically distinct populations with respect to jurisdictional boundaries, and differences in management practices across jurisdictions. As a case study, we used genetic tools and policy analysis to evaluate nine common target species spanning multiple jurisdictions along India's western coast. We showed that distinct populations of a species differed greatly in how they are managed by respective fisheries, and management disparities across jurisdictions can strongly influence the overexploitation risk for a population. IRIIS clearly identified these differences and helped determine specific fisheries policies that need reconciling across jurisdictions to reduce the risk of overexploitation. As a comprehensive management instrument, IRIIS is dynamic and sensitive to periodic revisions in fishery policy and could guide managers across jurisdictions in lowering the overexploitation risks to multispecies coastal fisheries.

When stock‐level catch advice is generated at a broader spatial scale than management implementation, empirical catch allocation strategies provide a way to distribute harvest among regions and fleets.
