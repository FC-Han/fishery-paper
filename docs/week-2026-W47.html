渔业资源保护与利用研究热点

本周研究概览

本周共收录6篇渔业相关论文，研究覆盖鱼类多样性保护、渔业资源评估与管理、鱼类分类学、渔业经济政策以及水产品加工与食品安全等多个方向。从研究区域看，非洲内陆水域（埃塞俄比亚Lake Tana、坦桑尼亚盐碱裂谷湖、尼日利亚渔业贸易）和南美洲（阿根廷Bermejo流域）成为本周的关注热点，反映出全球渔业研究对发展中国家内陆渔业可持续性的持续关切。方法学上，本周论文涵盖了传统形态分类与分子遗传学结合、时间序列计量经济分析、以及生物化学层面的污染物-蛋白互作机制等多种手段，整体呈现出渔业科学跨学科交叉的研究态势。

创新性研究

  1. Interaction Mechanism Between Grass Carp Fish Maw Collagen and Heavy-Metal Ions（*International Journal of Molecular Sciences*）

该研究首次系统表征了草鱼鱼鳔胶原蛋白（热溶性胶原HSC及另一组分）与常见重金属离子的结合行为与分子机制，填补了鱼鳔胶原-金属离子互作分子层面认识的空白。其创新点在于将水产品加工副产物的安全性问题与蛋白质-金属配位化学相结合，为鱼鳔类产品的重金属风险评估和脱除工艺开发提供了分子基础。

  1. Ageing imprecision compounds the effects of fishing selectivity, increasing bias in estimates of growth parameters（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该研究的创新之处在于首次将 ageing error（年龄鉴定误差）与 fishing selectivity（捕捞选择性）的联合效应纳入生长参数估计偏差的量化框架，而非此前惯常的独立分析。这一发现对渔业资源评估中生长参数的可靠性具有直接影响，提示管理者在数据质量欠佳的情境下需重新审视基于长度频率的评估结论。

  1. A new species of *Pimelodella* from the upper Bermejo basin（*Journal of Fish Biology*）

该文以形态学与遗传数据相结合的方式描述并命名了Bermejo流域上游的*Pimelodella*属新种，以椎骨数目（37–39）和短脂鳍基等特征与同属多数物种相区分。其意义在于丰富了La Plata basin鱼类区系记录，并为该属的系统分类与生物地理学研究提供了新的分类学依据。

  1. Fished to the Edge: Severe Overfishing Threatens Fish Stocks in Tanzania's Saline Rift Valley Lakes（*Fisheries Management and Ecology*）

该研究对坦桑尼亚Kitangiri和Singida两个盐碱裂谷湖的鱼类种群状况进行了系统评估，基于渔获量、物种组成和单位努力渔获量等指标揭示了过度捕捞的严重程度。其价值在于为数据匮乏的内陆小型渔业提供了可操作的资源状态诊断框架，对东非裂谷湖区渔业管理具有直接警示意义。

💡 本研究基于1970–2024年尼日利亚年度时间序列数据，采用ARDL模型及FMOLS、DOLS、CCR等估计方法，考察货币政策工具对鱼类贸易流（fish trade flow）的短期与长期影响。ARDL边界检验确认变量间存在长期均衡关系；长期估计显示汇率（EXR）和利率（INTR）显著促进鱼类贸易，通胀（INF）和外汇储备（LFREV）则具显著抑制作用，货币供应（MS）影响为正但不显著；误差修正项表明每年约74.2%的短期失衡得到修正。研究建议将货币扩张导向渔业生产性融资，并战略性地运用外汇储备支持冷链、加工与出口基础设施建设。
Impact of Selected Monetary Policy Instruments on Fish Trade Flow in Nigeria For The Period 1970-2024
👤 Jegede, R. O.; Coker, A. A. .A.; Baba, K. M.; Ojo, A. O. 📅 2026-11-16
Purpose: This study therefore examines the impact of selected MP instruments on FISTR flow in Nigeria over the period 1970–2024. Design/Methodology/Approach: The study adopts an ex-post facto research design and relies on annual time-series secondary data covering the period 1970–2024 in Nigeria. The dataset comprises 55 annual observations for the study variables, sourced from official publications of the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Statistical Bulletin and World Development Indicators (WDI) of the World Bank. The Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model is employed to estimate the short-run and long-run relationships among the variables. To assess the robustness of the ARDL estimates, Fully Modified Ordinary Least Squares (FMOLS), Dynamic Ordinary Least Squares (DOLS), and Canonical Cointegrating Regression (CCR) estimators are also employed. Research Limitation: The study focuses exclusively on five monetary policy variables, excluding other potentially relevant factors such as trade openness, fuel prices, fisheries infrastructure, government expenditure, climate variability and institutional quality. Findings: The ARDL bounds test confirms the existence of a long-run equilibrium relationship among the variables. The long-run estimates reveal that EXR and interest rate (INTR) significantly promote FISTR, whereas inflation (INF) and foreign reserves (LFREV) exert significant adverse effects, while money supply (MS) has a positive but insignificant influence. In the short run, EXR and INF initially depress FISTR, whereas INTR and MS stimulate trade after short lags. The error correction term indicates that approximately 74.2% of short-run disequilibrium is corrected annually. The FMOLS, DOLS and CCR estimators largely validate the ARDL results, confirming the robustness of the findings. Practical Implication: Directing monetary expansion towards productive fisheries financing and strategically utilising foreign reserves for infrastructure development can strengthen cold-chain facilities, storage, processing capacity, transportation, and export infrastructure. Social Implication: It will improve the well-being of fishing and processing communities and the affordability of fish as a source of animal protein. Originality/Value: Beyond methodological novelty, it demonstrates that fisheries trade performance is shaped not only by sector-specific factors but also by broader macroeconomic conditions. These findings provide new insights for designing monetary policies that support fisheries financing, trade competitiveness, and sustainable industry development.

Fish Species Diversity, Seasonal Dynamics and Land Use Land Cover Associations With Fish Assemblages in the Gulf of Lake Tana, Ethiopia
👤 Tsegaye, Birtukan; Mingist, Minwyelet; Getahun, Abebe; Mengi... 📅 2026-11-16
ABSTRACT Lake Tana, Ethiopia, harbours the world's only remaining cyprinid species flock, making it globally significant for conservation. Yet, these resources face mounting anthropogenic pressures from overfishing and land use changes. We assessed fish assemblage structure and seasonal dynamics in the Gulf of Lake Tana from September 2024 to August 2025 and land use land cover (LULC) changes from 2000 to 2025 using Landsat and Sentinel imagery. Fish specimens were sampled monthly from fishers at landing sites employing gillnets of varying mesh sizes. The specimens were identified to species level and analysed using Shannon diversity ( H ′), evenness ( J ), dominance ( C ) and Index of Relative Importance (%IRI). Exploratory multivariate analyses (non‐metric multidimensional scaling [NMDS] and principal component analysis [PCA] of four seasonal aggregates) were used to describe seasonal assemblage patterns. A total of 2764 specimens representing 18 species were recorded, dominated by endemic Labeobarbus spp. (81.8%), followed by Oreochromis niloticus (11.9%) and Clarias gariepinus (6.3%). Diversity was moderate ( H ′ = 2.60) and differed significantly among four hydrological seasons (individual‐based permutation test, 9999 permutations: p = 0.0024), being highest in the dry season ( H ′ = 2.596). After Bonferroni correction, dry‐season H ′ differed significantly from pre‐rainy (Hutcheson t ‐test, t = 3.84, adjusted p = 0.0006) and post‐rainy H ′ ( p = 0.036), but not from rainy‐season H ′ (adjusted p = 0.066). Evenness was high in all seasons ( J = 0.87–0.95). Sex ratios were female‐biased in the catches of 12 of 18 species ( χ 2 , p < 0.001). Between 2000 and 2025, cropland and settlements expanded to nearly 60% of the mapped area, whereas wetlands declined by 92.7%. Because, fish data cover a single year and no environmental covariates were measured, these LULC trends are presented only as landscape context that is associated with the current assemblage composition. The findings provide baseline data for further studies, testing these associations directly, and support the need for watershed rehabilitation, buffer zone protection and sustained fisheries monitoring to protect the lake's globally unique cyprinid flock.

Fished to the Edge: Severe Overfishing Threatens Fish Stocks in Tanzania's Saline Rift Valley Lakes
👤 Said, Hadija Hussein; Mwandya, Augustine W.; Mwakosya, Cathe... 📅 2026-11-16
ABSTRACT The Saline Rift Valley Lakes (SRVLs) of Tanzania support important inland fisheries, but increasing fishing pressure threatens their sustainability. This study assessed the status of fish stocks in two Tanzanian SRVLs: Lakes Kitangiri and Singida. Data on catches, species composition, catch per unit effort, maturity stages, and length‐based indicators were collected from May to September 2023. Results indicate strong evidence of overfishing, reflected in declining catches and high exploitation rates. The endemic Oreochromis amphimelas (Manyara tilapia) was the most affected species, with exploitation rates (> 0.9) and fishing mortality 13‐18 times above sustainable reference points. Length‐frequency data showed that most individuals were captured before reaching optimal size. All sampled O. amphimelas exhibited small body depths relative to the legal 7.62 cm gill‐net mesh size, and a high proportion of individuals were immature or early maturing. Interviews revealed widespread use of small‐mesh nets, contributing to the capture of undersized fish. However, some individuals matured at relatively small sizes, suggesting possible shifts in population structure linked to environmental stressors. These findings reveal a mismatch between current mesh size regulations and the biological characteristics of target species. Revising gear regulations and adopting species‐specific management measures are recommended to improve fisheries sustainability in SRVL.

Interaction Mechanism Between Grass Carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) Fish Maw Collagen and Heavy-Metal Ions
👤 Feng, Qianqian; Xu, Wei; Yan, Xu; Ramaswamy, Hosahalli S.; A... 📅 2026-11-16
Heavy-metal residues in fish maw are closely associated with its collagen fraction, yet the binding behavior and molecular mechanism of grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) fish maw collagen toward common metal ions remain poorly characterized. To address this, hot-water-soluble collagen (HSC) and acetic acid soluble collagen (ASC) were extracted and characterized via SDS-PAGE, UV spectroscopy, zeta-potential measurement and amino acid analysis. Adsorption isotherms, fluorescence, CD and SEM-EDX were used to probe binding with Cu(II), Hg(II), Cd(II), and Cr(III). Dried fish maw contained 56.907 ± 0.319 g/100 g of protein and 25.01% collagen. In the four-ion mixed competitive system containing Cu(II), Hg(II), Cd(II) and Cr(III), the Langmuir and Freundlich models were applied only as empirical fitting tools, and the fitted parameters are apparent values under competition. Cd(II) showed the highest apparent adsorption capacity, while Cu(II), Cr(III) and Hg(II) exhibited stronger apparent affinity; Cu(II), Hg(II) and Cd(II) were better described by the Langmuir model, whereas Cr(III) was better described by the Freundlich model. Cu(II), Cr(III), and Hg(II) triggered static fluorescence quenching. The binding of Cr(III) and Hg(II) may involve hydrophobic interactions, while the binding of Cu(II) may involve hydrogen bonds and van der Waals forces, and it may also result from the non-specific intermolecular aggregation of collagen induced by Cu(II). Low-concentration Cr(III) disrupted collagen ellipticity bands and induced disordered conformations. This work deepens the mechanistic understanding of heavy-metal binding to fish maw collagen and offers fundamental insights for future relevant research.

A new species of P imelodella ( H eptapteridae: R hamdiinae) from the upper B ermejo basin, northwestern L a P lata basin
👤 Cortés‐Hernández, Miguel Ángel; Alonso, Felipe; Slobodian, V... 📅 2026-11-16
Abstract A new species of Pimelodella occurring in the upper Bermejo basin is described using morphological and genetic data. The new species is distinguished from most congeners by having fewer total vertebrae (37–39), short adipose‐fin base (20.3%–25.2% of L S ), maxillary barbel almost reaching or reaching the origin of the adipose fin, subequal lobes of caudal fin or upper lobe slightly longer, supraoccipital process articulating with the anterior nuchal plate and epiphyseal branch of supraorbital canal on the head ending in a single sensory pore (s6 + s6). The type locality of the new species is situated in the transition zone between the Yunga and Dry Chaco terrestrial ecoregions near the area of influence of Parque Nacional El Rey. Considering the high anthropogenic impact resulting from agricultural activities in the region and the high level of endemism present in the upper Bermejo basin, the new species could be categorized as Endangered (EN) in a future formal assessment, following the IUCN criteria.

Ageing imprecision compounds the effects of fishing selectivity, increasing bias in estimates of growth parameters
👤 Chamberlin, Derek W. 📅 2026-11-16
Abstract Accurately estimating fish growth is critical for fisheries management, but growth parameter estimates are often affected by multiple sources of error. While the independent effects of ageing error and fishing selectivity are well documented, their combined effects remain unknown. This study used a simulation approach to determine how ageing imprecision and selectivity (logistic and dome‐shaped) compound when estimating growth parameters across four rockfish ( Sebastes spp.) life histories. Results demonstrated that ageing imprecision and selectivity exacerbate biases in growth parameter estimates that exceed the error caused by selectivity alone, while selectivity was the primary driver in estimating size‐at‐age. The magnitude of this compounded bias was heavily dependent on the species' life history and the shape of the selectivity curve. Because growth parameters are frequently used as proxies for critical stock benchmarks like natural mortality and stock depletion, these compounded errors could distort population models. Current growth models fail to account for both sources of error simultaneously, highlighting the critical need to develop integrated frameworks that account for both ageing imprecision and selectivity.
