海洋热浪与微塑料污染成渔业研究热点

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文17篇，研究主题涵盖气候变化对渔业资源的影响、渔业数据整合与种群评估、水产养殖环境效应与病害监测、微塑料污染、鱼类检测技术及渔业社会经济等多个方向。整体来看，本周研究呈现出两个显著趋势：一是气候变化与人类活动对渔业生态系统的复合影响受到持续关注，尤其是海洋热浪引发的生态连锁反应；二是方法学创新突出，包括将渔民知识纳入种群评估、物理信息神经网络用于海洋浮标轨迹预测、以及无酸微波消解等绿色分析化学方法。此外，微塑料污染在水产养殖和海产品中的分布与风险仍是热点议题。

高影响力期刊亮点

PNAS 发表了两篇值得关注的论文。其一，*A Bering Sea marine heatwave triggers unprecedented crab hybridization, challenging fisheries management*，记录了白令海海洋热浪发生7年后出现的意外生态响应——先前未见的蟹类杂交现象，揭示了海洋热浪通过延迟性生态途径产生管理挑战的机制，对渔业资源管理具有重要警示意义。其二，*Biodiversity supports coastal nutrient cycling with a stable source of fish excretion*，探讨了生物多样性对鱼类排泄物养分供给稳定性的支撑作用，为理解生物多样性与生态系统功能的关系提供了新证据。

Communications Sustainability 刊发了 *Hidden population declines revealed by integrating long-term fisheries data with fishers' knowledge*，该研究将1972–2022年长期渔业统计数据与渔民知识相结合，揭示了"技术蠕变"（technological creep）如何掩盖种群下降并偏倚渔业评估，为改进渔业资源评估方法提供了重要思路。

创新性研究

  1. Hidden population declines revealed by integrating long-term fisheries data with fishers' knowledge（Communications Sustainability）：创新性地将渔民口述知识与长期统计序列整合，弥补了技术采纳历史数据缺失的空白，为纠正技术蠕变导致的评估偏差提供了可操作框架，对数据有限渔业的管理具有广泛借鉴意义。
  1. Physics-informed LSTMNet for short-term marine buoy trajectory prediction under dynamic ocean environmental conditions（Frontiers in Marine Science）：将物理约束嵌入LSTM网络用于海洋浮标短期轨迹预测，兼顾了数据驱动模型的灵活性与物理规律的一致性，在航行安全与搜救成本降低方面具有应用潜力。
  1. A Greener Acid-free Microwave-Assisted Digestion Method for Multielement Analysis of Fish Tissues by ICP–MS（ACS Omega）：开发了仅基于H₂O₂的无酸微波消解方法用于鱼组织多元素分析，显著降低了传统酸消解的环境负担和操作风险，为渔业环境监测的绿色分析化学提供了新方案。
  1. SAG-DETR: spatio-spectral adaptive gating with prompt-aware state-space refinement for underwater fish detection（Aquaculture International）：提出融合空谱自适应门控与提示感知状态空间细化的水下鱼类检测框架，针对水下图像退化问题设计了专门的结构改进，对水产养殖智能化监测具有推进作用。
  1. Management implications of misspecification in observed recreational removals for reef fish and coastal pelagic stocks（Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences）：基于美国东海岸休闲渔业移除量可能被高估30–40%的实证发现，系统评估了观测误差对管理策略的影响，为休闲渔业统计体系改进提供了定量依据。

A Greener Acid-free Microwave-Assisted Digestion Method for Multielement Analysis of Fish Tissues by ICP–MS
👤 Pereira, Marcos V. S.; Fonseca, Elisânia K. B.; de Jesus, J... 📅 2026-10-19
Abstract Trace element monitoring in fish tissues requires reliable and sustainable sample preparation methods, particularly for complex biological matrices. In this study, an acid-free microwave-assisted digestion (AFMAD) method based exclusively on H2O2 was developed and optimized for multielement determination in fish tissues by ICP–MS. A central composite design coupled with response surface methodology was employed to evaluate the influence of H2O2 concentration, ramp time, and digestion temperature on matrix decomposition efficiency using 100 mg of dry fish tissue subsamples throughout the method development. The optimized conditions consisted of 30% (v/v)H2O2, 8 min ramp time, a 20 min hold time and 240 °C, resulting in residual carbon contents below 20%, which indicates efficient oxidation of the organic matrix using H2O2 in the absence of HNO3. The analytical performance of the proposed method was evaluated using cod muscle certified reference material (BCR-422), providing recoveries ranging from 76 to 101% for Fe, Cu, Zn, Mn, As, and Cd. Excellent linearity was obtained for all analytes (R2 &gt; 0.990) within the 0–50 μg L–1 range. Limits of detection ranged from 0.0004 to 0.04 μg g–1, demonstrating suitable sensitivity for trace elemental analysis in fish matrices. The applicability of the method was demonstrated through the analysis of five commercial fish species collected in the Mariana-MG region. Concentrations of toxic elements were found in Megaleporinus conirostris (Cd, 0.003 mg kg–1) and Cichla ocellaris (As, 0.07 mg kg–1) and remained below the maximum limits established by Brazilian legislation. The proposed AFMAD method demonstrated satisfactory analytical performance for complex fish matrices while eliminating the use of concentrated mineral acids, reducing hazardous reagent consumption, and improving the sustainability characteristics of sample preparation procedures for elemental analysis.

Effects of Fish Farming Prior to Farm Removal on Macrozoobenthic Crustacean and Molluscan Assemblages in a Major Turkish Aquaculture Gulf: Integrating Historical Data with Literature Insights
👤 Aslan, Berivan Elif; Mutlu, Erhan 📅 2026-10-19
Fish farming can significantly impact benthic ecosystems, particularly through alterations in water and sediment quality, potentially disrupting ecosystem functioning and biodiversity. This study investigates the effects of fish-farming on crustacean and molluscan assemblages in Güllük Gulf and Bozburun bay along Turkey’s Aegean coast. Macrozoobenthic samples were collected from fish farm sites and corresponding control sites, representing a baseline prior to the relocation of many farms to offshore areas in the 2010s. A total of 114 crustacean and 54 molluscan species were identified at farm sites, compared to 88 crustaceans and 52 molluscs at control sites. Crustaceans, particularly amphipods, were the most diverse group, while bivalves dominated the molluscan assemblages. Species such as Moerella donacina and Varicorbula gibba (Bivalvia) were most abundant at farm sites, whereas Chondrochelia savignyi (Tanaidacea) and Apseudopsis latreillii (Tanaidacea) were dominant at control sites. Ecological differences between Güllük Gulf and Bozburun bay, as well as between farm and control sites, were evident, with faunal abundance at farm sites showing stronger correlations with environmental variables, including orthophosphate, silicate, and temperature. Biomass emerged as a key indicator for assessing ecological status, alongside abundance and species composition. Fish-farm capacity, as determined by the number of fish cages, played a significant role in shaping faunal patterns, with higher capacity farms exhibiting greater ecological disturbance. This study provides a crucial baseline for future research on the long-term ecological impacts of fish farming, including ecosystem recovery and the role of environmental variables. The findings suggest the importance of incorporating biomass-based indicators and considering farm capacity and site-specific conditions in fish-farming management strategies to mitigate environmental degradation.

Management implications of misspecification in observed recreational removals for reef fish and coastal pelagic stocks
👤 Peterson, Cassidy D; Sagarese, Skyler R; Vaughan, Nathan R.;... 📅 2026-10-19
Recreational removals of fishes are particularly challenging to estimate accurately. A recent pilot study indicated that U.S. East Coast recreational removals may be overestimated by 30-40%, which is concerning in the Southeast, where recreational removals often exceed commercial. Management strategy evaluation was used to identify the management implications of recreational removal misspecification on nine reef and coastal pelagic fishes in the U.S. Gulf of America. The R package SSMSE was used to test performance of “Status Quo” management across seven removal misspecification scenarios. When recreational removals were misspecified, absolute stock magnitude was not estimated accurately. However, relative status reference points were generally well estimated when there was constant or randomly varying misspecification (representative relative index of removals), such that Status Quo management was still able to meet biomass, exploitation, and yield targets. The ability to identify reference points and manage the stock was adversely impacted by abrupt shifts or annually time-varying trends in misspecification (non-representative index of removals). Results emphasize that a relative index of recreational removals is generally sufficient to successfully manage a stock.

Behrangi’s The Little Black Fish: A Tale of Endless Resistance and Resilience
👤 Şencan, Selin 📅 2026-10-19
Reading Samad Behrangi’s The Little Black Fish (1968) as a children’s story of political and philosophical significance, this article examines how the fish’s movement from the pond toward the sea reconfigures relations among space, authority, and agency. Drawing on Beauvais’s distinction between adult authority and child might, the article argues that resistance develops as the younger subject tests inherited knowledge across changing spatial and social conditions. The pond establishes an enclosed order sustained by inherited knowledge and collective conformity, whereas the fish’s departure brings it into encounters that alter its understanding and capacity to act. The sea intensifies this tension by combining expanded mobility with continuing exposure to capture and violence. The article also considers the story’s framing structure, in which the grandmother fish recounts the journey to a younger generation and one red fish remains awake thinking about the sea. This final act of transmission gives the narrative a pedagogical dimension. The article argues that The Little Black Fish presents resistance as a relational process produced through questioning, encounter, and collective action, while resilience emerges through the capacity to retain, adapt, and reactivate knowledge across changing spatial conditions. Beauvais’s account of authority and child might consequently intersects with the tale’s changing geographies where agency develops through what the younger subject learns, carries forward, and makes usable under circumstances that could not have been anticipated in advance.

Hidden population declines revealed by integrating long-term fisheries data with fishers’ knowledge
👤 Nishimoto, Makoto; Aoki, Yoshinori; Matsubara, Naoto; Hamer,... 📅 2026-10-19
Abstract Technological creep, the gradual increase in fishing power due to technological innovation, can mask population declines and bias fisheries assessments, yet information on historical technology adoption is rarely available. Here, we integrate long-term fisheries statistics (1972–2022) with structured interviews with experienced fishers to reconstruct technological change in the Japanese pole-and-line skipjack tuna ( Katsuwonus pelamis ) fishery. We estimate population trajectories over five decades using a Bayesian state-space model with time-varying catchability informed by questionnaire data. Biomass estimates decline substantially when catchability increases over time, by approximately 3.5-fold over the study period, equivalent to about 2.5% annually, relative to models assuming constant catchability. The resulting biomass trajectory indicates that the stock off Japan is below levels compatible with maximum sustainable yield (MSY). Fishers’ perceptions corroborate the inferred decline in skipjack tuna abundance despite apparently stable catch rates. Our findings highlight the importance of incorporating dynamic catchability into population models and provide a scalable framework for correcting for technological creep using fishers’ knowledge.

Physics-informed LSTMNet for short-term marine buoy trajectory prediction under dynamic ocean environmental conditions
👤 Zhou, Tianrui; Wang, Jinggai; Li, Hao 📅 2026-10-19
Introduction Marine buoys are essential aids to navigation, yet their positions can vary continuously under dynamic environmental forcing. It is therefore important for navigational safety, maintenance planning, and reducing search-and-recovery costs to achieve accurate short-term prediction of buoy displacement. Physics-based models often depend on accurately specified hydrodynamic and meteorological parameters, whereas many existing data-driven approaches employ shallow, unidirectional recurrent architectures that may not fully capture the temporal context embedded in historical observations. Methods To address these limitations, this study proposes LSTMNet, a two-layer bidirectional long short-term memory network for next-step buoy trajectory prediction. The model learns temporal dependencies from multivariate buoy observations and predicts future buoy positions under dynamic marine conditions. Results The model was evaluated using hourly observations collected from a marine buoy during May 2026. On the held-out test set, LSTMNet achieved a mean geodesic distance error of 18.297 m, representing a 9.747% reduction relative to a single-layer unidirectional LSTM baseline. These results indicate that combining stacked recurrent learning with bidirectional temporal encoding improves the extraction of contextual dependencies from recent observations and enhances short-term buoy trajectory prediction. Discussion The proposed framework provides a practical data-driven approach for forecasting buoy displacement and may support safer and more efficient management of marine navigation infrastructure.

Epidemiological surveillance reveals major pathogen dynamics in Brazilian Nile tilapia aquaculture from 2023 to 2025
👤 Cardoso-Guimarães, Mateus; Fernandez-Alarcon, Miguel Frederi... 📅 2026-10-19
Introduction Brazil is among the world’s largest producers of Nile tilapia ( Oreochromis niloticus ), a species increasingly challenged by a complex of cocirculating pathogens that threatens production sustainability. Methods We conducted a structured epidemiological surveillance program across the three main tilapia-producing macro-regions of Brazil (Southeast, Central-West, and Northeast) between 2023 and 2025. A total of 26,057 diagnostic samples, generating 68,514 pathogen-specific tests, were analyzed for Aeromonas spp., Edwardsiella spp., Flavobacterium spp., Francisella orientalis , Infectious Spleen and Kidney Necrosis Virus (ISKNV), Lactococcus spp., and Streptococcus spp. using standardized bacteriological, real-time PCR, and MALDI-TOF MS protocols, and antimicrobial susceptibility (minimum inhibitory concentration, MIC) was determined for four antimicrobials. Generalized linear models (with a farm-clustered mixed-model sensitivity analysis), time-series analysis (ADF stationarity, ARIMA), co-detection network analysis, and Gamma-log MIC modelling were applied. Results The cumulative overall detection positivity rate (DPR) was 18.1%, with Flavobacterium spp. showing the highest cumulative positivity (21.6%) on the smallest testing base, and Lactococcus spp. (18.4%), ISKNV (23.3%), and Streptococcus spp. (15.2%) representing the dominant, most widely sampled agents; S. agalactiae serotype Ia was not detected by serotype-specific qPCR. Detection was structured principally by macro-region and pathogen-specific temporal dynamics, whereas host developmental stage exerted a heterogeneous, generally weak influence. Five of the seven pathogens showed stationary, endemically compatible dynamics; Streptococcus spp. was non-stationary, consistent with active geographic expansion into the Northeast, whereas non-stationarity in Flavobacterium spp. reflected sampling instability rather than directional change. Quarterly aggregation minimized forecast error among the frequencies evaluated. Co-detection was predominantly independent, with only a weak module linking the three dominant agents. Antimicrobial resistance was structured by pathogen and antibiotic, with significant pathogen-specific interactions, and the fry stage showed higher MICs. Discussion These findings reframe the historically Streptococcus -centered view of Brazilian tilapia health and, within the constraints of a structured but non-probabilistic design, provide a descriptive, data-driven baseline to inform regionalized early-warning systems and rational antimicrobial stewardship in Brazilian tilapia aquaculture.

The Perceived Impact of the Single-Use Plastics Implementation for Micro Fish Vendors in Island Garden City of Samal
👤 T. Debalos, Shania Mariz; A. Galanido, Christine; O. Loreño,... 📅 2026-10-19
The study evaluated the perceived effects of implementing single-use plastic regulations on micro fish vendors in the Island Garden City of Samal. It used a quantitative descriptive-comparative study design to examine the vendors' demographic characteristics and their perceptions of the regulation's impact on their business operations. The study also found no differences in perceived impact among vendors when divided into groups by age, gender, and educational attainment. A structured questionnaire, which was adopted, was used to collect data from the registered micro fish vendors in the three districts of the Island Garden City of Samal. The results highlighted that the vendors were mostly female, of working age, and with secondary-level education. The vendors felt that the implementation of single-use plastic had a moderate impact, mainly on packaging practices, operating costs, and customer-related aspects of the business. In addition, statistical analysis revealed no significant differences in perceived impacts when vendors were grouped by age, gender, and educational attainment, suggesting that regulatory difficulties were fairly evenly distributed across groups. The findings were analyzed using Kurt Lewin's Change Management Theory and Nudge Theory to provide insights into how vendors respond to regulatory changes and adjust their approach towards more sustainable methods.
