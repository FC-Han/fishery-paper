基于进化途径的保育水产养殖与鱼类健康多维研究趋势

本周研究概览

本周共收录渔业相关论文17篇，研究主题涵盖气候变化对渔业资源的影响、渔业数据整合与种群评估、水产养殖环境效应与病害监测、微塑料污染、鱼类检测技术及渔业社会经济等多个方向。整体来看，本周研究呈现出两个显著趋势：一是气候变化与人类活动对渔业生态系统的复合影响受到持续关注，尤其是海洋热浪引发的生态连锁反应；二是方法学创新突出，包括将渔民知识纳入种群评估、物理信息神经网络用于海洋浮标轨迹预测、以及无酸微波消解等绿色分析化学方法。此外，微塑料污染在水产养殖和海产品中的分布与风险仍是热点议题。

高影响力期刊亮点

PNAS 发表了两篇值得关注的论文。其一，*A Bering Sea marine heatwave triggers unprecedented crab hybridization, challenging fisheries management*，记录了白令海海洋热浪发生7年后出现的意外生态响应——先前未见的蟹类杂交现象，揭示了海洋热浪通过延迟性生态途径产生管理挑战的机制，对渔业资源管理具有重要警示意义。其二，*Biodiversity supports coastal nutrient cycling with a stable source of fish excretion*，探讨了生物多样性对鱼类排泄物养分供给稳定性的支撑作用，为理解生物多样性与生态系统功能的关系提供了新证据。

Communications Sustainability 刊发了 *Hidden population declines revealed by integrating long-term fisheries data with fishers' knowledge*，该研究将1972–2022年长期渔业统计数据与渔民知识相结合，揭示了"技术蠕变"（technological creep）如何掩盖种群下降并偏倚渔业评估，为改进渔业资源评估方法提供了重要思路。

创新性研究

  1. Hidden population declines revealed by integrating long-term fisheries data with fishers' knowledge（Communications Sustainability）：创新性地将渔民口述知识与长期统计序列整合，弥补了技术采纳历史数据缺失的空白，为纠正技术蠕变导致的评估偏差提供了可操作框架，对数据有限渔业的管理具有广泛借鉴意义。
  1. Physics-informed LSTMNet for short-term marine buoy trajectory prediction under dynamic ocean environmental conditions（Frontiers in Marine Science）：将物理约束嵌入LSTM网络用于海洋浮标短期轨迹预测，兼顾了数据驱动模型的灵活性与物理规律的一致性，在航行安全与搜救成本降低方面具有应用潜力。
  1. A Greener Acid-free Microwave-Assisted Digestion Method for Multielement Analysis of Fish Tissues by ICP–MS（ACS Omega）：开发了仅基于H₂O₂的无酸微波消解方法用于鱼组织多元素分析，显著降低了传统酸消解的环境负担和操作风险，为渔业环境监测的绿色分析化学提供了新方案。
  1. SAG-DETR: spatio-spectral adaptive gating with prompt-aware state-space refinement for underwater fish detection（Aquaculture International）：提出融合空谱自适应门控与提示感知状态空间细化的水下鱼类检测框架，针对水下图像退化问题设计了专门的结构改进，对水产养殖智能化监测具有推进作用。
  1. Management implications of misspecification in observed recreational removals for reef fish and coastal pelagic stocks（Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences）：基于美国东海岸休闲渔业移除量可能被高估30–40%的实证发现，系统评估了观测误差对管理策略的影响，为休闲渔业统计体系改进提供了定量依据。

fishFingers: Prioritization of High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry Features by Prediction of Bioconcentration Factors in Fish Species
👤 Szabo, Drew; MacOscar, Kian; Plaisted, Katie; McGarrigle, Ma... 📅 2026-10-24
Abstract The number of novel entities produced around the world exceeds our capacity to evaluate the risk of each chemical under traditional frameworks. Detecting, prioritizing, and identifying thousands of chemicals present in the environment is a challenge to understanding this planetary boundary. Machine learning has demonstrated quick and accurate predictions for ecotoxicological end points for chemicals with structural fingerprints derived from high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS). This study provides a new nontargeted screening strategy to prioritize HRMS features by the prediction of bioconcentration factors (BCF) without a priori structural annotation. An XGBoost model was trained on a database with structural fingerprints from 1285 unique substances and log10 BCF measurements from 111 species of fish. Validation was performed using structural fingerprints derived from high-quality tandem mass spectrometry (MS2), with RMSE = 1.02, MAE = 0.81, and ρ = 0.50, demonstrating its capability in predicting log10 BCF and to rank HRMS features by predicted bioconcentration from a wide chemical space. Variations in BCF were observed in species such as common carp (Cyprinus carpio), which exhibits lower average BCF compared with zebrafish (Danio rerio). Halogenation, aliphatic and aromatic carbon, and heteroatomaticity were among the most important structural fingerprints for BCF. The model and package are available from https://github.com/drewszabo/fishFingers.

The quality and quantity of raw materials used to make feed must be considered to enhance the development of catfish. One of the nutrients in the feed needed by catfish is protein. Catfish utilize protein for the growth process. One alternative protein source to fish meal is fish waste meal. Fish waste meal contains a protein content of 46.02% and has a lysine amino acid profile content of 3.68%. Variable dissolved organic carbon trends in the tributaries of the St. Lawrence River driven by natural geographic features and human activities
👤 Dormoy-Boulanger, Jade; Lapierre, Jean-François; Guillemette... 📅 2026-10-24
Over the past decades, dissolved organic carbon (DOC) concentrations in boreal rivers have increased broadly. At the regional scale, however, the magnitude of these trends varies, likely reflecting differences in natural and anthropogenic catchment features that remain poorly understood. Here we examined long-term DOC trends data from 89 stations located on 54 tributaries of the St. Lawrence River (Qc, Canada) over 35 years, assessing DOC changes among catchments with contrasting natural characteristics and land-use patterns. We observed a significant increase in DOC in 46 stations (0.08–1.54% per year), and a significant decrease in 19 stations (−0.25 to −1.59% per year). When considering climatic variables and land cover features, the proportion of forested area within the watershed emerged as the most significant factor; DOC tended to increase at forest cover >60% and to be stable or decrease at forest cover <60%. Watershed slope, water area, and annual mean precipitation were also significantly associated with DOC changes. This study emphasizes the importance of accounting for land-use differences when interpreting regional patterns of river DOC trends.

An integrated approach to estimating the effective sampling area of baited underwater camera traps
👤 Goldstein, Benjamin R.; Pacifici, Krishna; Buckel, Jeffrey A... 📅 2026-10-24
Baited remote underwater video (BRUV) surveys are increasingly used to monitor fish, but estimating absolute abundance remains challenging because effective sampling areas (ESA) are unknown. We present an approach to estimate ESAs using an unmarked spatial capture-recapture (USCR) framework where observed counts arise from fish movement and space use. The model integrates telemetry data on movement and counts from independent remotely operated vehicle surveys. We applied this method to red snapper (Lutjanus campechanus) at two sites in the southeastern United States. At Chicken Rock, North Carolina, estimated ESAs were 8,806-10,532 m^2, consistent with estimates from previous studies and telemetry data. At Turtle Mound, Florida, estimated ESAs were unrealistically large (230,129-444,977 m^2), with low estimated abundance (241-528). Poor performance at Turtle Mound likely resulted from wide camera spacing and limited information on the spatial distribution of individuals from telemetry data. Our results demonstrate the potential of USCR models to estimate fish density, absolute abundance, and BRUV ESAs, while emphasizing the sensitivity of model performance to camera placement and robust auxiliary data in heterogeneous systems.

Experimental evaluation of habitat preferences of invasive crayfish species in Europe in context of co-invasions
👤 Plichta, Zuzanna; Maciaszek, Rafał; Augustyniak, Mateusz; Ka... 📅 2026-10-24
Abstract Biological invasions commonly occur in series, leading to new interactions between multiple invaders in novel habitats. Their coexistence or competitive exclusion depends on the width and overlap of their habitat preferences. We evaluated the habitat preferences of North American crayfishes invasive in Europe: Faxonius limosus ( FL ), Procambarus clarkii ( PC ) and Procambarus virginalis ( PV ), to check their selectivity for substratum grain sizes, shelter sizes, and shells of various bivalve species as shelters. The high overlap of habitat preferences and low probability of habitat partitioning suggests that the coexistence of these crayfishes will be temporary, and FL is likely to get displaced by new Procambarus invaders, P. clarkii being more explorative and aggressive, P. virginalis being less selective.

👤 Helmud, Ellya; Kiswanto 📅 2026-10-24
This study proposes an asymmetric hybrid feature-fusion network that combines EfficientNet-B0 and MobileNetV3Large in parallel for multi-class classification of eight fish disease categories. The approach is designed to exploit the complementary feature-learning capabilities of both lightweight convolutional neural network architectures while maintaining computational efficiency for potential diagnostic applications. The model was evaluated on a public dataset of 2,400 digital images, divided into training, validation, and test sets using an 80:10:10 ratio. The held-out test set contained 240 unseen images, with 30 images representing each class. As all data partitions originated from the same dataset, the evaluation reflects performance on the same source distribution rather than external generalization. The proposed architecture fuses spatial feature representations through parallel Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Global Max Pooling (GMP) layers. Further evaluation using external farm datasets and low-power edge devices is required before real-world deployment.

Partitioning patterns of fish and sea urchin herbivory from tropical to temperate reefs
👤 Rose, Matthew; Ruiz-Ruiz, Paula A.; Tebbett, Sterling B.; Jo... 📅 2026-10-25
Abstract Herbivory can structure vegetation and affect the states of sunlit ecosystems. On shallow tropical and temperate reefs, herbivory can inhibit macroalgal dominance and promote turf-dominated benthic states. However, standardised comparisons of tropical and temperate herbivory are primarily focussed on fish, leaving the relative contributions of sea urchins poorly understood. Here, we examined thermal realm (tropical vs temperate) and depth (2, 5, 8 and 12-m) effects on relative herbivory patterns and herbivore distributions, using standardised macroalgal assays (N = 1,340) and underwater visual surveys (N = 67 surveys) at 49 sites across six locations spanning approximately 29° of latitude in eastern Australia. Assays were deployed to partition the relative intensity of herbivory using two partitioning treatments: 1) assays were suspended above the benthos allowing only fish consumption (water-column herbivory), and 2) assays were laid flat on the benthos enabling fish and urchin consumption (benthic herbivory). Assay results showed higher fish herbivory on tropical reefs than on temperate reefs, whereas relative benthic herbivory did not differ between thermal realms. Variation in assay consumption across depth was limited. Results from assays and surveys aligned, with higher herbivorous fish abundance on tropical reefs and higher urchin abundance on temperate reefs. Relative patterns in fish and urchin herbivory between thermal realms underscores differences in how herbivory occurs and where in the water-column it predominates, highlighting a key axis of variation for tropicalizing reefs.
